PG&E's true-up is the once-a-year bill that settles your solar account. Each month you pay a small fixed charge to stay connected to the grid, while your electricity charges and solar credits build up as a running balance. In the twelfth month PG&E adds it all up: if you used more than you earned in credits, you pay the balance; if you sent more to the grid than you used, you may get Net Surplus Compensation. The surprise comes when people do not watch that running balance.

## Key numbers

- **12 months:** the true-up period. You get 11 monthly NEM statements and the True-Up statement in the twelfth month (PG&E bill explainer).
- **About $24 a month:** the Base Services Charge most PG&E customers pay monthly from March 2026, about $6 for CARE and $12 for FERA (PG&E).
- **4 p.m. to 9 p.m.:** typical PG&E peak hours, when grid power costs more (PG&E bill explainer; check your own rate).
- **20 years:** how long a NEM 2 system keeps NEM 2 billing from its original permission-to-operate date (PG&E NEM2 tariff).

## How does the PG&E true-up work?

| | Paid monthly | Settled at true-up |
|---|---|---|
| Base Services Charge (grid connection) | Yes | Shown and reconciled |
| Gas charges, if any | Yes | No |
| Electricity used from the grid (energy charges) | No | Yes |
| Credits for solar sent to the grid | No | Yes |
| Net Surplus Compensation (if you exported more than you used) | No | Yes, shown as a credit |

PG&E's explainer describes two statements. The monthly one shows the grid connection charge you pay now, any gas charges, and "your year-to-date NEM charges which will be paid at your annual True-up." The annual True-Up statement shows the total NEM energy charges minus credits for the previous twelve months, plus taxes.

## What changed in 2026?

PG&E's sample True-Up statement for March 2026 to March 2027 says: "Starting in March 2026, this charge will be replaced with Base Services Charge." It refers to the old Minimum Delivery Charges that all PG&E customers paid monthly to stay connected. The same page notes that the Energy Charge "will not affect your monthly bill but may increase the amount owed at True-Up."

The Base Services Charge cannot be paid off with solar credits. See [California's Base Services Charge and solar payback](/guides/california-base-services-charge-solar).

## How to read your true-up statement

1. **Account Summary.** The total amount due and the due date. PG&E says customers are responsible for any reconciled NEM charges at the annual anniversary.
2. **Summary of Your NEM True-Up Period Charges.** A month-by-month chart of net energy and the charge or credit for each month, split by time-of-use period.
3. **How Your True-Up Is Calculated.** Total NEM charges before taxes, plus taxes, minus any Net Surplus Compensation, which appears as a negative number.

## Why is my true-up bill so high?

Common causes:

- **More evening use.** Peak hours, typically 4 to 9 p.m., are priced higher, and solar produces little then.
- **New loads.** An EV, pool pump or heat pump added after the system was sized.
- **Lower production.** Shade from new growth, dirty panels, or an inverter fault. Compare your production to the installer's estimate.
- **Not watching the balance.** PG&E's explainer says to monitor the NEM summary monthly "so you can adjust your energy use" and budget for the payment.

## How to avoid a surprise true-up

- Check the year-to-date NEM charges on every monthly statement.
- Set money aside each month equal to the year-to-date balance divided by the months so far.
- Shift flexible use, such as laundry and EV charging, out of peak hours.
- If you are on NEM 2 and add a battery, read [adding a battery to a NEM 2 system](/guides/add-battery-to-nem-2-system) first so you do not lose NEM 2.

## Community Choice Aggregator (CCA) customers

If a CCA buys your power and PG&E delivers it, PG&E's explainer says Net Surplus Compensation may vary. Check the statements from both PG&E and your CCA.
