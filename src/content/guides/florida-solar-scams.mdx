Most Florida solar complaints don't involve fake panels. They come from sales pitches: savings that never show up, loans that cost more than expected, and promises about tax credits or "free" power. Florida law gives you real protection. Every solar contract must come with a separate written disclosure, and you have at least 3 business days to cancel. In 2026, two common pitches are simply false: there is no 30% federal credit, and FPL customers can't get a $0 bill.

## Key numbers

- **Complaints:** WFLX reported nearly **750** complaints to the Florida Attorney General against three solar companies it examined, with formal investigations into two (June 30, 2026).
- **Right to cancel:** at least **3 business days** after signing a solar contract (s. 520.23); door-to-door sales can be cancelled until midnight of the **third business day** (s. 501.025).
- **Federal 25D credit:** **$0** for systems placed in service after **December 31, 2025** (IRS).
- **FPL minimum bill:** **$30** a month, with kWh credits rather than cash for extra solar (FPL, October 2026).
- **Disclosure type size:** at least **12-point**, on a separate, separately signed statement (s. 520.23).

## Common solar scams and red flags in Florida

| The pitch | What's actually true |
|---|---|
| "The government pays 30% back." | The federal residential credit ended for systems placed in service after December 31, 2025 ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/residential-clean-energy-credit)). |
| "You'll never pay FPL again." | FPL has a $30 minimum base bill, and extra solar earns kWh credits, not money ([FPL FAQ](https://www.fpl.com/clean-energy/net-metering/faq.html)). |
| "It's a free government program." | Florida's disclosure must list the total cost, including interest and fees. Read that number. |
| "Sign today or lose the price." | You still have at least 3 business days to cancel in writing. |
| "Your rates go up every year, so savings will grow." | Florida law requires a seller giving savings estimates to describe its assumptions and warn that future utility rates are uncertain. Ask for them. |
| "We'll handle everything; just sign the tablet." | The disclosure must be a separate statement you acknowledge separately. Get a copy before anyone starts work. |

The WFLX report shows how these pitches play out. Families described similar door-to-door sales visits. One homeowner was locked into a $60,000 loan over 25 years, another had a $40,000 loan, and a couple said their panels never lowered FPL bills of nearly $300 a month ([WFLX, June 2026](https://www.wflx.com/2026/06/30/solar-panel-complaints-mount-treasure-coast-attorney-general-investigates-companies/)).

## What a Florida solar contract must disclose

Section [520.23](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0500-0599/0520/Sections/0520.23.html) requires every agreement to sell or lease a home solar system to include a written statement, in at least 12-point type, separate from the agreement and separately acknowledged by you. It must include, among other items:

- The installer's name, contact details and **valid state contractor license number**
- The **DBPR** customer contact center phone number
- Whether you are **buying or leasing**
- The **total cost**, including interest, installation, document, service and other fees
- A **payment schedule**, including what's due at signing and at completion
- Each tax incentive or rebate the seller **relied on to set the price**
- The **assumptions** behind any savings estimate
- Make and model of major parts, system size, estimated **first-year production** and yearly decline
- Any one-time or recurring fees, including removal and maintenance fees
- Whether the seller will place a **lien** on your home or file a **UCC-1** financing statement on the system
- Roof warranties and a warning to consider your roof's age (see [solar and your Florida roof](/guides/florida-roof-age-solar-insurance))
- A warning that panels may affect your insurance premiums
- Your right to rescind for **at least 3 business days**

If the seller listed a federal tax credit as something it relied on to set the price of a 2026 install, that's a red flag on paper.

## Can you cancel a solar contract in Florida?

Yes, within the window. The solar disclosure law requires a right to rescind "for a period of at least 3 business days after the agreement is signed." For sales made at your home, section [501.025](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0500-0599/0501/Sections/0501.025.html) separately gives you the right to cancel "until midnight of the third business day after the day on which the buyer signs." Cancellation is by written notice. The statute says a mailed notice counts once it's postmarked, and it need not take any particular form.

Send it in writing to the address on the contract, keep a copy, and use a mailing method with proof of date. If financing was arranged through the seller, contact the lender too.

## How to check a Florida solar contractor's license

Florida law defines a "solar contractor" as one whose work covers installing and repairing photovoltaic systems, and also lets licensed general, building and residential contractors do solar work within their scope ([s. 489.105](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0400-0499/0489/Sections/0489.105.html)). The license number has to be on your disclosure. Look it up with the Department of Business and Professional Regulation before you sign, and make sure the name on the license matches the company on the contract.

## Questions to ask before you sign

The FTC's [solar guide](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/solar-power-your-home) suggests settling these first:

- **Buy, lease or PPA?** Each one changes who owns the system and who gets any incentives. See [solar lease vs buy](/guides/solar-lease-vs-buy).
- **Will you move?** The FTC notes a home solar system is designed to stay on the home for at least 20 years. Ask how a lease or loan affects selling your house.
- **What happens if the panels underperform?** Florida's disclosure must describe any performance or production guarantee. If there isn't one, assume the loan payments don't change.

## If something already went wrong

WFLX reported that Seniors vs. Crime, part of the Attorney General's office, offers free mediation to help people cancel contracts and recover money. You can also contact DBPR about the contractor, using the number your disclosure must list.

Before you sign anything new, check whether the savings are real for your home: run the [Florida solar payback calculator](/us/solar-payback/florida) with your own FPL, Duke or TECO bill, and read [red flags in a solar quote](/blog/solar-quote-red-flags-2026) and [how to compare solar quotes](/guides/how-to-compare-solar-quotes).
