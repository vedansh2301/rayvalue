The federal Canada Greener Homes Grant and Loan are closed to new solar buyers. The program that replaced them, the Canada Greener Homes Affordability Program, is aimed at low-to-median-income households and lists insulation and heat pumps, not solar. What is left for solar is provincial: Ontario, British Columbia and Quebec each pay $1,000 per kW, with different caps and rules.

## Key numbers

- **Grant closed**: 31 Dec 2025 was the last day to upload documents; payment requests are no longer accepted (NRCan, checked 22 Nov 2026).
- **Applications** were open from **May 2021 to February 2024** (NRCan).
- **409,000+ households**, average grant **about $4,430**, **$1.8 billion** in total grants (NRCan).
- **Loan closed**: "New loan applications cannot be approved" (NRCan).
- **Ontario**: up to $5,000 for solar and $5,000 for a battery (Home Renovation Savings).
- **BC**: up to $5,000 for solar; battery up to $1,500, or $5,000 with Peak Saver (BC Hydro).
- **Quebec**: $1,000 per kW, up to 40% of cost (Hydro-Québec LogisVert, 31 Mar 2026).

## What happened to the Canada Greener Homes Grant?

NRCan's page now reads "Closed". It says December 31, 2025 "was the final day for applicants to upload their documents" and that "requests for payments are no longer accepted". New applications had stopped long before: the intake ran from May 2021 to February 2024.

NRCan's summary of results: more than 409,000 households upgraded, the average grant was about $4,430, and the program paid $1.8 billion in total. More than 230,000 heat pumps were installed.

If a salesperson tells you in 2026 that you can still claim the federal Greener Homes Grant for solar, they are wrong. Ask them for the government page that says so.

## Is the Greener Homes Loan still open?

No, not for new applicants. NRCan's loan page carries a "Program closure" notice: "New loan applications cannot be approved." People whose loans were already approved keep that funding and can continue their projects.

The loan was interest-free, so its loss hurts as much as the grant for people who were planning to finance a system.

## Does the Greener Homes Affordability Program cover solar?

The Canada Greener Homes Affordability Program (CGHAP) is a different kind of program. NRCan says it "provides low-to-median-income homeowners and tenants with no-cost home retrofits, such as insulation and heat pumps". It began in 2025 and is delivered through participating provinces and territories using a direct-install approach.

Two points matter for solar buyers:

- **You cannot apply to Ottawa.** NRCan says the federal government "is not accepting applications from households". You apply through your province's program.
- **Solar is not on the list of examples.** NRCan's examples are insulation and heat pumps. If your province's CGHAP program covers solar, it will say so on its own page.

## What solar rebates are left in Canada?

| Province | Program | Solar | Battery | Key condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ontario | Home Renovation Savings (IESO and Enbridge Gas) | $1,000/kW, up to 50% of costs, max $5,000 | $300/kWh, up to 50%, max $5,000 | No net metering if you take it |
| British Columbia | BC Hydro solar and battery rebates | $1,000/kW DC, up to 50% of cost, max $5,000 | $500/kWh, up to 50%, max $1,500 ($5,000 with Peak Saver) | First come, first served while funding lasts |
| Quebec | Hydro-Québec LogisVert | $1,000/kW installed, up to 40% of cost | Not in the LogisVert solar measure | Installed from 30 June 2025; master electrician connection |
| Alberta | None found province-wide | | | Exports credited at retail rate for systems under 150 kW |

All rebate figures were checked on 22 November 2026, except Quebec, which comes from the LogisVert guide dated 31 March 2026.

### Ontario

The Home Renovation Savings program is the most generous for solar plus storage: up to $10,000 combined. But you give up net metering. See [Ontario solar and battery rebates explained](/ca/guides/ontario-solar-battery-rebates).

### British Columbia

BC Hydro pays rebates on both. Battery-only installs qualify only for residential customers who enroll in Peak Saver. Net metering is closed to new customers, who now get a fixed 10¢ per kWh for exports. See [BC Hydro battery rebate and the end of net metering](/ca/guides/bc-hydro-battery-rebate-net-metering-end).

### Quebec

Hydro-Québec's LogisVert grant is the newest. It requires panels certified to CSA and IEC standards, bought in Quebec, and a connection request completed by a master electrician. See [Subvention solaire Hydro-Québec](/ca/guides/subvention-solaire-hydro-quebec).

### Alberta

We found no province-wide solar rebate. Alberta's micro-generation rules still help: small systems are credited at their retail energy rate and do not pay the ordinary costs of interconnection. See [Is solar worth it in Alberta?](/ca/blog/is-solar-worth-it-alberta).

## Is it worth waiting for a new federal program?

We found no announced federal solar grant to replace Greener Homes as of 22 November 2026. Waiting has a cost: every year without panels is a year of full grid bills, and provincial rebates are first come, first served or can change. If the numbers work today with your provincial rebate, waiting for a federal program that has not been announced is a gamble.

## How do I avoid Greener Homes scams?

NRCan warns that it "has not approved any third parties to make unsolicited contact with homeowners" for these programs, and that the government never phones, emails or knocks on doors to sell heating and cooling products. Treat any caller offering "Greener Homes" money in 2026 with suspicion.
