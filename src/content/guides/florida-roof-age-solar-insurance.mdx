If your roof is near the end of its life, deal with it before you add solar. Florida law makes every solar seller warn you that replacing a roof later "may require reinstallment" of the system. The state's insurance law also ties coverage to roof age at 15 years, and the FTC notes a home solar system is designed to stay on the roof for at least 20 years. Plan the roof and the panels together.

## Key numbers

- **Under 15 years:** an insurer may not refuse to issue or renew a homeowners policy "solely because of the age of the roof" (s. 627.7011(5), read November 2026).
- **15 years or older:** you can get a roof inspection at your expense before the insurer can require replacement. If it shows **5 years or more** of useful life left, the insurer can't refuse solely for roof age.
- **The 25% rule:** if a roof built to the **2007** Florida Building Code or later needs **25% or more** repaired or replaced, only that part must meet the current code (s. 553.844(5)).
- **Disclosures:** every Florida solar sale or lease must come with a separate written disclosure in at least **12-point** type that covers roof age, roof warranties and insurance (s. 520.23).
- **No federal credit:** the 25D credit is $0 for 2026+ installs, so a roof plus solar is a fully self-funded project.

## Should I replace my roof before installing solar in Florida?

Florida's solar disclosure law ([s. 520.23](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0500-0599/0520/Sections/0520.23.html)) requires the seller to give you a statement in substantially this form: "You should consider the age and remaining life of your roof prior to installing a distributed energy generation system. Replacement of your roof may require reinstallment of the distributed energy generation system."

That isn't small print. Removing panels, reroofing and putting them back is a separate job with a separate bill. A practical way to decide:

| Roof situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| New or recently replaced roof | Go ahead. Ask the installer how roof penetrations are sealed and warranted. |
| Roof around 10 to 15 years old | Get a roof inspection first. Price solar with and without a roof replacement. |
| Roof 15+ years, or the insurer is asking about it | Expect insurance questions. Replace or inspect before panels go on. |
| Partial damage on a roof built to the 2007 code or later | The 25% rule may let you repair only the damaged section. |

The FTC's [solar guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/solar-power-your-home) makes the same point from the other side: a residential system is designed to stay on the home for at least 20 years.

## How roof age affects your homeowners insurance

Section [627.7011(5)](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0600-0699/0627/Sections/0627.7011.html) sets the rules:

- **Under 15 years old:** an insurer "may not refuse to issue or refuse to renew" a homeowners policy solely because of roof age.
- **15 years or older:** before requiring a replacement as a condition of coverage, the insurer must let you have the roof inspected by an authorized inspector at your expense. If the inspection shows 5 years or more of useful life left, the insurer can't refuse to issue or renew solely because of roof age.
- **Who can inspect:** the statute lists licensed home inspectors, certified building code inspectors, licensed general, building or residential contractors or roofing contractors, professional engineers, architects, and others the insurer recognizes.
- **How age is counted:** from the last date when 100% of the roof's surface was built or replaced under the code in force at the time.

Why this matters for solar: if your roof is 13 years old today, it crosses 15 during the first years of a solar loan. Reroofing then means paying to remove and reinstall the panels too.

## Will solar panels affect my Florida insurance premium?

The law makes the seller tell you it might. One required statement reads, in substance: "Placing a distributed energy generation system on your roof may impact your future insurance premiums. You are responsible for contacting your insurance carrier, prior to entering into a purchase or lease agreement."

The disclosure also has to say you are responsible for insuring the system against loss or damage, and for leases, whether the leasing company insures it. Before you sign:

1. Call your insurer with the system size, cost and whether you will own or lease it.
2. Ask whether coverage limits need to rise to cover the panels.
3. Ask whether the roof's age or condition will be reviewed when you report the change.

## The 25% reroofing rule after storm damage

Section [553.844(5)](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0500-0599/0553/Sections/0553.844.html) says that if a roof was built, repaired or replaced to the 2007 Florida Building Code or a later edition, and 25% or more of it is being repaired, replaced or recovered, only the repaired part must meet the current code. Local governments can't amend that exception.

For a solar home, this can mean a storm repair to one section doesn't automatically force a full reroof. If the panels sit on the damaged section, they still have to come off for the repair.

## Roof questions to settle before you sign

Florida's disclosure law requires a solar contract to describe "any roof warranties". Use that requirement:

- Who warrants roof leaks around the mounts, and for how long?
- Does the installer's work void your roofer's warranty?
- What does it cost to remove and reinstall the panels later? Get it in writing.
- For a lease: can you take the system down for a reroof, and who pays?
