Most home solar panels in England do not need planning permission. Roof panels on a house are "permitted development" if they stick out no more than 200mm from the roof, sit no higher than the ridge, and the home is not a listed building or on a scheduled monument site. The rules were updated on 27 August 2026, and you can use either the old or new version until 27 August 2027.

## Key numbers

- **200mm**: maximum projection from a pitched roof slope (Planning Portal, checked 22 Nov 2026).
- **600mm**: maximum height above the highest part of a flat roof.
- **0.2m / 0.4m**: new wall, balcony and roof-enclosure limits for houses, next to a highway / elsewhere (rules from 27 Aug 2026).
- **27 August 2027**: end of the transition period in which the old rules can still be used.
- **9 square metres and 4 metres**: maximum array size and height for stand-alone (ground-mounted) solar at a house, with lower height limits near boundaries.

## Do you need planning permission for solar panels in England?

The Planning Portal's guidance for England says installing solar panels on homes "may be 'permitted development' with no need to apply to the local authority for planning permission", provided the limits and conditions are met. It also advises checking with your local planning authority if you are unsure.

## What are the rules for roof panels on a house?

These apply under the rules from 27 August 2026. All of them must be met:

| Rule | Limit |
|---|---|
| Pitched roof | Not above the highest part of the roof (excluding the chimney); no more than 200mm from the roof slope |
| Flat roof | Highest part of the equipment no more than 600mm above the highest part of the roof (excluding chimney) |
| Wall, balcony or roof enclosure next to a highway | No more than 0.2m beyond the plane of the wall |
| Other walls, balconies or roof enclosures | No more than 0.4m |
| Listed buildings and scheduled monuments | Not on a building within the grounds of a listed building or on a scheduled monument site |
| Conservation areas and World Heritage Sites | Not on a wall, balcony or roof enclosure that fronts a highway |
| Plug-in solar | Not on a wooden wall, balcony or enclosure, or any timber-clad exterior |

Two conditions also apply. Equipment should be sited, so far as practicable, to minimise its effect on the building's appearance and the area. And it should be removed as soon as reasonably practicable when no longer needed.

## What were the rules before 27 August 2026?

The older rules were similar for roofs: not above the highest part of the roof, no more than 200mm from the roof slope or wall surface, 600mm on flat roofs, and the same listed building and conservation area limits. The new rules add the separate limits for walls, balconies and roof enclosures on houses and the plug-in solar condition. Until 27 August 2027 you may follow whichever set suits your project.

## Do I need permission in a conservation area or national park?

Not automatically. Roof panels can still be permitted development in a conservation area, provided they are not on a wall (or, under the new rules, a balcony or roof enclosure) that fronts a highway. For flat roofs on "Article 2(3)" land, you must apply to the council for prior approval first. Article 2(3) land includes national parks and the Broads, Areas of Outstanding Natural Beauty, conservation areas and World Heritage Sites.

If your home is listed, or within the grounds of a listed building, permitted development does not cover the panels, so you need to apply.

## What about flats?

For a block of flats, the panel limits are similar: not above the highest part of a pitched roof, no more than 200mm from the wall or roof slope, and 600mm on flat roofs. The Planning Portal notes that leaseholders may need permission from their landlord, freeholder or management company, which is a separate question from planning.

## Can I put solar panels in my garden?

Stand-alone solar in the grounds of a house is also permitted development within limits:

- The array must be no more than 9 square metres.
- Height must be no more than 1m if within 5m of the boundary and in front of the main elevation, 2m if within 5m of the boundary or nearer the highway in a conservation area, and 4m otherwise.
- Not within the grounds of a listed building or scheduled monument.
- On Article 2(3) land within 5m of the boundary, or in a conservation area closer to the highway than the house, you need prior approval.

## Do building regulations apply?

Normally, yes. The Planning Portal says that if you install solar panels on your roof, building regulations will normally apply. The roof must be able to carry the weight, and the electrical installation is covered too. Using an installer in a [Competent Person Scheme](https://www.gov.uk/building-regulations-competent-person-schemes) lets them self-certify. Keep the paperwork: you will need it when you sell (see [solar panels and selling your house](/uk/blog/solar-panels-selling-house-mortgage)).

## What about Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland?

Each has its own planning system. The Planning Portal notes its guidance is for England and that policy in Wales may differ. Check with your local planning authority before you start. For Northern Ireland costs and tariffs, see our [Northern Ireland solar guide](/uk/guides/solar-panels-northern-ireland-guide).
