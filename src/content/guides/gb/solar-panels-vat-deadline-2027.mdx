Installing solar panels or a home battery in the UK carries 0% VAT until 31 March 2027. From 1 April 2027 the rate goes back to 5%, and the Budget 2026 papers confirmed that. If you are already thinking about solar, finishing before the deadline saves 5% of the installed price.

## Key numbers

- **0% VAT** on installed solar panels in homes, 1 May 2023 to 31 March 2027 (HMRC Notice 708/6, checked 22 Nov 2026).
- **0% VAT** on home batteries, including batteries added to existing solar, from 1 February 2024 to the same end date (HMRC Notice 708/6, section 2.19).
- **5% VAT** from 1 April 2027, reconfirmed in the Budget 2026 overview of tax legislation (published October 2026).
- **£1,546 per kW**: median installed cost of 4–10 kW MCS solar systems in 2025/26, batteries excluded (DESNZ solar cost data).
- **£15,000**: maximum Warm Homes Loan for solar panels, and another £15,000 for a battery (GOV.UK factsheet, 27 Oct 2026).

## Is there VAT on solar panels in the UK?

Not at the moment. HMRC's VAT Notice 708/6 says a zero rate applies to the installation of certain energy-saving materials in residential accommodation from 1 May 2023 to 31 March 2027. Solar panels are on that list. HMRC defines them to include photovoltaic panels "with cabling, control panel and AC/DC inverter", so the main parts of a normal rooftop system are covered when they are supplied and installed together.

The relief is for installation. Buying panels yourself without installation is a different supply, so check with the seller before assuming it is zero-rated.

## Are home batteries VAT free too?

Yes. From 1 February 2024, HMRC added "batteries for storing energy converted from electricity" to the relief. Section 2.19 of the notice says this includes retrofitting a battery to store electricity from an existing microgeneration system such as solar panels, as well as batteries installed with new panels. The same 31 March 2027 end date applies.

## What changes on 1 April 2027?

HMRC's notice says that from 1 April 2027 supplies of installations of energy-saving materials "will revert to the reduced rate of VAT of 5%". The Budget 2026 overview of tax legislation and rates says the same: "the rate of VAT relief for energy-saving materials will revert to 5 per cent from 1 April 2027."

The Budget also announced two related changes, which are easy to confuse with this one:

| Change | What it covers | Status (22 Nov 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| ESM relief reverts to 5% | Installing solar, batteries, heat pumps, insulation | From 1 April 2027 (HMRC, OOTLAR) |
| Heat batteries added to ESM relief | Thermal stores such as heat batteries | From 1 April 2027, by secondary legislation (OOTLAR) |
| 0% VAT on domestic electricity extended | Your electricity bill in Great Britain | Announced to 30 June 2027; legislation still to be laid (OOTLAR) |

So the electricity bill relief is being extended, but the installation relief is not.

## How much does 5% add to a typical quote?

DESNZ publishes the installed cost of MCS-certified solar from MCS data. For 2025/26 (April 2025 to March 2026) the median was £1,595 per kW for systems up to 4 kW and £1,546 per kW for 4–10 kW systems. These figures exclude batteries and are total installed cost, which was mostly at the zero rate during that period.

Simple arithmetic, not a quote: a 4 kW system at the 4–10 kW median of £1,546 per kW is about £6,184. Adding 5% VAT would make it about £6,493, roughly £309 more. On a quote that also includes a battery, the 5% applies to the battery as well, so the difference grows with the size of the job.

That is the whole effect. 5% is useful money, but it should not push you into a rushed decision or a poor installer.

## How to make sure you get the 0% rate

1. **Leave time.** Surveys, any planning checks and grid notification all take time. If you want the zero rate, aim to finish well before late March 2027 rather than on the last week.
2. **Get the VAT line in writing.** Your quote should show the VAT rate and amount. If an installer adds 20% VAT to a domestic install before April 2027, ask why.
3. **Check the battery is on the same paperwork.** A battery fitted to an existing system also qualifies until 31 March 2027, but make sure it is invoiced as an installation.
4. **Ask what happens if the job slips.** If the install could move past 31 March 2027, ask the installer how they will treat VAT and whether the price is fixed.

## Can I spread the cost?

The UK-wide Warm Homes Loan Scheme launched on 27 October 2026. The government factsheet lists loans of up to £15,000 for solar panels and up to £15,000 for an electrical storage battery, through ten lenders at launch, with some offering interest-free loans. The scheme works with MCS-certified installers. A loan does not change the VAT treatment, but it can make finishing before April 2027 more realistic.

## Does the deadline change whether solar is worth it?

Only at the margin. The bigger drivers are how much sunshine your roof gets, how much of the power you use yourself and which export tariff you choose. Rates and dates were checked on 22 November 2026. VAT treatment of a specific job depends on the facts, so confirm with your installer, and with HMRC if anything is unclear.
