Solar works differently in Northern Ireland. There is no Smart Export Guarantee and no Ofgem price cap, so export income depends on your supplier: Power NI pays 11.88p per kWh from October 2026. Installation is still 0% VAT until 31 March 2027, the UK-wide Warm Homes Loan applies, and a Belfast roof gets about as much sun as Manchester.

## Key numbers

- **11.88p per kWh**: Power NI microgeneration export tariff, 1 Oct 2026 to 30 Sep 2027 (Power NI, checked 22 Nov 2026).
- **32.52p per kWh excluding VAT**: Power NI maximum average charge from 1 July 2026, up from 30.62p (Utility Regulator).
- **5% VAT** on household electricity in NI; the 0% electricity rate is for Great Britain only (HMRC).
- **0% VAT** on installed solar and batteries until **31 March 2027**, then 5% (HMRC Notice 708/6).
- **922 kWh per kW a year**: PVGIS yield for Belfast, optimal angle (PVGIS 5.3).

## Does the Smart Export Guarantee apply in Northern Ireland?

No. Ofgem states that SEG "installations must be located in Great Britain". Rates you see in GB comparison tables, including ours in [SEG rates compared](/uk/guides/best-seg-rates), do not apply in NI.

Power NI publishes an annual export price for microgeneration. For 1 October 2026 to 30 September 2027 it is **11.88p per kWh**. Power NI says the tariff is approved by the Utility Regulator each year, that new rates appear towards the end of September, and that payments go directly into your bank account, separate from your electricity bill. It also says prices are subject to fluctuation each year.

## How much does electricity cost in Northern Ireland?

Northern Ireland is outside the Ofgem price cap. The Utility Regulator approves Power NI's regulated tariff instead. From 1 July 2026, Power NI's maximum average charge rose from 30.62p to 32.52p per kWh, excluding VAT.

VAT on NI household electricity stays at 5%. HMRC's paper on the temporary zero rate says the order "will only apply to supplies made in Great Britain" and that NI supplies "will continue to be charged at the reduced rate". So 32.52p plus 5% VAT is about 34.15p.

| | Northern Ireland | Great Britain |
|---|---|---|
| Export scheme | Supplier-set (Power NI 11.88p) | Smart Export Guarantee |
| Import price benchmark | Power NI max average 32.52p ex VAT (from 1 Jul 2026) | Ofgem cap 26.32p, Oct–Dec 2026 |
| VAT on electricity | 5% | 0% (1 Oct 2026 to 31 Mar 2027) |
| VAT on installing solar | 0% until 31 Mar 2027 | 0% until 31 Mar 2027 |
| Warm Homes Loan | Yes | Yes |

The two import figures are measured differently, so treat the comparison as a guide only. Each kWh you use from your panels saves your full unit rate, so higher NI prices make self-use more valuable.

## Is there VAT on solar panels in Northern Ireland?

Not until 31 March 2027. HMRC's VAT Notice 708/6 says a zero rate applies to installing energy-saving materials, including solar panels and batteries, from 1 May 2023 to 31 March 2027, reverting to 5% from 1 April 2027. The notice records that Northern Ireland moved from the 5% rate to this zero rate on 1 May 2023. See [Solar panels VAT: 0% until 31 March 2027](/uk/guides/solar-panels-vat-deadline-2027).

## How much sun does Northern Ireland get?

PVGIS, the European Commission's solar model, gives Belfast about 922 kWh per kW a year at the best angle. That is close to Edinburgh (908) and Manchester (888), and below London (1,023). A 4 kW system in Belfast would make about 3,690 kWh in an average year. East and west roofs in Belfast produce about 79% of south-facing output; see [our orientation guide](/uk/guides/east-west-facing-solar-panels). Monthly figures are in [do solar panels work in winter?](/uk/guides/solar-panels-winter-uk).

## How much does solar cost in Northern Ireland?

There is no official NI figure. The DESNZ cost data that covers England, Scotland and Wales states that "Northern Ireland installations accredited through the MCS are excluded". The GB medians, £1,546 to £1,595 per kW in 2025/26, are a reasonable starting point for comparison, but NI quotes may differ. See [how much solar panels cost in the UK](/uk/guides/how-much-do-solar-panels-cost-uk).

## Can I get a loan or grant?

The [Warm Homes Loan](/uk/guides/warm-homes-loan-solar-battery) covers the whole UK, including Northern Ireland: up to £15,000 for solar and £15,000 for a battery, through participating lenders and MCS-certified installers. England's Warm Homes: Local Grant does not apply in NI.

## What about "free" panels on a lease?

UK Finance and the Building Societies Association note that a market of providers offering free panel installation emerged in NI, funded by renewables obligation certificates. They say that in NI the arrangement should be a "Lease of Rights" rather than a lease, that most lenders require their consent, and that a lender may refuse to lend on a property with an existing panel arrangement. Read [solar panels and selling your house](/uk/blog/solar-panels-selling-house-mortgage) before signing one.

## Is solar worth it in Northern Ireland?

The ingredients are a higher import price than GB, a fixed annual export price of 11.88p from Power NI, and similar sunshine to northern England. Using more of your own power is what makes the numbers work. Get an MCS performance estimate and ask installers which export arrangement they assume. For the general case, see [are solar panels worth it in the UK?](/uk/guides/are-solar-panels-worth-it-uk)
