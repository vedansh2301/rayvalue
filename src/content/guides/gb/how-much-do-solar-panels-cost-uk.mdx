A typical UK home solar system cost about £1,546 to £1,595 per kW in 2025/26, based on official government data from MCS-certified installs. That puts a 4 kW system at roughly £6,200 and a 6 kW system at roughly £9,300, before any battery. Prices have fallen each year since 2023/24.

## Key numbers

- **£1,595 per kW**: median installed cost of systems up to 4 kW, April 2025 to March 2026 (DESNZ).
- **£1,546 per kW**: median for 4–10 kW systems, same period (DESNZ).
- **£1,424 to £2,101 per kW**: range of regional averages (means) across England, Scotland and Wales, 2025/26 (DESNZ).
- **Batteries are not included** in these figures (DESNZ note 1).
- **0% VAT** on installed solar until **31 March 2027**, then 5% (HMRC Notice 708/6, checked 22 Nov 2026).

## How much do solar panels cost in the UK?

The Department for Energy Security and Net Zero (DESNZ) publishes installed costs from the MCS database each year. The 2025/26 workbook covers installations from April 2025 to March 2026. DESNZ says the cost includes "generation equipment, cost of installing and connecting to the electricity supply and VAT where applicable". It does not include extended warranties, other works, or "additional devices, such as battery storage".

| System size | Median £/kW | Mean £/kW | 3 kW / 4 kW / 6 kW at the median |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–4 kW | £1,595 | £1,808 | 3 kW: about £4,785 |
| 4–10 kW | £1,546 | £1,622 | 4 kW: about £6,184 · 6 kW: about £9,276 |
| 10–50 kW | £1,204 | £1,267 | (mostly non-domestic) |

The system totals are simple multiplication of the median by the size. The median is the middle install, so half of jobs cost less per kW and half cost more. The mean is higher because some expensive jobs pull the average up.

## Are solar panels getting cheaper?

Yes. The official medians have fallen two years in a row:

| Year | 0–4 kW median | 4–10 kW median |
|---|---|---|
| 2023/24 | £1,910 | £2,067 |
| 2024/25 | £1,667 | £1,641 |
| 2025/26 | £1,595 | £1,546 |

From 2023/24 to 2025/26 the 4–10 kW median fell by £521 per kW, about a quarter. DESNZ's commentary says the average cost fell for all sizes compared with 2024/25.

## Does the cost depend on where I live?

Somewhat. DESNZ publishes mean costs by region. These include domestic and non-domestic installs and exclude Northern Ireland.

| Region (2025/26) | 0–4 kW mean £/kW | 4–10 kW mean £/kW |
|---|---|---|
| North East | £1,760 | £1,540 |
| Scotland | £1,767 | £1,578 |
| West Midlands | £1,778 | £1,628 |
| East of England | £1,796 | £1,663 |
| North West | £1,800 | £1,627 |
| Yorkshire and The Humber | £1,800 | £1,617 |
| Wales | £1,838 | £1,424 |
| East Midlands | £1,840 | £1,642 |
| South East | £1,859 | £1,721 |
| South West | £1,887 | £1,631 |
| London | £2,101 | £1,759 |

London is the most expensive region in both size bands. Wales has the lowest 4–10 kW mean. Northern Ireland installs are excluded from the statistics; see our [Northern Ireland solar guide](/uk/guides/solar-panels-northern-ireland-guide).

## Why does a bigger system cost less per kW?

Some costs are fixed for each job: scaffolding, the inverter, the survey, the electrical connection and paperwork. Spread over more panels, they add less per kW. That is why the 4–10 kW median is lower than the 0–4 kW median, and why 10–50 kW systems are cheaper again. A bigger system still costs more in total, and is only worth it if you can use or export the extra output at a fair rate.

## What is not included in these figures?

- **Batteries.** DESNZ excludes battery storage. There is no official UK series for installed battery cost, so ask for the battery as a separate line on your quote. Our [battery guide](/uk/guides/solar-battery-worth-it-uk) explains how batteries earn their keep.
- **Extended warranties and other works.** Roof repairs, bird mesh, extra monitoring and similar extras are outside the DESNZ figures.
- **Northern Ireland.** Not in the statistics.

## How do I use this to check a quote?

1. Take the solar part of the quote, without the battery.
2. Divide by the system size in kW.
3. Compare with the median for your size band and the mean for your region.

A quote well above both deserves questions. It may be justified by a difficult roof or premium panels, but you should know why. Our [UK solar quote red flags](/uk/blog/solar-panel-scams-uk) list other warning signs.

## Will the price change after March 2027?

The VAT relief ends. Installed solar and batteries are zero-rated until 31 March 2027, and HMRC says the rate reverts to 5% from 1 April 2027. On a £6,184 system, 5% would add about £309. See [Solar panels VAT: 0% until 31 March 2027](/uk/guides/solar-panels-vat-deadline-2027).

If the upfront cost is the obstacle, the [Warm Homes Loan](/uk/guides/warm-homes-loan-solar-battery) lends up to £15,000 for solar and £15,000 for a battery. Whether the cost is worth paying depends on your roof and how you use power: see [are solar panels worth it in the UK?](/uk/guides/are-solar-panels-worth-it-uk).

Figures are from the DESNZ 2025/26 workbook, checked on 22 November 2026.
