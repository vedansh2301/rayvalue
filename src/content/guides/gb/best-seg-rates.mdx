The best Smart Export Guarantee (SEG) rate depends on whether you will also buy your electricity from the same company. Rates open to anyone run from 2p to 6p per kWh. Rates tied to your import supplier, or to buying solar from the supplier, run from 12p to 25p. Pick the tariff that gives the best total of import cost and export income, not just the highest export number.

## Key numbers

- **2.0p/kWh**: EDF SEG Export Variable since 28 October 2026, down from 3.0p (EDF rate history).
- **6p/kWh**: E.ON Next Flex Export, the highest open rate we found (variable, checked 22 Nov 2026).
- **12p/kWh**: Outgoing Octopus, British Gas Export Premium, Ecotricity, Good Energy Solar Savings and OVO SEG Beyond Exclusive (each tied to supply).
- **26.32p/kWh**: average capped unit rate you avoid for each kWh you use yourself, Oct–Dec 2026 (Ofgem).
- **12 mandatory and 4 voluntary** SEG licensees for 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027 (Ofgem).

## What is the Smart Export Guarantee?

The SEG is Ofgem's scheme that makes larger suppliers offer a paid export tariff to small generators. Ofgem says it covers solar PV and other technologies up to 5 MW, installations must be in Great Britain, and "SEG tariff rates must always be above zero". Suppliers set the rate, the contract length and the other terms. There is no minimum rate beyond that.

For the 2026/27 SEG year, Ofgem lists 12 mandatory licensees (British Gas, E (Gas and Electricity), E.ON Next, EDF, Utility Warehouse, Foxglove, Fuse, Octopus, OVO, ScottishPower, So Energy and Utilita) and 4 voluntary ones. Ecotricity and Good Energy are not on the list, so their export tariffs are their own commercial offers.

## Is a tied tariff always better?

Not always. A 12p export rate is worth about 8p more per exported kWh than a 4.1p rate. If the import tariff that comes with it costs more than the price cap, the extra you pay on every kWh you buy can cancel the gain. Compare both sides:

1. Estimate how much you export in a year. Your export meter or app shows this.
2. Multiply by the difference in export rates.
3. Compare the import unit rate and standing charge with your current tariff and with the cap.

A home that exports 2,000 kWh a year would earn about £82 at 4.1p and £240 at 12p, a £158 difference. That difference has to beat any extra import cost.

## What do I need to get paid?

- **Certification.** Suppliers ask for MCS (or equivalent, such as Flexi-Orb) certification. Keep your MCS certificate.
- **A smart export meter.** E.ON Next, for example, requires a meter that records export every half hour.
- **A system in Great Britain.** Northern Ireland is not covered by the SEG.

## How does this affect whether solar is worth it?

Export is usually the smaller part of the value. Each kWh you use at home is worth around 26p at the current cap, compared with 2p to 18p exported. See [Are solar panels worth it in the UK?](/uk/guides/are-solar-panels-worth-it-uk) and, if you export a lot in the evening, [Home batteries and time-of-use tariffs](/uk/guides/solar-battery-worth-it-uk).

Rates change without much notice, and several here are variable. Check the supplier's page before you switch.
