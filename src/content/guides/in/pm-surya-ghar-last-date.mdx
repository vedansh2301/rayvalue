The PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana is approved **till FY 2026-27**, so the last date is **31 March 2027**. No extension had been announced as of 22 November 2026. The subsidy is only paid after your system is installed, net-metered and inspected, so treat 31 March 2027 as the date your system should be *approved*, not the date you start.

RayValue is an independent site, not a government portal. Applications go only through [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/).

## Key numbers

- **31 March 2027**: end of FY 2026-27, the period the scheme is approved for (MNRE guidelines).
- **₹75,021 crore**: total scheme outlay, of which ₹65,700 crore is for subsidies to homes (MNRE guidelines).
- **1 crore homes**: the scheme's target by 2026-27 (PIB factsheet, 13 Mar 2025).
- **65 lakh homes** installed by 4 Nov 2026, with more than 1 lakh installs every six days (News On AIR, quoting the Union Minister).
- **One to two months**: usual time from installation to subsidy release, per Delhi's Gazette of 17 Sep 2026.

## What is the last date to apply for PM Surya Ghar?

MNRE's operational guidelines open by saying the Cabinet approved the scheme on 29 February 2024 with an outlay of ₹75,021 crore, "to be implemented till FY 2026-27". India's financial year 2026-27 ends on **31 March 2027**.

Delhi's Gazette of 17 September 2026 repeats this in plain words: "Presently PM Suryaghar scheme is valid upto 31st March 2027."

Neither document says what happens to an application that is still in progress on that date. That is why the safe reading is to get your system fully commissioned and approved on the portal before 31 March 2027.

## How long does the process take?

Each step depends on your DISCOM and vendor. Here is the sequence from the PIB factsheet and MNRE guidelines:

| Step | Who acts | Typical delay risk |
|---|---|---|
| Register and apply on the portal | You | Low |
| Feasibility approval | DISCOM | Medium. Deemed approval up to 10 kW applies only once your state puts it into effect. |
| Choose vendor, agree price, install | You and vendor | Medium. It depends on vendor backlog. |
| Upload plant details, apply for net meter | You or vendor | Low |
| Net meter fitted, inspection, commissioning | DISCOM | High. This is the usual bottleneck. |
| Bank details, e-token redemption | You | Low |
| Subsidy release | Government | 15 days after DISCOM approval (MNRE target) |

Delhi's Gazette says subsidies "are released to the consumer only after installation and commissioning of the system, which ordinarily takes one to two months". Leave more room than that, because March is the end of the financial year and DISCOM and vendor queues will be longest then.

## Will the scheme be extended?

Nobody can say yet. We found no Cabinet or MNRE decision on extension as of 22 November 2026.

Two things are on the record:

- **The national target is not yet met.** The target is 1 crore homes. The Minister reported 65 lakh on 4 November 2026, with more than 1 lakh new installs every six days. At that pace, the 147 days from 4 November 2026 to 31 March 2027 would add roughly 24 to 25 lakh, leaving the total short of 1 crore. That is simple arithmetic on the Minister's figures, not a forecast.
- **States are planning around the date.** Delhi's free-solar plan for subsidised consumers depends on the central subsidy. Its Gazette says: "it will be reviewed, if PM Surya Ghar scheme is not extended beyond March 2027." Delhi's own state subsidy policy runs to 31 March 2030, but the central part does not.

An extension is possible but not promised. Don't plan on one.

## What should I do now?

1. **Check your bill first.** The subsidy only matters if solar pays for your home. Heavy users on high tariff slabs gain the most. See [Is rooftop solar worth it in India in 2026?](/in/guides/is-rooftop-solar-worth-it-india).
2. **Apply on the official portal early**, and register with your exact consumer number as it appears on your bill. Mismatches cause rejections.
3. **Get two or three quotes** from vendors registered on the National Portal, and confirm in writing that they will use DCR (made-in-India) panels. Non-DCR panels make the system ineligible.
4. **Ask the vendor about net meter timing** in your DISCOM area. That is usually the slowest step.
5. **Sort out finance before you start.** The subsidy arrives after installation. See [PM Surya Ghar loans](/in/guides/pm-surya-ghar-loan).
6. **Beware of "last chance" pressure.** Deadline urgency is a common sales tactic. No genuine vendor can "guarantee" a subsidy or speed up a DISCOM. See [Rooftop solar scams and fake subsidy sites](/in/guides/rooftop-solar-scams-fake-subsidy-sites).

## Does Delhi's own subsidy last longer?

Yes. Delhi's state top-up of ₹26,000 per kW (up to ₹78,000) comes from the Delhi Solar Energy Policy 2023 as amended on 17 September 2026. That policy runs to **31 March 2030**. The central PM Surya Ghar subsidy is a separate amount with its own end date of 31 March 2027. If the central scheme is not extended, a Delhi home installing after March 2027 could still qualify for the state part, but not the central part. This is our reading of the two documents, not an official ruling.

## How much is at stake?

The central subsidy is ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the 3rd kW, up to ₹78,000. It is ₹85,800 in special category states. Delhi residents can get up to another ₹78,000 from the state.
