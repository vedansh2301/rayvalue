Delhi now pays **₹26,000 per kW, up to ₹78,000**, on top of the central PM Surya Ghar subsidy. A 3 kW home system can therefore get up to **₹1,56,000** in combined subsidy, capped at the plant's cost. The change came in the Delhi Solar Energy Policy 2023 (Second Amendment), published in the Delhi Gazette on **17 September 2026**. It also ends generation-based incentives for new home systems and sets up a plan for free rooftop solar for Delhi's subsidised consumers.

RayValue is an independent site, not a government portal. Apply through [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/) or the [Delhi Solar Portal](https://solar.delhi.gov.in/page/state-subsidy).

## Key numbers

- **₹26,000 per kW**, capped at **₹78,000**: Delhi state capital subsidy for homes, up to 3 kW (Delhi Gazette, 17 Sep 2026).
- **₹78,000**: central PM Surya Ghar subsidy for 3 kW (MNRE).
- **₹11,000 per kWp**: Delhi subsidy for housing societies and RWAs, for common areas and EV charging, up to 500 kWp (Gazette).
- **₹5,000 per kW**: the most a household pays under the planned Utility-Led Aggregation (ULA) model (Gazette).
- **2,20,000** new residential rooftop systems targeted. The policy is valid to **31 March 2030** (Gazette).
- **1,623 kWh per kW per year**: PVGIS solar yield for New Delhi, optimally tilted (EU JRC, retrieved 22 Nov 2026).

## How much solar subsidy do I get in Delhi?

| System size | PM Surya Ghar (central) | Delhi state | Maximum combined |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 kW | ₹30,000 | ₹26,000 | ₹56,000 |
| 2 kW | ₹60,000 | ₹52,000 | ₹1,12,000 |
| 3 kW | ₹78,000 | ₹78,000 | ₹1,56,000 |
| 4 kW to 10 kW | ₹78,000 | ₹78,000 | ₹1,56,000 |

Two caps apply:

- **Delhi stops at 3 kW.** The Gazette's table gives ₹26,000 per kW up to 2 kW and from 2 to 3 kW, and "no additional subsidy" from 3 kW to 10 kW.
- **Subsidy cannot exceed cost.** "In any case, total subsidy (Central + State) will not exceed project cost." If your plant costs less than the combined subsidy, the state part is cut to match.

## What changed in September 2026?

The Second Amendment made four big changes for homes.

1. **Bigger upfront subsidy.** The earlier policy paid ₹10,000 per kW up to 3 kW, capped at ₹30,000. That is now ₹26,000 per kW, capped at ₹78,000.
2. **GBI ends for new home systems.** The Gazette says GBI "for domestic rooftop solar beneficiaries shall be discontinued for new installations". The GBI was "upfronted as additional state capital subsidy". Existing beneficiaries keep theirs.
3. **New ways to buy.** A *DISCOM Trusteeship Model* and the central *Utility-Led Aggregation (ULA)* model let a government-selected vendor install the system. The subsidy is paid directly to the vendor, so you pay less upfront.
4. **Free solar for subsidised consumers.** Homes using up to 400 units a month on average can get up to 3 kW installed at no capital cost, paid by GNCTD.

## How do the three buying models compare?

| | Existing PM Surya Ghar route | DISCOM Trusteeship | ULA model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vendor | You choose a registered vendor | Selected by bidding (IPGCL or other authorised agency) | Selected by bidding |
| Price | Agreed between you and vendor | Discovered through bidding | Discovered through bidding |
| What you pay | Full cost upfront, subsidies refunded later | Cost minus state subsidy | Cost minus central and state subsidy, capped at ₹5,000 per kW |
| Who gets the subsidy | You, after commissioning | State subsidy to vendor | Both subsidies to vendor |

The Gazette also says that under ULA the Power Department owns the system for the first five years, the mandatory O&M period. Ownership then passes to you.

The Gazette gives its own worked examples for a 3 kW ULA system:

- **Project cost ₹1,75,000:** minus ₹78,000 central and ₹78,000 Delhi subsidy, you pay ₹19,000.
- **Project cost ₹1,50,000:** the Delhi subsidy falls to ₹72,000, and you pay nothing.

These are the Gazette's illustrations, not market prices.

The trusteeship and ULA routes depended on MNRE sanctioning Delhi's ULA proposal. Vendors were then to be empanelled within 45 days of the amendment. Until that happens, the Gazette says you can use any PM Surya Ghar registered vendor and still get ₹26,000 per kW.

## Who qualifies for free solar in Delhi?

Delhi residential consumers already get a zero bill up to 200 units a month, and up to ₹800 off between 201 and 400 units, according to the Gazette. The free-solar offer applies if:

- your average gross consumption in the previous financial year, including solar, was **within 400 units a month**;
- your roof is technically feasible; and
- you have **not** taken a solar subsidy before under PM Surya Ghar or the Delhi scheme.

Surplus units are paid at the DISCOM's APPC rate. The Gazette's example uses ₹6.5 per unit. Homes under 200 units a month also get an incentive of 50% of energy savings, paid annually.

There is a catch. The free model includes the central subsidy, and the Gazette says it "will be reviewed, if PM Surya Ghar scheme is not extended beyond March 2027." See [PM Surya Ghar last date](/in/guides/pm-surya-ghar-last-date).

## How much will a Delhi rooftop system generate?

PVGIS, the European Commission's solar model, gives **1,623 kWh per kW per year** for New Delhi. That assumes optimal tilt and 14% losses. Simple arithmetic:

| System | kWh per year | Average units per month |
|---|---|---|
| 2 kW | about 3,250 | about 270 |
| 3 kW | about 4,870 | about 405 |

Shade, dust and roof direction will reduce this. The Gazette itself assumes 300 units a month for 3 kW.

## How do I apply in Delhi?

1. Apply on [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/) with your BRPL, BYPL, TPDDL or NDMC consumer number.
2. Pick a registered vendor, install, and complete net metering and DISCOM inspection.
3. The central subsidy comes through the portal. The Delhi subsidy is then paid to you by DBT, using portal data.
4. If you install outside PM Surya Ghar, the Gazette says you can still claim the state subsidy by registering on the Delhi Solar Portal.

For central scheme rules, see [PM Surya Ghar subsidy 2026](/in/guides/pm-surya-ghar-subsidy-2026). Delhi's Chief Minister said on 4 November 2026 that the state aims to put rooftop solar on two lakh homes by March 2027 (News On AIR).
