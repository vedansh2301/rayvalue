If you install a grid-connected rooftop solar system on your home in 2026, the central government pays **₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the 3rd kW**, so the most any home gets is **₹78,000**. You apply on the official National Portal, pick a registered vendor, and the money comes to your bank account after your DISCOM inspects the installation. The scheme runs until 31 March 2027.

RayValue is an independent site, not a government portal. Use this guide to understand the rules, then apply only at [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/).

## Key numbers

- **₹30,000 per kW** for the first 2 kW, **₹18,000** for the 3rd kW, **₹78,000** maximum (MNRE guidelines, effective 13 Feb 2024; still current as of 22 Nov 2026).
- **₹33,000 and ₹19,800 per kW** in special category states and UTs (MNRE).
- **₹18,000 per kW** for housing societies and RWAs, for common facilities up to 500 kW, at 3 kW per house (MNRE).
- **₹50,000 per kW** benchmark cost for the first 2 kW and ₹45,000 for additional kW. This is MNRE's reference, not a market price (MNRE, Feb 2024).
- **15 days**: target time to process the subsidy after DISCOM approval (MNRE guidelines).
- **65 lakh** homes had installed rooftop solar under the scheme by 4 Nov 2026 (News On AIR, quoting the Union Minister).

## How much PM Surya Ghar subsidy will I get?

The subsidy is called Central Financial Assistance (CFA). It is calculated on the DC capacity of the solar panels, not the inverter. The table uses MNRE's own rates and worked examples.

| System size | General states | Special category states |
|---|---|---|
| 1 kW | ₹30,000 | ₹33,000 |
| 1.5 kW | ₹45,000 | ₹49,500 |
| 2 kW | ₹60,000 | ₹66,000 |
| 2.5 kW | ₹69,000 | ₹75,900 |
| 3 kW | ₹78,000 | ₹85,800 |
| 4 kW to 10 kW | ₹78,000 | ₹85,800 |

Special category means Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, J&K, Ladakh, the North-Eastern states including Sikkim, and the UTs of Andaman & Nicobar and Lakshadweep.

The 1.5 kW and 2.5 kW rows come straight from MNRE's worked examples: 1.5 kW × ₹30,000 = ₹45,000, and 2 kW × ₹30,000 + 0.5 kW × ₹18,000 = ₹69,000.

Some states add their own top-up. Delhi, for example, now adds up to ₹78,000 more. See [Delhi rooftop solar subsidy 2026](/in/guides/delhi-solar-subsidy-2026).

## What size does the government suggest?

MNRE's leaflet links system size to your average monthly consumption:

| Average monthly units | Suggested system | Subsidy |
|---|---|---|
| 0–150 | 1–2 kW | ₹30,000 to ₹60,000 |
| 150–300 | 2–3 kW | ₹60,000 to ₹78,000 |
| Above 300 | Above 3 kW | ₹78,000 |

You can install a bigger system. You just do not get extra subsidy beyond 3 kW.

## Who is eligible?

According to the PIB factsheet and MNRE guidelines, you must:

1. Be an Indian citizen.
2. Own a house with a roof suitable for solar. MNRE also counts terraces, balconies and elevated structures.
3. Have a valid electricity connection with your DISCOM. The system is tagged to that connection.
4. Not have taken any other subsidy for solar panels. If you got a subsidy under the older Phase II programme, you can claim the new subsidy only for extra capacity up to 3 kW in total.

The system must also meet the scheme's technical conditions:

- **Made-in-India panels (DCR).** Modules must be made in India from Indian-made cells. MNRE says using any non-DCR module makes the installation ineligible.
- **Grid-connected.** Off-grid systems get no subsidy. Hybrid systems with batteries can qualify if your state regulator allows them. The subsidy is still based only on panel capacity. See [On-grid vs hybrid vs off-grid](/in/guides/on-grid-vs-hybrid-vs-off-grid).
- **Registered vendor.** You must use a vendor registered on the National Portal.
- **Capex model.** You pay for the system yourself, with your own money or a loan. Third-party (RESCO) models are not covered by these guidelines.

## How do I apply for PM Surya Ghar?

The PIB factsheet sets out nine steps. In plain terms:

1. **Register** on pmsuryaghar.gov.in. Select your state and DISCOM and enter your consumer number and mobile number.
2. **Apply** for rooftop solar through the online form.
3. **Wait for DISCOM feasibility approval.** Under the Electricity (Rights of Consumers) Rules, systems up to 10 kW are meant to be deemed approved. MNRE notes that until your state puts this into effect, you may still need to upload feasibility documents.
4. **Choose a registered vendor** and agree the price and design. MNRE leaves the price to you and the vendor. There is no government rate list, so compare quotes.
5. **Get the system installed.** Then upload the plant details and geo-tagged photos on the portal and apply for a net meter.
6. **DISCOM inspection.** The DISCOM installs the net meter, inspects the system and approves it on the portal. You then get the commissioning certificate.
7. **Submit bank details**, with a cancelled cheque or passbook scan, and redeem your e-token.
8. **Receive the subsidy.** MNRE's target is 15 days from DISCOM approval. The PIB factsheet says "within 30 days" of submitting bank details.

## How is the subsidy paid?

The subsidy is not a discount from the vendor. You pay the vendor, and the government refunds the CFA to you afterwards. If you took a loan through the portal, MNRE sends the CFA into your **loan account** first. Any amount above the loan balance goes to your savings account.

Delhi's 2026 policy amendment says this usually takes **one to two months** after installation. Plan your cash flow for that gap, or look at a loan. See [PM Surya Ghar loans](/in/guides/pm-surya-ghar-loan).

## What does the vendor have to provide?

Under the guidelines, registered vendors must give you **five years of free repairs and maintenance** from commissioning. The whole system must be under warranty for five years. Panels must carry a performance warranty of at least 25 years. Get these in writing in your vendor agreement. MNRE publishes a sample agreement in the guidelines.

## Is the subsidy enough to make solar worth it?

The subsidy covers a large share of a small system's cost. As a reference point, MNRE's benchmark cost for 3 kW is ₹1,45,000 (₹50,000 + ₹50,000 + ₹45,000), and the ₹78,000 subsidy is just over half of that. Real quotes vary. Whether it pays off depends far more on your tariff slab than on the subsidy. Homes in high slabs gain a lot. Homes on subsidised low slabs may not. ## Is there a deadline?

Yes. MNRE's guidelines say the scheme is to be implemented **till FY 2026-27**, which ends on 31 March 2027. See [PM Surya Ghar last date](/in/guides/pm-surya-ghar-last-date) for what that means for an application started now.
