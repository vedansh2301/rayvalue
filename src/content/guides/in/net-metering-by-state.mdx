India has no single net metering rule. Each state electricity regulator (SERC) decides how your exported solar units are credited. PM Surya Ghar accepts net metering, gross metering, net billing and virtual or group net metering wherever the regulator allows them. In **Karnataka**, surplus is bought each month at a fixed rate. That rate is lower if your system took the PM Surya Ghar subsidy. In **Tamil Nadu**, net-metered surplus units carry forward but **lapse on 31 March**. In **Kolkata (CESC)**, net billing pays **₹4.80 per kWh**, locked for the system's life.

This page only covers states where we read the regulator's or utility's own document. We will add more as we verify them.

## Key numbers

- **₹1.96 / ₹2.14 / ₹2.58 per kWh**: Karnataka export rate for PM Surya Ghar-subsidised domestic systems of 1–2 kW, 2–3 kW and above 3 kW (KERC order, 25 Aug 2026).
- **₹3.89 per kWh**: Karnataka export rate without subsidy, 1–10 kW (KERC).
- **₹5.80 per kWh** plus **₹150 per kW per month** fixed charge: BESCOM domestic LT-1 tariff for FY 2026-27 (KERC).
- **31 March**: Tamil Nadu net-metering credits lapse (TNERC Order 8 of 2021).
- **₹3.61 per kWh**: Tamil Nadu feed-in tariff for 1–10 kW, plus a network charge of ₹1.27 per kWh, of which domestic systems up to 10 kW pay 20% (TNERC).
- **₹4.80 per kWh**: CESC feed-in tariff for LV and MV net billing and gross metering (CESC, retrieved 22 Nov 2026).

## Net metering vs net billing vs gross metering

| Method | How exports are counted | What you get for surplus |
|---|---|---|
| Net metering | Exported units are subtracted from imported units | Units first offset your use. Leftovers are paid or carried as the state decides. |
| Net billing (net feed-in) | Imports are valued at your retail tariff, exports at a feed-in tariff | Export value in rupees is deducted from your bill |
| Gross metering | All generation is sold to the DISCOM | Feed-in tariff on every unit, and you buy everything you use |

Net metering is usually best for homes, because each exported unit cancels a unit you would have paid full retail price for. That only holds while your use is at least as large as your generation over the settlement period.

## How does net metering work in Karnataka?

The **KERC Distributed Solar PV Regulations 2026** were published in the Karnataka Gazette on 24 August 2026. KERC's generic tariff order followed on 25 August 2026. It covers 25 Aug 2026 to 30 Jun 2029.

- **All consumer categories** can choose net metering, net billing, gross metering or behind-the-meter. The minimum is 1 kW and the maximum is your sanctioned load, or 1,000 kW for net metering.
- **Monthly settlement.** Under net metering, excess energy is "purchased by the Distribution Licensee at the PPA tariff, and amount settled on monthly basis."
- **Fixed charges stay.** You keep paying fixed and demand charges and government levies "regardless of availability of excess electricity".
- **One switch only.** You can move from net metering to another mechanism only once in the life of the system.
- **Review trigger.** Net metering applies until the state's total net-metered, VNM and GNM solar reaches **3 GW**.

### Karnataka export rates (KERC, 25 Aug 2026)

| Domestic system | With PM Surya Ghar subsidy | Without subsidy |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 kW | ₹1.96 per unit | ₹3.89 per unit |
| above 2 kW to 3 kW | ₹2.14 per unit | ₹3.89 per unit |
| above 3 kW | ₹2.58 per unit | ₹3.89 per unit |

KERC sets the subsidised rates lower because the capital subsidy already covers part of the cost.

Compare these rates with what a unit you use yourself saves. BESCOM's FY 2026-27 domestic rate is **₹5.80 per unit**. In Bengaluru, every unit you use yourself is worth about two to three times a unit you export. Size the system to your monthly use, not to the largest roof area you can fill.

## How does net metering work in Tamil Nadu?

Tamil Nadu's rules come from **TNERC Order No. 8 of 2021** (22 October 2021), as summarised by TANGEDCO.

- **Net metering (domestic).** Surplus units carry forward to the next billing cycle "until the end of the settlement period i.e., 31st March. At the end of the settlement period, the credit ... shall get lapsed."
- **Net feed-in (any domestic consumer can opt).** Imports are charged at the retail tariff and exports are credited at the feed-in tariff. On 31 March you can **take the net credit as a payment** or carry it forward.
- **Feed-in tariff:** ₹3.61 per kWh for 1–10 kW, ₹3.37 for 11–150 kW and ₹3.10 for 151–999 kW. The rate is 20% higher in the evening peak, 6 pm to 9 pm.
- **Network charge:** ₹1.27 per kWh for LT prosumers on net metering or net billing, charged on total units generated. Domestic systems up to 10 kW pay **20%** of it, and those above 10 kW pay 75%. Gross metering has no network charge.

| TNPDCL domestic slab (bi-monthly) | Rate from 1 Jul 2025 |
|---|---|
| 0–400 units | ₹4.95 |
| 401–500 units | ₹6.65 |
| above 1,000 units | ₹12.15 |

What this means: in Tamil Nadu, an oversized system on net metering gives away its surplus every 31 March. Either size the system to your annual use or choose net feed-in so surplus is paid in money.

## How does solar export work in Kolkata (CESC)?

CESC's tariff page lists feed-in tariffs for **net billing and gross metering**:

| Voltage | Feed-in tariff |
|---|---|
| LV (up to 250 V) | ₹4.80 per kWh |
| MV (250–650 V) | ₹4.80 per kWh |
| HV (650 V to 33 kV) | ₹4.60 per kWh |
| EHV (above 33 kV) | ₹4.42 per kWh |

CESC adds that the feed-in tariff, "once agreed in the Agreement between the prosumer and the licensee shall be valid for the entire useful life" of the system. Few states lock the rate like this. CESC's 2025-26 domestic rates run from ₹5.18 per unit for the first 25 units up to ₹9.21 above 300 units, so self-use is still worth more than export at the higher slabs. This applies to CESC's Kolkata area. We have not yet verified WBSEDCL's terms for the rest of West Bengal.

## What should I take from this?

- **Self-use beats export everywhere we checked.** Size the system close to your consumption.
- **Ask which mechanism you are signing up for.** It is written in your DISCOM agreement, and in Karnataka you can switch only once.
- **Check the date of the rate.** Karnataka's rates are new (August 2026). Tamil Nadu's date from 2021 and may be revised.
