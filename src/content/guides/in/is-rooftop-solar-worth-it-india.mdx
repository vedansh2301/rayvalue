For many Indian homes, yes. Rooftop solar pays best where three things meet: **strong sun** (most cities get 1,450–1,650 kWh per kW per year), the **₹78,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy**, and a **high tariff slab**. A household on MSEDCL's top slab pays over ₹19 per unit. For a home like that, a 3 kW system can save tens of thousands of rupees a year. Solar is weakest for homes with small bills on heavily subsidised low slabs.

RayValue is an independent site, not a government portal.

## Key numbers

- **1,329 to 1,677 kWh per kW per year**: PVGIS solar yield across 45 Indian cities, from Guwahati to Jodhpur (EU JRC, retrieved 22 Nov 2026).
- **₹78,000**: maximum central subsidy, for 3 kW and above (MNRE, current as of 22 Nov 2026).
- **₹50,000 per kW**: MNRE benchmark cost for the first 2 kW, ₹45,000 per kW after that. This is a reference, not a market price.
- **₹19.13 per unit**: MSEDCL residential energy plus wheeling charge above 500 units, FY 2026-27 (MERC).
- **₹2.90 per unit**: Odisha domestic rate up to 50 units, FY 2026-27 (OERC).
- **65 lakh homes** had installed solar under PM Surya Ghar by 4 Nov 2026 (News On AIR).

These PVGIS figures assume a south-facing system at the best tilt with 14% losses. Monthly output swings with the season, and the monsoon months are lowest. For more cities, see [How many units does a 3 kW system generate?](/in/guides/3kw-solar-units-per-month).

## What decides whether solar pays?

Each unit of solar you use saves whatever your **last, most expensive** unit costs. Indian tariffs rise steeply by slab, so the same solar unit is worth very different amounts in different homes:

| DISCOM, FY 2026-27 | Low slab | Top slab |
|---|---|---|
| MSEDCL (Maharashtra), energy + wheeling | ₹5.56 (0–100) | ₹19.13 (above 500) |
| BESCOM (Bengaluru) | ₹5.80 flat | ₹5.80 flat |
| UPPCL urban (payable after subsidy) | ₹5.50 (0–150) | ₹6.50 (above 300) |
| Odisha DISCOMs | ₹2.90 (0–50) | ₹6.10 (above 400) |

Two other rules matter:

- **Fixed charges stay.** Solar cuts energy charges, not fixed charges. Karnataka's 2026 regulations and MPERC's FY 2026-27 order both say this explicitly.
- **Exports are often worth much less than self-use.** Karnataka pays ₹1.96–₹2.58 per exported unit for subsidised systems. See [Net metering by state](/in/guides/net-metering-by-state).

## Worked example: a heavy user in Pune

Take a Pune home on MSEDCL using **600 units a month**, with a 3 kW system making about 380 units a month (PVGIS). This assumes net metering offsets those units within the month.

| | Units billed | Energy + wheeling charge per month |
|---|---|---|
| Before solar | 600 | 100 × ₹5.56 + 200 × ₹12.40 + 200 × ₹16.64 + 100 × ₹19.13 = **₹8,277** |
| After solar | about 220 | 100 × ₹5.56 + 120 × ₹12.40 = **₹2,044** |

The saving is about **₹6,200 a month**, or roughly ₹75,000 a year, before the ₹130 fixed charge, which stays. Two reference costs for a 3 kW system net of the ₹78,000 subsidy:

- MNRE's benchmark of ₹1,45,000 leaves **₹67,000**.
- Delhi's Gazette illustration of ₹1,75,000 leaves **₹97,000**.

On those reference figures, savings cover the net cost in roughly one to one and a half years. Your actual quote will decide the real answer. See [MSEDCL tariff 2026-27 and solar savings](/in/guides/msedcl-tariff-2026-27-solar).

## Worked example: a light user in Odisha

A home using **50 units a month** pays ₹2.90 per unit, or **₹145 a month** in energy charges. Even if a 1 kW system wiped out every unit, the bill saving would be about ₹1,740 a year. At MNRE's benchmark, 1 kW costs ₹50,000, minus ₹30,000 subsidy, leaving ₹20,000. That is more than ten years of savings, unless the export rate adds a lot. We have not verified Odisha's export rate. See [When rooftop solar doesn't pay](/in/guides/when-rooftop-solar-doesnt-pay).

## Who does rooftop solar pay best for?

- **Homes in high slabs.** Maharashtra (MSEDCL above 300 units), Telangana (₹9.00 to ₹10.00 per unit above 300 units, TGERC FY 2026-27), and big users in Tamil Nadu (₹12.15 above 1,000 units bi-monthly) and Kolkata (₹9.21 above 300 units).
- **Homes with daytime use**, such as air conditioning, pumps, or people working from home, so more solar is used directly.
- **Homes in Delhi**, where the state adds up to ₹78,000 on top of the central subsidy. See [Delhi rooftop solar subsidy 2026](/in/guides/delhi-solar-subsidy-2026).
- **Anyone who can finish before 31 March 2027**, when the scheme period ends. See [PM Surya Ghar last date](/in/guides/pm-surya-ghar-last-date).

## Who should think twice?

- **Small users on subsidised slabs**, especially where the state already gives free units.
- **Shaded roofs**, or rented homes where you may move soon.
- **Anyone tempted to oversize.** Surplus earns less, and in Tamil Nadu net-metered surplus lapses on 31 March.

## How do I make it pay more?

1. Size the system to your consumption. Use the MNRE guide: 1–2 kW below 150 units, 2–3 kW for 150–300, more above 300.
2. Get several quotes from vendors registered on the National Portal, and insist on DCR panels so you keep the subsidy.
3. Use a low-rate loan if needed, rather than vendor finance. See [PM Surya Ghar loans](/in/guides/pm-surya-ghar-loan).
4. Read [Rooftop solar scams and fake subsidy sites](/in/guides/rooftop-solar-scams-fake-subsidy-sites) before paying any advance.
