Rooftop solar saves you the price of each unit you no longer buy. If that price is low, the saving is low. With the ₹78,000 subsidy, solar is still a **weak investment** for many Indian homes: **small users on subsidised slabs** (₹2–₹5.50 per unit), **homes that already get free units**, and **oversized systems** whose surplus is bought cheaply or lapses. Fixed charges stay on the bill in every case.

RayValue is an independent site. This page is meant to stop people buying solar that will not pay them back. For the cases where it does pay, see [Is rooftop solar worth it in India in 2026?](/in/guides/is-rooftop-solar-worth-it-india).

## Key numbers

- **₹2.90 per unit**: Odisha domestic rate up to 50 units a month, FY 2026-27 (OERC).
- **₹1.95 per unit**: Telangana domestic rate for the first 50 units, homes using up to 100 units, FY 2026-27 (TGERC).
- **₹2.95 per unit**: Haryana (UHBVN) domestic rate for 0–150 units, FY 2026-27 (UHBVN schedule).
- **₹3.35 (rural) and ₹5.50 (urban) per unit**: UP domestic rate payable after state subsidy, first 100 units, FY 2026-27 (UPERC).
- **Zero bill up to 200 units**: Delhi domestic consumers (Delhi Gazette, 17 Sep 2026).
- **₹1.96 per unit**: Karnataka export rate for a subsidised 1–2 kW system (KERC, 25 Aug 2026).

## Why does a low tariff slab make solar weak?

Tariffs in most states rise by slab. The government also subsidises the first units heavily. So a small household's units are its cheapest:

| State / DISCOM, FY 2026-27 | Low-use slab | Rate per unit |
|---|---|---|
| Telangana (LT-I A) | 0–50 units | ₹1.95 |
| Odisha | up to 50 units | ₹2.90 |
| Haryana (UHBVN) | 0–150 units | ₹2.95 |
| UP rural (payable) | up to 100 units | ₹3.35 |
| Madhya Pradesh | up to 50 units | ₹4.71 |
| UP urban (payable) | up to 150 units | ₹5.50 |

UP's press note shows the effect of the state subsidy. The approved urban rate is ₹7.35 per unit, but the household pays ₹5.50 after a ₹1.85 government subsidy. Solar saves you the ₹5.50, not the ₹7.35.

## Worked example: a 150-unit home in Lucknow

A UPPCL urban household using **150 units a month** pays ₹5.50 a unit, or **₹825 a month** in energy charges.

- A **1.5 kW** system in Lucknow makes about 1,581 × 1.5 ÷ 12 ≈ **200 units a month** on PVGIS's figures. That is more than the home uses.
- The best case for the bill is about **₹825 a month**, or ₹9,900 a year. The fixed charge of ₹110 per kW per month stays.
- The extra ~50 units a month are exported. We have not yet verified UP's export rate, so we give them no value here.

At MNRE's benchmark, 1.5 kW costs ₹75,000. The subsidy is ₹45,000, leaving ₹30,000, or roughly three years of the best-case saving. That is a reasonable result on paper. But the benchmark is not a market price, and small systems often cost more per kW. If your real net cost is double the benchmark figure, the payback doubles too. And if you use 100 units rather than 150, the saving drops by a third.

The same arithmetic for a 50-unit home in Odisha gives **₹145 a month** at most. That is under ₹1,800 a year, against a net cost of ₹20,000 for 1 kW at the benchmark. That takes more than ten years, before any export income.

## When you already get free units

**Delhi** gives a **zero bill up to 200 units a month** and up to ₹800 off between 201 and 400 units, according to the Delhi Gazette. If you're under 200 units, solar cannot cut a bill that is already zero. Delhi's 2026 policy deals with this by offering eligible subsidised homes **free installation** plus payment for surplus. That makes sense only because the government pays for the system. See [Delhi rooftop solar subsidy 2026](/in/guides/delhi-solar-subsidy-2026).

If your state gives a fixed number of free units, check whether they are tied to staying under a limit. Then work out what solar would actually remove from your bill.

## When the system is too big

Each unit you export is usually worth much less than one you use:

- **Karnataka** pays **₹1.96, ₹2.14 or ₹2.58 per unit** for PM Surya Ghar-subsidised home systems, by size, and ₹3.89 without subsidy. Bengaluru's BESCOM domestic rate is ₹5.80.
- **Tamil Nadu**: under net metering, surplus units carry forward until **31 March, then lapse**. You get nothing for them unless you chose net feed-in at ₹3.61 per unit.

A vendor quoting a 5 kW system for a home using 200 units a month is selling you surplus at a low price. The central subsidy also stops at 3 kW, so the extra kW carry no subsidy. See [Net metering by state](/in/guides/net-metering-by-state).

## The fixed charge never goes away

Solar lowers energy charges, not fixed charges:

- **Karnataka (KERC 2026 regulations):** "Regardless of availability of excess electricity ... the consumer shall continue to pay all other charges such as fixed/demand charges and government levy".
- **Madhya Pradesh (MPERC FY 2026-27 order):** rooftop solar consumers "remain connected to the grid and rely on it for backup supply, seasonal variations, and net metering adjustments. Therefore, recovery of such costs through fixed charges is justified."

In Bengaluru that fixed charge is ₹150 per kW per month. In urban UP it is ₹110 per kW per month. A "zero bill" promise should always be read as "zero energy charge, at best".

## Other cases where solar may not pay

- **Shaded roofs.** PVGIS figures assume an open, well-tilted roof. Trees, water tanks and neighbouring buildings cut output.
- **Rented homes, or homes you plan to sell soon.** The system is tagged to your electricity connection.
- **Non-DCR panels.** Imported panels may look cheaper, but MNRE says they make the system ineligible for the subsidy.
- **Missing the deadline.** If your system is not commissioned and approved before the scheme ends on 31 March 2027, you may lose the ₹78,000. See [PM Surya Ghar last date](/in/guides/pm-surya-ghar-last-date).

## How to check your own case in five minutes

1. Take a year of bills and find your **average monthly units** and the **rate on your top slab**.
2. Multiply the units solar would replace by that rate. That is your best-case monthly saving.
3. Subtract nothing for the fixed charge, because it stays.
4. Compare the result with real quotes, net of the subsidy (see [PM Surya Ghar subsidy 2026](/in/guides/pm-surya-ghar-subsidy-2026)).
5. If the answer is many years, a smaller system or no system may be the better choice.

Tariffs checked on 22 November 2026 against FY 2026-27 regulator orders.
