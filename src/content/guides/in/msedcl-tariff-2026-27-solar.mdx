From 1 April 2026, MSEDCL's residential (LT-I B) energy charges run from **₹3.96 to ₹17.53 per unit**. A **₹1.60 wheeling charge** is added to every unit, and the fixed charge is **₹130 a month**, or ₹435 for three-phase. The rates come from MERC's order in Case No. 75 of 2025. Because the slabs climb so steeply, every rooftop solar unit used by a home above 300 units a month saves **₹16.64 to ₹19.13**. That makes Maharashtra one of the strongest states for home solar.

RayValue is an independent site, not affiliated with MSEDCL or MERC.

## Key numbers

- **₹3.96 / ₹10.80 / ₹15.03 / ₹17.53 per unit**: energy charge for 1–100, 101–300, 301–500 and above 500 units a month, FY 2026-27 (MERC).
- **₹1.60 per unit**: wheeling charge on all residential slabs (MERC).
- **₹130 per month** single-phase, **₹435** three-phase: fixed charge per connection (MERC).
- **₹1.48 per unit** and ₹35 per month fixed charge: BPL residential (LT-I A) (MERC).
- **1,504 to 1,537 kWh per kW per year**: PVGIS yield for Nagpur, Pune and Nashik (EU JRC, retrieved 22 Nov 2026).
- **₹2.82 per unit**: MERC generic rate for surplus rooftop solar, as reported in April 2025 (Saur Energy, secondary).

## What is the MSEDCL tariff for 2026-27?

MERC's Case 75 of 2025 order (25 June 2025) revised MSEDCL's tariffs for FY 2026-27, effective 1 April 2026:

| LT-I(B) residential, units per month | Energy charge | Wheeling | Total variable charge |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–100 | ₹3.96 | ₹1.60 | ₹5.56 |
| 101–300 | ₹10.80 | ₹1.60 | ₹12.40 |
| 301–500 | ₹15.03 | ₹1.60 | ₹16.64 |
| above 500 | ₹17.53 | ₹1.60 | ₹19.13 |
| Fixed charge | ₹130 per month single-phase, ₹435 three-phase | | |

Slabs are telescopic. A 400-unit month is billed as 100 units at ₹5.56, 200 at ₹12.40 and 100 at ₹16.64. Electricity duty and any fuel adjustment charge come on top. We have not included them here.

## What does a unit of rooftop solar save on an MSEDCL bill?

Solar replaces your **most expensive** units first, because it cuts your total, and the top slab is the last part billed. That makes it worth:

| Your monthly use | Value of each solar unit used |
|---|---|
| up to 100 units | ₹5.56 |
| 101–300 units | ₹12.40 |
| 301–500 units | ₹16.64 |
| above 500 units | ₹19.13 |

Once solar pushes your billed units down into a lower slab, the later solar units save the lower rate.

## Worked examples: a 3 kW system in Pune

PVGIS gives **1,514 kWh per kW per year** for Pune, so 3 kW makes about **380 units a month** on average. These examples assume net metering offsets those units within the month. They show energy and wheeling charges only.

| Monthly use | Bill before solar | Billed units after | Bill after solar | Saving per month |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 300 units | ₹3,036 | 0 | ₹0 | ₹3,036 (80 surplus units) |
| 400 units | ₹4,700 | 20 | ₹111 | about ₹4,590 |
| 600 units | ₹8,277 | 220 | ₹2,044 | about ₹6,230 |

The fixed charge of ₹130 stays in every case. In the 300-unit case, about 80 units a month are surplus. They earn only the MERC surplus rate, reported as ₹2.82 a unit for FY 2025-26, so a smaller system would serve that home almost as well.

Arithmetic for 400 units: 100 × ₹5.56 + 200 × ₹12.40 + 100 × ₹16.64 = ₹4,700 before solar. After solar, 20 × ₹5.56 = ₹111.

## How does this compare with what solar costs?

The PM Surya Ghar subsidy for 3 kW is **₹78,000**. MNRE's benchmark cost for 3 kW is **₹1,45,000**, leaving **₹67,000**. This is a reference figure, not a market price. Against savings of ₹3,000 to ₹6,200 a month, even a quote well above the benchmark pays back quickly for heavy users. Get real quotes from vendors registered on the National Portal, and see [PM Surya Ghar subsidy 2026](/in/guides/pm-surya-ghar-subsidy-2026).

## What about Mumbai on Tata Power?

Parts of Mumbai are supplied by other licensees, so MSEDCL's rates do not apply there. Tata Power's Mumbai distribution (TPC-D) tariff for FY 2026-27, from MERC's MYT order in Case 210 of 2024, is much lower per slab. Energy charges are ₹1.90 (0–100 units), ₹4.70 (101–300), ₹9.24 (301–500) and ₹10.24 (above 500), plus a ₹2.40 wheeling charge. Solar still saves ₹11.64 to ₹12.64 a unit for TPC-D homes above 300 units, but less than on MSEDCL.

## What is changing for rooftop solar in Maharashtra?

MERC published **draft rooftop renewable energy regulations** in 2026, as reported by Energetica India on 23 September 2026. They cover net metering, net billing, gross metering, group and virtual net metering. They would allow systems up to your sanctioned load or contracted demand. They would require energy storage only for new systems above 100 kW, so typical homes are not affected. They are drafts. Check the final MERC regulations before relying on any change.

## Who should be careful?

- **Homes under 100 units a month** save only ₹5.56 a unit. See [When rooftop solar doesn't pay](/in/guides/when-rooftop-solar-doesnt-pay).
- **BPL consumers (₹1.48 a unit)** save very little on bills.
- **Anyone oversizing.** Surplus earns about a seventh of the top-slab rate.
