Money your supplier pays for the solar power you export is tax-free up to €400 a year per person, for the tax years 2024 to 2028. The exemption covers income tax, USC and PRSI, and you do not need to declare amounts under the limit. Each person named on the electricity bill gets their own €400. A rise to €600 is proposed in the Finance (No.2) Bill 2026 but is not law yet.

## Key numbers

- **€400 a year** of microgeneration profit exempt from income tax, USC and PRSI, tax years 2024 to 2028 (Revenue TDM 07-01-44, updated January 2026).
- **Per person named on the bill**: two named people means two €400 exemptions (Revenue).
- **€200** was the limit in 2022 and 2023 (Revenue).
- **€600**: proposed new limit in the Finance (No.2) Bill 2026, not yet enacted (KPMG summary, checked 22 Nov 2026).
- **19.5c per kWh**: Electric Ireland's current variable export rate, which it estimates gives customers €50 to €300 a year (Electric Ireland, checked 22 Nov 2026).

## Is solar export income taxable in Ireland?

In principle yes, but most homes pay nothing. Section 216D of the Taxes Consolidation Act 1997 exempts "certain profits arising to a qualifying individual from the microgeneration of electricity". Revenue's Tax and Duty Manual says profits of up to €400 arising in any of the years 2024 to 2028 "are exempt from Income Tax, USC and PRSI".

The exempt profit is the money you get for electricity "supplied to the national grid". Power you use yourself is not a payment at all; it simply lowers your bill.

## Who qualifies?

Revenue lists these conditions:

- The electricity comes from a renewable source, such as solar panels or a domestic wind turbine.
- It is generated by an individual, not a company. The exemption "is not available to companies or corporate entities".
- It is generated at your sole or main residence (including the surrounding grounds), for your own consumption.
- You are named on the electricity bill for the home.

You do not need to own the home. Revenue says it "could be a rented property", as long as it is your main residence during the year.

## Is the €400 per household or per person?

Per person. Revenue says: "Where more than one individual is named on the electricity bill each individual can avail of the exemption i.e. the exemption is not split between them." A couple both named on the bill can therefore receive up to €800 a year in export payments without tax, on the current limit.

## How much export does €400 cover?

At Electric Ireland's 19.5c per kWh, €400 covers about 2,051 kWh of exported electricity a year. Electric Ireland itself estimates customer export payments of €50 to €300 a year, depending on the system. So for most single-named households the exemption already covers all export income. ## Do I need to declare it?

Not if you are under the limit. Revenue says there is no requirement to include exempt profits in an income tax return (Form 11 or Form 12). If you do not otherwise file a return, export income under the limit does not mean you have to start.

If your profit goes over €400, the excess "must be declared and will be subject to income tax, USC and PRSI in the usual manner". Revenue's example: Eleanor received €500 in export credits in 2025 with no costs. The first €400 is exempt; the other €100 is taxable and goes on a return.

## Can I deduct the cost of the panels?

No. Revenue says no deduction is allowed for capital costs "(e.g. the cost of acquiring and installing the solar panels)", and capital allowances are not available either. You can deduct revenue expenses incurred wholly and exclusively in earning the export income, but for most households there are none.

## Is the exemption going up to €600?

It is proposed, not enacted. KPMG's summary of the Finance (No.2) Bill 2026 says the exemption "increases from €400 to €600", implementing the Budget announcement. As of 22 November 2026, the Finance Act has not been confirmed as passed, and the year the higher limit would apply from has not been confirmed in a primary source. Until Revenue updates its guidance, plan on €400.

## Does this change whether solar is worth it?

Only slightly. For most homes the exemption means export income is simply tax-free. The bigger lever is using more of your own power: SEAI estimates each kWh you use from your panels saves about €0.35, against roughly €0.18 to €0.20 for each kWh exported.
