SEAI now offers a flat €600 grant towards a home battery of 5 kWh or more. You can claim it with new solar panels in a single application, or add it later to a home that already has solar. If you install the battery at the same time as panels it is also VAT-free, which matters more than the grant itself on most quotes.

## Key numbers

- **€600** flat grant for batteries of **5 kWh or more** (SEAI, archived 27 Oct 2026).
- **€2,400**: maximum solar plus battery grant (€1,800 + €600) (SEAI).
- **€3,500 to €5,500**: SEAI's indication of a fully installed battery cost.
- **0% VAT** if installed with solar panels; **23% VAT** if standalone (SEAI FAQ).
- **About €0.35** saved per kWh used from your own panels vs **€0.18–€0.20** earned per kWh exported (SEAI FAQ).
- **38.71 c/kWh**: average Irish household electricity price, 2026 first half, all taxes, 2,500–5,000 kWh band (Eurostat).

## Who qualifies for the SEAI battery grant?

SEAI lists these criteria:

- **Applicants:** homeowners (including private landlords), owner management companies and Approved Housing Bodies.
- **The home:** needs an MPRN and must have been built and occupied before 2025.
- **The battery:** 5 kWh or larger. Smaller batteries are not eligible.
- **Timing:** you must receive a Grant Offer from SEAI before installing. Applications cannot be accepted for installations that have already started.
- **One battery grant per home:** if the property has had a battery grant before, it cannot apply again.

## How do I apply?

| Your situation | How to apply |
|---|---|
| New solar and battery together | One application covering both grants |
| You already have a solar PV application in progress | Submit a separate battery application in the portal; no need to amend the solar one |
| You already have solar panels | Apply for the battery grant on its own, provided you meet the criteria when you apply |

Use an SEAI registered company, wait for the Grant Offer, then install. As with the solar grant, SEAI's process includes paperwork from your installer after the work. See [the SEAI solar grant guide](/ie/guides/seai-solar-grant-2026) for the full steps.

## Can I add a battery to my existing panels?

SEAI says yes, but your installer needs to check whether your current inverter is compatible. Its FAQ notes that standard installations use a string inverter, which is not compatible with battery storage, so you may need extra equipment. Ask for that cost in the quote.

## Why does VAT matter more than the grant?

SEAI's FAQ says a battery installed at the same time as solar panels carries 0% VAT, while a standalone installation carries 23% VAT. On SEAI's indicative installed cost of €3,500 to €5,500, simple arithmetic puts 23% VAT at about €805 to €1,265. That is more than the €600 grant. If you are planning solar and a battery, installing them together is the cheaper route.

## How does a battery save money?

Without a battery, solar power you do not use straight away is exported. SEAI's FAQ puts the export payment at about €0.18 to €0.20 per kWh, and the saving on each kWh you use from your own panels at about €0.35. A battery lets you store surplus daytime power and use it in the evening, so each stored kWh is worth roughly the difference: about 15 to 17 cent, before battery losses.

Current export rates from the large suppliers are 18.5c (Bord Gáis Energy) to 19.5c (Electric Ireland), so that range still holds. See [export rates by supplier](/ie/blog/best-export-rate-ireland).

SEAI also notes a battery can be charged at cheaper night rates and used during the day, which can help even in winter when there is little solar.

## Is a battery worth it with the grant?

It depends on how much you shift each day, which depends on your solar size and your evening use. A simple way to check a quote:

1. Take the installed price, less €600, and less nothing for VAT if fitted with panels.
2. Estimate the kWh the battery will shift on a typical day. Your installer should give you this.
3. Multiply by about 15 to 17 cent and by 365 for a yearly figure, and compare it with the net price.

If the yearly saving is small compared with the price, the battery may still be worth it for other reasons, such as backup during outages (ask your installer whether the system supports this), but it will not pay for itself quickly.

## Other tips

- SEAI suggests getting at least three quotes.
- Check with your home insurer before installing, as SEAI advises.
- Keep the grant and export income in view together: Revenue exempts up to €400 a year of export income per person named on the bill (2024 to 2028), so storing more and exporting less does not change your tax position for most homes.
