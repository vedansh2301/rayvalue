A grant-funded home solar system in Ireland cost about €10,500 before the grant in 2025, or €1,689 per kWp, according to an industry analysis of SEAI scheme data. The average system was 6.23 kWp, and about half included a battery. A 4 kWp system at that rate is about €6,750, or about €4,950 after the €1,800 SEAI grant. VAT is 0%.

## Key numbers

- **€1,689 per kWp**: average cost of SEAI grant-funded solar in 2025, down from €2,762 in 2020 (EnergyEfficiency.ie report on SEAI data, published 2026).
- **€10,529**: average pre-grant system cost in 2025; about **€9,450** without a battery and about **€12,800** with one (same report).
- **€1,532 to €1,893 per kWp**: county averages, Tipperary lowest, Dublin highest (same report).
- **€1,800**: maximum SEAI solar grant; **€2,400** with a battery (SEAI, archived 27 Oct 2026).
- **0% VAT** on supply and installation on private homes since 1 May 2023 (Revenue).

## How much do solar panels cost in Ireland?

There is no official SEAI price list. The most detailed recent figures come from EnergyEfficiency.ie's Home Solar PV Report 2026, which analyses monthly SEAI Solar PV Scheme data on grants, installation costs and capacity for every county from January 2020 to December 2025. It covers grant-funded installs only.

| Year | Average cost per kWp | Average system size |
|---|---|---|
| 2020 | €2,762 | 3.7 kWp |
| 2025 | €1,689 | 6.23 kWp |

The report says the average pre-grant cost of all grant-funded installs in 2025 was €10,529. It estimates around €9,450 for systems without a battery and roughly €12,800 for those with one, adding that battery-equipped systems are typically €2,000 to €4,000 more expensive in total.

Because 40–50% of the installs included a battery, the €1,689 per kWp average is a little higher than the cost of panels alone.

A consumer site gives a similar range: Switcher says a domestic system "should cost around €1,500 - €2,000 per kW installed", so a 4 kW system could cost around €8,000 before grants.

## Does the price vary by county?

Yes, by up to about €360 per kWp. 2025 averages from the same report:

| County | Cost per kWp | Average system cost | Average size |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tipperary | €1,532 | €10,459 | 6.83 kWp |
| Kerry | €1,577 | €10,702 | 6.79 kWp |
| Sligo | €1,581 | €9,743 | 6.16 kWp |
| Limerick | €1,622 | €10,450 | 6.44 kWp |
| Galway | €1,641 | €11,100 | 6.77 kWp |
| Cork | €1,649 | €10,452 | 6.34 kWp |
| Kildare | €1,728 | €10,616 | 6.14 kWp |
| Wicklow | €1,730 | €10,956 | 6.33 kWp |
| Dublin | €1,893 | €10,080 | 5.32 kWp |

Dublin has the highest cost per kWp and the smallest systems. Smaller systems usually cost more per kWp, because fixed costs such as scaffolding and the inverter are spread over fewer panels.

## How much is the SEAI grant?

SEAI pays €700 per kWp for the first 2 kWp and €200 per kWp from 2 to 4 kWp, capped at €1,800:

| System size | SEAI solar grant |
|---|---|
| 2 kWp | €1,400 |
| 3 kWp | €1,600 |
| 4 kWp or more | €1,800 |
| Battery of 5 kWh or more | plus €600 |

The grant stops rising at 4 kWp, so on a 6.23 kWp system it covers a smaller share. Homes must have been built and occupied before 2025, and you need the grant offer before work starts. Details are in our [SEAI solar grant guide](/ie/guides/seai-solar-grant-2026).

## What will I pay after the grant?

Simple arithmetic using the 2025 average of €1,689 per kWp:

| System | Before grant | SEAI grant | After grant |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 kWp | about €5,067 | €1,600 | about €3,467 |
| 4 kWp | about €6,756 | €1,800 | about €4,956 |
| 6.23 kWp (2025 average, avg cost) | €10,529 | €1,800 | about €8,729 |

Your quote will differ with your roof, panels and county. Use these as a check, not a price.

## Is there VAT on solar panels in Ireland?

No, for homes. Revenue says that from 1 May 2023 "the zero rate applies to the supply and installation of solar panels" on private dwellings. It also applies to ancillary equipment supplied and installed with the panels under the same contract, including "the batteries". A battery installed on its own carries 23% VAT, according to SEAI; see [a battery without solar panels](/ie/guides/battery-without-solar-night-rate).

## How much does a battery add?

SEAI says a fully installed battery "can cost of the order of €3,500 to €5,500", before the €600 grant. The industry report's €2,000 to €4,000 difference between systems with and without batteries is lower, partly because battery systems also had more panels. See [the €600 battery grant](/ie/guides/seai-battery-grant-how-to-claim).

## Can I finance it?

The government-backed Home Energy Upgrade Loan Scheme is offered by PTSB, AIB, Bank of Ireland, Avant Money (with An Post Money) and seven credit unions, with rates "starting from as low as 2.99%", according to the Department of Climate, Energy and the Environment.

## Is the cost worth it?

That depends on how much of the output you use at home and how sunny your location is. See [are solar panels worth it in Ireland?](/ie/blog/are-solar-panels-worth-it-ireland) and, for the cases where it struggles, [when solar is not worth it in Ireland](/ie/blog/when-solar-isnt-worth-it-ireland).
