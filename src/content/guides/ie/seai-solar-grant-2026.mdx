The SEAI solar PV grant pays up to €1,800 towards rooftop solar on an Irish home, and a separate €600 battery grant can take the total to €2,400. Homes built and occupied before 2025 are eligible, which brings in many newer houses. The one rule that catches people out: you must have grant approval before any work starts.

## Key numbers

- **€700 per kWp** up to 2 kWp, then **€200 per kWp** up to 4 kWp; maximum **€1,800** (SEAI, archived 27 Oct 2026).
- **€600** flat battery grant for 5 kWh or larger; solar plus battery maximum **€2,400** (SEAI).
- **Built and occupied before 2025**: eligibility cut-off (SEAI).
- **8 months** to finish works after approval; **4 to 6 weeks** for payment (SEAI).
- **Enhanced grant**: €3,350 per kWp up to 2 kWp, then €850 per kWp, capped at €8,400 (or €9,000 with a battery), for homeowners on qualifying Department of Social Protection payments (SEAI).
- **€400 a year** of export income tax-free per person named on the bill, 2024 to 2028 (Revenue).

## How much is the SEAI solar grant in 2026?

The grant is paid per kWp (kilowatt peak) of panels, on a pro-rata basis:

| System size | Solar grant | With a 5 kWh+ battery |
|---|---|---|
| 2 kWp | €1,400 | €2,000 |
| 2.5 kWp | €1,500 | €2,100 |
| 3 kWp | €1,600 | €2,200 |
| 4 kWp or larger | €1,800 (cap) | €2,400 |

Figures from SEAI's grant page. The grant stops at 4 kWp, so a bigger system gets the same €1,800.

## Who is eligible?

According to SEAI:

- Homeowners, including private landlords, owner management companies and Approved Housing Bodies can apply.
- The home needs an MPRN (electricity meter point reference number).
- The house must have been **built and occupied before 2025**.
- The grant is not open to homes that already had solar PV funding at that MPRN.
- You must use an **SEAI registered company**.

## Is there a bigger grant for people on social welfare?

Yes. SEAI's enhanced solar PV grant is for homeowners who personally receive a qualifying Department of Social Protection payment. If the payment goes to another household member, the application does not qualify.

| System size | Enhanced grant |
|---|---|
| 2 kWp | €6,700 |
| 3 kWp | €7,550 |
| 4 kWp | €8,400 |
| Battery 5 kWh+ | €600 flat |

The enhanced grant is capped at €8,400 without a battery and €9,000 with one. Homes in Dublin with a long-term tenant on a Rental Accommodation Scheme payment through one of the four Dublin councils can also get up to €9,000.

## What are the steps from quote to payment?

1. **Choose an SEAI registered company and get quotes.** SEAI advises shopping around, checking references and getting a written contract with works, prices, timing, payment terms and warranties. Ask how much energy the system will produce in a year.
2. **Apply and wait for approval.** Approval must be in place before any works start.
3. **Install within 8 months.** Your solar company arranges an SEAI registered installer.
4. **Grid notification.** The company submits an NC6 form to ESB Networks. ESB Networks says there is no charge to connect a micro-generator under its "inform and fit" process, for systems up to 25 A single-phase (about 6 kVA) or 16 A three-phase (about 11 kVA).
5. **Post-works BER.** A registered BER assessor must carry out a post-works BER, which is needed before the grant is paid.
6. **Submit the documents.** These include the declaration of works, a Safe Electric certificate, the NC6 form, the invoice and photos.
7. **Payment.** SEAI says to allow 4 to 6 weeks once the documents are in and the BER is published.

## What else should I know?

- **Export payments.** Once ESB Networks has the NC6, your supplier can pay you for exported power under the Clean Export Guarantee. See [Clean Export Guarantee rates by supplier](/ie/blog/best-export-rate-ireland).
- **Smart meter.** ESB Networks aims to install a smart meter within four months of processing a valid NC6.
- **Tax.** Revenue says up to €400 a year of export income is exempt from income tax for each person named on the electricity bill, for 2024 to 2028.
- **Battery.** If you want a battery, apply for both grants in one application. See [the €600 battery grant](/ie/guides/seai-battery-grant-how-to-claim).

## Is it worth it?

The grant covers part of the cost, not all of it. Whether solar pays for your home depends on your roof, your electricity use and your export rate. Our guide [Is solar worth it in Ireland in 2026?](/ie/blog/are-solar-panels-worth-it-ireland) walks through the numbers.
