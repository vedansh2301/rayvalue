Yes, a battery can save money without solar panels in Ireland, but only on a day/night or time-of-use tariff, and the sums are tighter than with solar. You charge at the cheap night rate and use the stored power in the day. A standalone battery carries 23% VAT, against 0% when fitted with panels, though the €600 SEAI battery grant still helps.

## Key numbers

- **€3,500 to €5,500**: SEAI's estimate for a fully installed battery (SEAI FAQ, archived 27 Oct 2026).
- **23% VAT** on a standalone battery, **0%** if installed with solar panels on the same contract (SEAI; Revenue).
- **€600**: flat SEAI grant for batteries of 5 kWh or more (SEAI).
- **40.97c day / 23.79c night** per kWh incl. VAT: Yuno Energy Day/Night unit rates, variable (Yuno, checked 22 Nov 2026).
- **17.18c**: the day/night gap on those prices, before battery losses.

## Can I get a battery without solar panels?

SEAI's battery FAQ answers this directly: "Yes, you can. However, it is important that you are on an electricity tariff where there are times when it is cheaper to use electricity, e.g. a day/night rate." It explains that the battery "can be charged at cheaper overnight electricity rates", letting you use cheaper stored power when daytime prices are higher.

Without a cheaper period, a battery on its own has nothing to earn from.

## What does a standalone battery cost?

SEAI says a fully installed battery "can cost of the order of €3,500 to €5,500". If it is "a standalone installation, there will be 23% VAT on top of that cost". Revenue's VAT guidance explains why: the 0% rate covers batteries supplied and installed with solar panels as part of the same contract.

| | With solar panels | Battery on its own |
|---|---|---|
| SEAI cost range | €3,500 to €5,500 | €3,500 to €5,500 |
| VAT | 0% | 23% |
| Cost incl. VAT | €3,500 to €5,500 | about €4,305 to €6,765 |
| After €600 grant | €2,900 to €4,900 | about €3,705 to €6,165 |

The VAT difference, about €805 to €1,265, is bigger than the grant. If you might add panels within a year or two, fitting both together may cost less overall. See [the €600 SEAI battery grant](/ie/guides/seai-battery-grant-how-to-claim) for how VAT and the grant combine.

## Can I get the SEAI battery grant without solar?

SEAI's battery page offers "a flat rate grant of €600 to install home battery storage systems of 5kWh or larger". Its criteria do not list solar panels; the post-works BER is required "if you have Solar PV". The main conditions are:

- the home has an MPRN and was built and occupied before 2025;
- no previous battery grant at that MPRN;
- you receive a Grant Offer before installation starts;
- an SEAI-registered Solar PV Contractor installs it;
- your installer applies to ESB Networks first, which usually takes at least 20 working days;
- works finish within 8 months of approval.

## How much can a battery save on a night rate?

The saving per kWh is the gap between the day and night price. On Yuno Energy's Day/Night tariff (variable, checked 22 November 2026):

| Yuno Energy unit rate | Ex VAT | Inc VAT |
|---|---|---|
| Day | 37.59c | 40.97c |
| Night | 21.83c | 23.79c |
| 24hr (standard plan) | 35.87c | 39.10c |

Each kWh moved from day to night saves 17.18c including VAT. A 5 kWh battery charged and emptied once every day would move up to about 1,825 kWh a year, worth up to about €314. That is an upper bound: it assumes a full cycle every day and ignores the energy lost in charging and discharging, which we have not sourced.

There is a catch. On these prices the Day/Night tariff's day rate (40.97c) is higher than the 24hr rate (39.10c). Any daytime use the battery does not cover costs more than before. A battery only pays if it covers most of your daytime and evening use.

## Who does a battery without solar suit?

- **Homes with high evening use** that a 5 kWh or larger battery can cover.
- **Homes already on a day/night or smart tariff**, with a smart meter. Yuno's prices, for example, depend on meter type.
- **People planning solar later.** SEAI notes you can apply for the grant for the other measure at a later date, if you still meet the criteria then.

It suits less well where use is low, mostly in the daytime, or where you cannot easily change tariff.

## How long will a battery last?

SEAI says batteries "typically have a 10-year warranty, retaining at least 70% of the original storage capacity", and are often rated for 6,000 to 10,000 cycles at 70–80% capacity retention. A battery cycled daily for 10 years does about 3,650 cycles, within that rating.

## Is a battery without solar worth it?

Compare the installed cost after VAT and grant, €3,705 to €6,165 on SEAI's range, with an honest annual saving well under the €314 upper bound for a 5 kWh unit on Yuno's prices. With solar the case is usually stronger: a battery stores power worth about €0.35 a kWh that would otherwise be exported for €0.18 to €0.20, on SEAI's figures. See [is solar worth it in Ireland?](/ie/blog/are-solar-panels-worth-it-ireland) and [when solar is not worth it in Ireland](/ie/blog/when-solar-isnt-worth-it-ireland).

Prices are variable and were checked on 22 November 2026. Get your own supplier's day and night rates before deciding.
