From 1 July 2026, an Israeli household on the standard IEC flat rate pays 63.52 agorot per kWh including VAT, plus about 30 shekels a month in fixed charges and a small yearly capacity charge. A rooftop system is paid a separate, fixed export tariff of about 48 agorot per kWh, set by the Electricity Authority. That tariff is changing for new systems from 2027. Here is how each part works.

## Key numbers

- **53.83 ag/kWh before VAT, 63.52 ag/kWh including 18% VAT**: residential flat rate from 1 July 2026 ([IEC](https://iecmedia.iec.co.il/media/5malqupk/n%D7%AA%D7%A2%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%A4%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%91%D7%90%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%AA-1726-%D7%9E%D7%A2%D7%9E-18.pdf)).
- **ILS 11.76 + 18.41 a month** including VAT: distribution and supply fixed charges with a single-phase meter, for a home billed every two months (IEC, same sheet).
- **ILS 7.29 per kVA per year** including VAT: capacity charge (IEC).
- **172.23 ag/kWh**: summer peak on the low-voltage time-of-use tariff, including VAT (IEC).
- **About 48 ag/kWh** for 25 years: export tariff for home systems up to 15 kW connected by 31 Dec 2026 (mako/Bizportal; press report).
- **9.09 ag/kWh**: IEC "system management" charge on self-consumed rooftop power (Globes English, 2026).

## What makes up an Israeli electricity bill?

The IEC tariff sheet for low-voltage customers has three parts.

| Charge | Before VAT | Including 18% VAT |
|---|---|---|
| Energy, residential flat rate | 53.83 ag/kWh | 63.52 ag/kWh |
| Distribution fixed charge, single-phase meter | ILS 9.97/month | ILS 11.76/month |
| Supply fixed charge, single-phase meter | ILS 15.60/month | ILS 18.41/month |
| Distribution fixed charge, three-phase meter | ILS 11.56/month | ILS 13.64/month |
| Supply fixed charge, three-phase meter | ILS 15.53/month | ILS 18.33/month |
| Capacity charge | ILS 6.18 per kVA/year | ILS 7.29 per kVA/year |

Source: IEC low-voltage flat rates from 1 July 2026. The fixed charges are for a consumer billed every two months.

The energy charge is by far the largest part. A household using 700 kWh a month pays about ILS 445 for energy (700 x 0.6352) and about ILS 30 in fixed charges with a single-phase meter. That is why solar savings track the per-kWh price closely in Israel: fixed charges are small, so most of the bill can be offset.

## What is the time-of-use tariff?

The IEC also publishes a low-voltage time-of-use (TOU) tariff. Prices change with season and hour:

| Season | Peak hours (Sun to Thu) | Peak, incl. VAT | Off-peak, incl. VAT |
|---|---|---|---|
| Summer (June to September) | 17:00 to 23:00 | 172.23 ag | 50.42 ag |
| Winter (December to February) | 17:00 to 22:00 (every day) | 114.70 ag | 46.63 ag |
| Spring and autumn | not listed in the PDF | 49.58 ag | 45.78 ag |

Source: IEC, from 1 July 2026. In summer, Friday, Saturday and holidays are off-peak all day.

The TOU fixed charges are higher than on the flat rate. For a consumer billed every two months they are ILS 37.88 (distribution) and ILS 18.33 (supply) a month including VAT. For solar homes the key point is that summer peak starts at 17:00, as solar output fades. That is where a battery earns its keep (see [batteries in Israel](/il/guides/home-battery-israel)).

Since the electricity market opened, homes can also buy from private suppliers. According to TheMarker's Supermarker, citing the Electricity Authority's switching guide, a home with solar can switch only if it has a smart meter. The IEC keeps paying for the power the roof produces.

## How is rooftop solar paid?

There are two separate flows:

1. **Power you use yourself** never reaches the meter as a purchase, so you avoid the 63.52 ag grid price. Globes reports that the IEC is entitled to a "system management" charge of 9.09 ag per self-consumed kWh. After billing problems, the Electricity Authority limited the IEC's back-billing to six months instead of two years.
2. **Power you export** is paid at a fixed tariff set by the Electricity Authority. According to mako/Bizportal (2026), the current tiers for new joiners are:

| System size | Tariff (25 years, not CPI-linked) |
|---|---|
| Up to 15 kW | about 48 ag/kWh |
| 15 to 100 kW | about 41 ag/kWh |
| Larger tiers | about 34, then about 28 ag/kWh |
| Urban premium (towns over 50,000 residents) | +6 ag/kWh for the first 15 years |

Net metering is closed to new entrants. Existing members keep it for 25 years.

## What changes in 2027?

The Electricity Authority's draft of 19 October 2026 is reported by Calcalist and Globes. It proposes:

- about 5% cuts a year from 2027 to 2030 for systems up to 15 kW: 45.44 ag in H1 2027 and 36.77 ag by 2030;
- half of any shekel-dollar move against a 3.5 base added to or offsetting the cut;
- a 20-year term instead of 25, with all guarantees ending in 2047;
- no more storage incentives;
- connection tenders from January 2028 for systems above 15 kW (homes up to 15 kW are exempt).

Comments closed on 10 November 2026, and we found no final decision as of 22 November. Calcalist reports that about 10% of the electricity tariff currently funds renewable-energy support, which is the Authority's stated reason to reduce it. For what this means for timing, read [why connecting before 2027 matters](/il/blog/lock-in-before-2027).

## Why are the export rates so much higher than utility solar?

Calcalist puts large high-voltage solar on the free market at about 15 to 20 ag/kWh off-peak without subsidy, against 28 to 48 ag for low-voltage rooftop systems. The gap is a deliberate subsidy to get panels onto roofs near where power is used. Globes reported in 2025 that the Electricity Authority estimated the 150,000-roof target would cost ILS 888 million a year, or 2.5% on everyone's bills. That cost is the background to the cuts.

## How to read your own bill

- Check whether you have a single-phase or three-phase meter, and whether you are billed monthly or every two months. Both change the fixed charges.
- Multiply your kWh by 63.52 ag to check the energy line.
- If you have solar, look for the system management line and any export credit, and keep a copy of your tariff-track confirmation.
