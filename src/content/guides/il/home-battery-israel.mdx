A home battery in Israel can make sense, mainly for two reasons. It moves midday solar power into the evening peak, which on the IEC time-of-use tariff costs 172.23 agorot per kWh in summer. And it keeps essentials running in an outage. The financial case is less certain than for panels alone: the storage incentive is reported to close at the end of 2026, and we found no reliable Israeli battery prices. Here is what we can and can't confirm.

## Key numbers

- **172.23 ag/kWh** including VAT: summer peak, 17:00 to 23:00 Sunday to Thursday, on the IEC low-voltage time-of-use tariff from 1 July 2026 ([IEC](https://iecmedia.iec.co.il/media/5malqupk/n%D7%AA%D7%A2%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%A4%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%91%D7%90%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%AA-1726-%D7%9E%D7%A2%D7%9E-18.pdf)).
- **50.42 ag/kWh**: summer off-peak on the same tariff, including VAT (IEC).
- **114.70 ag/kWh**: winter peak, 17:00 to 22:00 every day, December to February (IEC).
- **ILS 90,000 to 100,000**: the Ministry of Energy's example cost for 15 kW of solar with 10 kWh of storage, with about 10% return and about 8 years payback (Nadlan Center, Jan 2026).
- **9.09 ag/kWh**: IEC system management charge on power consumed from your own solar or storage (Globes English, 2026).
- **End of 2026**: the complementary tariff for systems with storage closes to new joiners (Calcalist, sponsored-style article). The 2027 draft drops storage incentives (Calcalist; Globes).

## How does a battery save money in Israel?

On the flat rate (63.52 ag/kWh including VAT), every kWh costs the same at any hour. A battery then only helps if your export rate is well below the price you avoid. With exports paid about 48 ag for systems connected by the end of 2026, the gap is small, and smaller still after the 9.09 ag system management charge.

On the time-of-use tariff, the picture changes:

| Season | Peak, incl. VAT | Off-peak, incl. VAT | Gap per kWh moved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Summer (Jun to Sep) | 172.23 ag | 50.42 ag | 121.81 ag |
| Winter (Dec to Feb) | 114.70 ag | 46.63 ag | 68.07 ag |
| Spring and autumn | 49.58 ag | 45.78 ag | 3.80 ag |

Source: IEC low-voltage TOU tariff from 1 July 2026.

In summer and winter, a kWh stored at midday and used at 18:00 avoids a peak price that is two to three times the off-peak price. In spring and autumn the gap is almost nothing, so the battery earns little for a third of the year. Check two things with your installer and the IEC: whether you can be on this tariff, and what its fixed charges are. On a two-monthly bill they are ILS 37.88 plus ILS 18.33 a month including VAT, higher than on the flat rate.

## What changes for batteries in 2027?

According to Calcalist and Globes, the Electricity Authority's draft of 19 October 2026 proposes ending support for batteries installed alongside rooftop solar. The Authority's stated reason is falling costs and cheaper low-voltage storage alternatives (Globes). The comment period closed on 10 November 2026, and we found no final decision as of 22 November.

A sponsored-style Calcalist article lists a "complementary tariff" for systems with storage among the tracks closing at the end of 2026. We could not find its value in any source we could open. If an installer quotes it, ask for the Electricity Authority decision number and the rate in writing.

## What does a battery do in a power cut?

The Ministry of Energy guide, as reported by Nadlan Center, says about 10 kWh of storage runs lighting, a fridge, chargers and an air conditioner for a few hours. That is backup, not whole-home independence. Two practical points:

- **Not every battery system provides backup.** The inverter and wiring must be able to island the home from the grid. Ask whether the quote includes a backup circuit, and which circuits it covers.
- **Backup has value that doesn't show on the bill.** If you work from home, keep medicine refrigerated or live where outages are common, it can justify a battery that doesn't pay back on tariffs alone.

## Is a battery worth it with new panels?

The Ministry's example, 15 kW with 10 kWh for ILS 90,000 to 100,000 paying back in about 8 years, suggests a paired system can still pay back within a reasonable time. Compare that to mako/Bizportal's range of ILS 3,500 to 5,500 per kW before VAT for panels alone. For 15 kW that is ILS 52,500 to 82,500 before VAT, or 61,950 to 97,350 with 18% VAT ([is rooftop solar worth it in Israel](/il/blog/is-rooftop-solar-worth-it-israel-2026)). The Ministry's combined figure overlaps with the panel-only range, so the battery's implied price is unclear. Ask for the battery to be priced as a separate line.

Globes (December 2025), citing GreenDays, notes that adding storage batteries raises the cost of the mandatory solar roofs on new houses. It gave no per-kWh battery price.

## How to judge a battery quote

1. **Get the battery priced separately** from the panels, in ILS per usable kWh.
2. **Ask which tariff the savings assume:** flat rate or time-of-use, and the storage complementary tariff if connecting in 2026.
3. **Deduct the 9.09 ag system management charge** from every kWh you plan to use from the battery.
4. **Check the warranty** in years and in cycles or throughput, and what happens to it if the installer closes.
5. **Size to your evening use.** A battery larger than what you use between 17:00 and 23:00 sits half-empty most days.

If the numbers don't work, panels alone may still pay. See [how much rooftop solar earns in Israel](/il/guides/how-much-rooftop-solar-earns-israel).
