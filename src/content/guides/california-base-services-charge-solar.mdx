PG&E and SDG&E now bill most homes a Base Services Charge of about $24 a month, with about $6 for CARE and $12 for FERA customers, while lowering the price per kWh. Solar credits cannot pay this charge. For solar owners that means two things: a fixed amount you pay no matter how much you generate, and a slightly lower value for each kWh your panels replace. For most systems the effect on payback is modest, but it is real, and quotes should reflect it.

## Key numbers

- **About $24 a month:** PG&E's and SDG&E's Base Services Charge for most residential customers (utility pages, November 2026).
- **About $6 / $12 a month:** the CARE and FERA versions (same pages).
- **October 2025:** SDG&E start; **March 2026:** PG&E start (utility pages).
- **About 5 cents per kWh:** SDG&E's estimate of how much lower its per-kWh delivery price is, about 10% (estimate as of February 1, 2025).
- **29.75 cents per kWh:** California's average residential price, August 2026, down from 30.92 cents in August 2025 (EIA, preliminary).

## What is California's Base Services Charge?

It is a flat monthly charge that covers part of the cost of being connected to the grid. PG&E says it covers infrastructure and maintenance, energy programs and call center services. Before, those costs were mostly built into the per-kWh price. Now part of them is billed as a fixed amount and the per-kWh price is lower.

| Utility | Standard | CARE | FERA | Start (per utility) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PG&E | about $24.00 | about $6.00 | about $12.00 | March 2026 bills |
| SDG&E | about $24 ($22.22 to $26.18 by billing days) | about $6 | about $12 | October 2025 |
| SCE | not yet confirmed by RayValue | not yet confirmed | not yet confirmed | not yet confirmed |

PG&E notes the charge is calculated per day, so the monthly amount moves slightly with the length of the billing period. PG&E also says the Base Services Charge replaces its old monthly Minimum Electric Charge.

## Can solar customers avoid the fixed charge?

No. PG&E says solar customers "still use the electric grid" and pay the same charge as non-solar customers. SDG&E says NEM customers keep getting monthly statements, that the charge "is not eligible to be offset by applied generation credits," and that for Solar Billing Plan customers it is a non-nettable charge that export credits cannot offset.

PG&E's sample NEM 2 true-up statement for March 2026 to March 2027 explains that the old monthly minimum is replaced by the Base Services Charge, which is paid monthly to stay connected to the grid, while energy charges and credits still roll up to the annual true-up. See [PG&E true-up bill explained](/guides/pge-true-up-bill-explained).

## How does the fixed charge change solar payback?

Two effects, in opposite directions for different homes.

**1. Lower per-kWh price, lower savings per kWh.** Solar saves you money by replacing kWh you would have bought. If each kWh costs less, each kWh of solar saves less. Illustrative arithmetic only: if your panels replace 400 kWh a month and the per-kWh price is about 5 cents lower, as SDG&E estimates for delivery, that is about $20 a month less in savings than under the old rates.

**2. A cost solar cannot touch.** Under the old structure, a home that covered most of its use with solar could push its bill close to the minimum. Now about $24 a month stays on the bill regardless. Over a year that is about $288 at the standard level (simple arithmetic, $24 × 12) that solar does not reduce.

What it does not change: the value of a kWh you export under the Solar Billing Plan is set by the export price sheet, not the retail price. See [California NEM 3.0 export rates by utility](/guides/california-solar-export-rates-by-utility).

## Who is hit hardest, and who is not?

- **Larger systems that offset most of a home's use** lose the most, because more of their savings came from avoiding per-kWh charges.
- **CARE and FERA households** pay a smaller fixed charge, so the change is smaller in dollars.
- **Homes that add electric loads** such as an EV or heat pump may benefit from the lower per-kWh price on the extra use, which also changes how much solar or battery they need.

## What to check in a solar quote now

1. Does the savings estimate include the Base Services Charge as an unavoidable cost?
2. Does it use today's per-kWh prices, not pre-2025 prices?
3. Does it assume a rate increase every year? Ask what rate and why. See [red flags in a solar quote](/blog/solar-quote-red-flags-2026).
4. Does it apply any federal credit? The Section 25D credit is $0 for 2026 installs.
