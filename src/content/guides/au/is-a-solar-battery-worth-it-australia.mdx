For many Australian homes with solar, yes. A battery now pays back fastest where grid power is expensive and evening use is high. CHOICE's 2026 estimate puts simple payback at about 6 to 8 years in Adelaide, Perth, Brisbane and Sydney, and 12 to 21 years in Canberra, Darwin, Melbourne and Hobart. The federal rebate is at its 2026 level until 31 December. After that it steps down every six months, so the case gets a little weaker over time.

## Key numbers

- **Federal rebate:** 6.8 STCs per usable kWh for batteries certified for compliance by 31 December 2026, falling to 5.7 from 1 January 2027 (regulation F2026L00093).
- **Target discount:** about 30% of the installed price (CER, 17 February 2026).
- **Installed price after the federal rebate:** about $7,000 to $11,000 for 10 kWh and $11,000 to $15,000 for 15 kWh (CHOICE, using SolarQuotes data, updated 12 June 2026; secondary).
- **Median annual bill, 2023-24 (ACCC):** $1,565 grid only, $1,279 with solar, $936 with solar and a battery, $580 in a virtual power plant.
- **Flat-rate usage caps for 2026-27 (AER DMO):** 27.97 c/kWh (Energex, SE Queensland) to 41.91 c/kWh (SA Power Networks).
- **NSW evening export value 2026-27 (IPART benchmarks):** 16.9 to 33.3 c/kWh, depending on the network. Midday exports are worth about 3 to 4 c/kWh.

## Is a solar battery worth it in Australia?

The basic sum is simple. Every kWh you store at midday instead of exporting earns you the low export rate. You then use it in the evening and avoid buying it at the full retail rate. The gap between the two is what a battery earns each day.

That gap is wide in 2026-27. IPART's [time-of-day benchmarks](https://www.ipart.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/cm9_documents/Fact-Sheet-Time-of-day-solar-feed-in-tariff-benchmarks-2026-27.PDF) value NSW solar exports at around 3 to 4 c/kWh for most of the day. The AER's [2026-27 default market offer](https://www.aer.gov.au/system/files/2026-05/AER%20-%20Final%20determination%20-%20Default%20market%20offer%202026%E2%80%9327.pdf) caps flat usage rates at between about 28 and 42 c/kWh. A battery that shifts 7 to 10 kWh a day across that gap is doing useful work every day of the year.

## Payback by capital city

CHOICE published a simple payback table using one set of assumptions in every city: $8,500 installed after the federal rebate only, and 7 kWh of overnight use. It is a rough guide, but it shows how much location matters.

| City | Annual saving (CHOICE estimate) | Simple payback |
|---|---|---|
| Adelaide | $1,350 | 6.3 years |
| Perth | $1,120 | 7.6 years |
| Brisbane | $1,100 | 7.7 years |
| Sydney | $1,030 | 8.3 years |
| Canberra | $700 | 12.1 years |
| Darwin | $620 | 13.7 years |
| Melbourne | $610 | 13.9 years |
| Hobart | $410 | 20.7 years |

Source: [CHOICE, solar home battery rebate](https://www.choice.com.au/home-and-living/energy-and-water/energy-saving-products/articles/solar-home-battery-rebate), updated 12 June 2026. CHOICE says payback depends on "electricity prices, feed-in tariffs and weather". It does not publish the tariff used for each city.

Adelaide leads because SA has the highest usage cap in the DMO regions. Hobart and Melbourne trail because their retail prices are lower, which shrinks the gap a battery can earn.

## Who a battery pays for

A battery tends to pay back fastest if most of these are true:

- **You already have solar and export a lot at midday.** The rebate requires solar, and a battery with nothing to store sits idle.
- **You use a lot of power after sunset.** Cooking, heating or cooling, a pool pump or an EV charged overnight all count.
- **You pay a high retail rate.** SA, regional NSW and Perth homes start ahead.
- **You can earn evening export rates or join a virtual power plant.** The ACCC found VPP households had a median bill of $580 in 2023-24, the lowest of any group. Some states pay extra for joining. See [state battery rebates](/au/guides/state-battery-rebates).
- **You get the 2026 factor.** Being certified for compliance by 31 December 2026 earns 6.8 STCs per kWh instead of 5.7. See [what you lose by waiting](/au/guides/battery-rebate-changes-2027).

## What the bill data shows

The ACCC's [August 2025 report](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/electricity-industry-on-notice-as-more-households-invest-in-subsidised-batteries-and-solar) compared 2023-24 bills across the National Electricity Market:

| Household type | Median annual bill | vs grid-only |
|---|---|---|
| Grid only | $1,565 | n/a |
| Rooftop solar | $1,279 | about 18% less |
| Solar and battery | $936 | about 40% less |
| In a virtual power plant | $580 | about 63% less |

These are different households, not the same home before and after. Battery owners may use power differently or have bigger solar systems. Read the gap as a sign of what is possible, not a promise.

## How to size it so it pays

Size the battery to your evening and overnight use, not to the biggest rebate. The rebate pays 100% of the factor only on the first 14 kWh of usable capacity, then 60% up to 28 kWh and 15% up to 50 kWh. A battery much bigger than you can empty each night earns less per dollar. See the [battery size guide](/au/guides/solar-battery-size-guide).

## When it doesn't pay

Batteries are a weaker buy for low-usage homes, homes on cheap tariffs, and homes without much spare solar. If CHOICE's Hobart or Melbourne paybacks look like your situation, read [when a battery is not worth it](/au/guides/when-a-battery-is-not-worth-it) first.
