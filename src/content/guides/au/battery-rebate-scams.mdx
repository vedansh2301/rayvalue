Most battery rebate scams lean on two facts: the federal rebate is real, and it is complicated. Watch for "government" callers and door-knockers, "free battery" offers, rebates for schemes that have closed, and pressure to sign before a deadline. Watch too for quotes that show one lump "rebate" with no STC count. Legitimate offers can show you the usable kWh, the number of STCs, the price per STC and the installer's accreditation.

## Key numbers

- **Rebate is a discount, not free:** the CER says the program aims for a discount of **about 30%** (17 February 2026).
- **STCs are fixed by formula:** **6.8 per usable kWh** for the first 14 kWh in 2026, **5.7** from 1 January 2027 (regulation F2026L00093).
- **Typical admin fees:** CHOICE says expect about **10%** of the rebate value to go in fees (secondary, June 2026).
- **Installation quality:** **59.72%** of **4,322** CER battery inspections found substandard work, and **0.86%** were unsafe (as of 30 September 2026).

## Common battery rebate scams

The [NSW Government](https://www.nsw.gov.au/legal-and-justice/consumer-rights-and-protection/be-a-smart-consumer/avoiding-energy-scams) says energy rebate scams "are becoming more common", especially for solar, home batteries and energy upgrades. Its red flags include:

- offers that sound too good to be true
- cold calls or door-to-door sales from people claiming to represent the government
- pressure to sign up or pay a deposit immediately
- government logos on unofficial websites
- large upfront payments
- promises of "no upfront costs"
- urgent phrases like "Register now" or "You'll miss out"

## Red flags specific to the battery rebate

| Red flag | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| "Free battery" or "government-funded battery" | The federal rebate is a partial discount. The CER's aim is about 30% off. |
| A rebate for QLD, NT or Victorian battery schemes | Queensland's Battery Booster and the NT grants have closed. Victoria no longer takes battery loan applications. |
| "Sign before 31 December to lock in the rebate" | The CER says rebates are set by installation date, not contract date. Only a completed, certified install gets 6.8. |
| One lump "rebate" figure | You can't check it. Ask for usable kWh, the STC count and the price per STC. |
| Rebate worked out on nominal (label) capacity | STCs are calculated on usable capacity only. |
| "Bigger battery, bigger rebate" | Above 28 kWh each kWh earns only 15% of the factor. |
| Installer can't give an SAA accreditation number | The CER requires a Solar Accreditation Australia installer with battery endorsement. |

## Is the installation date guaranteed?

Before the 1 May 2026 change, the CER [warned retailers](https://cer.gov.au/news-and-media/media/2026/march/battery-rebates-are-changing-1-may-2026) that the rebate change "could result in a price difference of thousands of dollars". It said retailers "should not be promising installations before 1 May unless they are certain the installations can be completed on time". Its advice to customers was to "confirm that the installation will be possible and the price quoted will be honoured". The same applies before the drop to 5.7 on 1 January 2027. Get the install date and the price-if-delayed in writing. See [what you lose by waiting](/au/guides/battery-rebate-changes-2027).

The CER also said it will report retailers who fail their obligations to fair trading bodies. It urged installers not to rush jobs to beat the changes.

## How to check a battery quote

1. **Usable capacity.** In kWh, not just the label figure.
2. **STCs.** Check the count against the formula: 6.8 × the first 14 kWh, then 60% and 15% tiers, rounded down. See [how the rebate is calculated](/au/guides/how-battery-stcs-work).
3. **Price per STC and fees.** CHOICE's figures imply about $40 per STC in 2026 and warn of about 10% in admin fees. A much lower price per STC means you get less of the rebate.
4. **Product.** On the Clean Energy Council approved battery list ([CER](https://cer.gov.au/batteries)).
5. **Installer.** Accredited by Solar Accreditation Australia with battery endorsement. The NSW Government also says the electrician needs a valid licence and suggests checking the seller is a New Energy Tech consumer code signatory.
6. **Savings claims.** Based on your own usage data, as the [ACCC](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/electricity-industry-on-notice-as-more-households-invest-in-subsidised-batteries-and-solar) recommends. The ACCC also advises comparing "a number of quotes from different providers".

## Poor installs are a real risk

The CER's [inspection results](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-renewable-energy-system-inspections/solar-battery-inspection-results-report) for batteries installed from 1 July 2025 found:

- 59.72% "substandard but were safe to remain in operation"
- 0.86% unsafe

The most common problem was labelling. All unsafe findings were due to installation practices and workmanship, not the batteries. A very cheap quote with a same-week install deserves extra questions.

## If something goes wrong

- Don't pay a large deposit to someone who contacted you out of the blue.
- If you feel pressured, the NSW Government's advice is to "say no and hang up or close the door".
- Report misleading conduct to your state or territory fair trading body or the ACCC.
