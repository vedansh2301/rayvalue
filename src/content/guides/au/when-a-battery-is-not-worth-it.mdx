A battery is not worth it when it can't save enough each day to repay its cost well within its life. That usually happens in homes with low evening use, low retail prices or little spare solar. Even at the 2026 rebate level, CHOICE estimates a $8,500 battery takes 12 to 21 years to pay back in Canberra, Darwin, Melbourne and Hobart. In Adelaide it is about 6 years. If your home looks like the slow cases, wait or spend the money elsewhere.

## Key numbers

- **Slowest paybacks (CHOICE, June 2026, secondary):** Hobart **20.7 years**, Melbourne **13.9**, Darwin **13.7**, Canberra **12.1**. Fastest is Adelaide at **6.3**. All assume $8,500 installed after the federal rebate and 7 kWh of overnight use.
- **Tasmania's regulated feed-in tariff:** **9.276 c/kWh** from 1 July 2026. That is a high export rate, which shrinks what a battery adds (Aurora Energy).
- **Rebate taper:** capacity above **28 kWh** earns only **15%** of the factor (regulation F2026L00093).
- **Installation quality:** of **4,322** CER inspections of batteries installed from 1 July 2025, **59.72%** were substandard but safe and **0.86%** unsafe (as of 30 September 2026).

## When is a solar battery not worth it?

### 1. You use little power after dark

A battery only earns money on the kWh it moves from midday to evening. If you use 3 or 4 kWh overnight, a 10 kWh battery mostly sits half-full. CHOICE's payback table assumes 7 kWh overnight. Lower use than that means a longer payback than the table shows.

### 2. Your retail price is low

The saving per kWh is the retail rate you avoid, minus the feed-in tariff you give up. Lower retail prices mean slower payback. CHOICE's 2026 figures show the effect:

| City | Annual saving (CHOICE) | Simple payback |
|---|---|---|
| Adelaide | $1,350 | 6.3 years |
| Sydney | $1,030 | 8.3 years |
| Canberra | $700 | 12.1 years |
| Melbourne | $610 | 13.9 years |
| Hobart | $410 | 20.7 years |

Source: [CHOICE](https://www.choice.com.au/home-and-living/energy-and-water/energy-saving-products/articles/solar-home-battery-rebate), updated 12 June 2026. The usage caps in the AER's [2026-27 determination](https://www.aer.gov.au/system/files/2026-05/AER%20-%20Final%20determination%20-%20Default%20market%20offer%202026%E2%80%9327.pdf) range from 27.97 c/kWh in SE Queensland to 41.91 c/kWh in SA, so the same battery earns very different amounts.

### 3. Your feed-in tariff is still decent

In Tasmania the regulated rate is [9.276 c/kWh](https://www.auroraenergy.com.au/residential/payments/tasmanian-energy-prices), well above the 3 to 6 c benchmarks in NSW. Each kWh you store instead of exporting gives up more, so the battery's margin is thinner. This is part of why Hobart has the slowest payback in CHOICE's table.

### 4. You don't have spare solar

The federal rebate needs solar, and a battery can only store your surplus. A small or shaded system that barely covers daytime use, especially in winter, leaves the battery empty. Fix the solar side first.

### 5. You oversize it for the rebate

The [regulation](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L00093/asmade/2026-02-09/text/original/pdf) pays full STCs on the first 14 kWh, 60% to 28 kWh, and 15% to 50 kWh. A 40 kWh battery earns 164 STCs in 2026, only 12 more than a 28 kWh one. If your nightly use is 8 kWh, the extra capacity mostly never cycles. See the [battery size guide](/au/guides/solar-battery-size-guide).

### 6. You may move soon, or can't install well

Paybacks of 12 years or more don't suit a home you'll sell in three. Batteries also need a good installation. The CER's [inspection report](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-renewable-energy-system-inspections/solar-battery-inspection-results-report) found 59.72% of 4,322 inspected installations substandard and 0.86% unsafe. It says the unsafe findings came from workmanship, not the batteries. A cheap, rushed job can cost you in repairs and lost savings.

## Warning signs in a sales pitch

- Savings projections with no reference to your own interval data.
- A payback that ignores the feed-in tariff you give up.
- "Bigger is better because of the rebate."
- Pressure to sign before a deadline without a guaranteed install date.

The ACCC tells buyers to "take their time and not feel pressured to rush in straight away" ([ACCC, August 2025](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/electricity-industry-on-notice-as-more-households-invest-in-subsidised-batteries-and-solar)).

## What to do instead

If a battery doesn't pay for you yet:

- Shift loads into the middle of the day.
- Look at time-of-use or Solar Sharer plans in NSW, SE Queensland and SA.
- Revisit in a year. The rebate will be lower, but the CER says the step-downs are meant to track falling battery costs.

If your situation is closer to the fast cases, see [who a battery pays for](/au/guides/is-a-solar-battery-worth-it-australia).
