An average Australian car's driving adds roughly 5 kWh a day of electricity once it's electric. That's small enough to charge straight from rooftop solar, or for free in the Solar Sharer midday window, if the car is home during the day. A home battery mainly makes sense for EV charging when the car is only home at night, and even then the rebate tiers make a battery beyond about 14 to 28 kWh poor value.

## Key numbers

- **Typical EV use:** **14.4 kWh per 100 km** (ARENA figure, quoted by the NSW Government).
- **Average distance:** **12,100 km** a year per vehicle (ABS, year to June 2020, all vehicle types).
- **So:** about **1,740 kWh a year**, or **4.8 kWh a day**, about **33 km a day** of driving (our arithmetic).
- **Charging speed (NSW Government):** wall socket **10-15 km of range per hour**; wallbox charger **40-70 km per hour**. Home charger install typically **$1,500 to $3,000**.
- **Solar Sharer:** free usage **11 am-2 pm** (NSW, SE Qld) or **12 pm-3 pm** (SA), up to **24 kWh a day**, from 1 July 2026 (AER).
- **Battery rebate tiers:** full STC factor to **14 kWh**, **60%** from 14 to 28 kWh, **15%** from 28 to 50 kWh (CER).

## How much power does an EV add to your home?

The NSW Government's [Let's Drive EVs](https://letsdriveevs.nsw.gov.au/quick-facts-about-evs/) page quotes ARENA's figure of 14.4 kWh per 100 km for a typical EV. The [ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/industry/tourism-and-transport/survey-motor-vehicle-use-australia/latest-release) found Australian vehicles averaged 12.1 thousand km in the year to June 2020. Multiply them:

| Driving per year | kWh per year (14.4 kWh/100 km) | kWh per day |
|---|---|---|
| 8,000 km | 1,152 | 3.2 |
| 12,100 km (ABS average) | 1,742 | 4.8 |
| 15,000 km | 2,160 | 5.9 |
| 20,000 km | 2,880 | 7.9 |

Bigger cars and highway driving use more per 100 km. Use your car's own figure if you know it.

## Charge from solar first, battery second

Every kWh stored in a home battery and then moved into the car passes through two sets of losses, and it uses up battery capacity you paid for. If the car is parked at home during the day, charging it directly from the panels is the cheaper route.

How much fits in a midday window? Using the NSW Government's charging speeds over 3 hours:

| Charger | Range added per hour | Range added in 3 hours |
|---|---|---|
| Wall socket (10 A) | 10 to 15 km | 30 to 45 km |
| Wallbox | 40 to 70 km | 120 to 210 km |

So even a standard wall socket, used for 3 midday hours, roughly covers the average 33 km a day. A wallbox can cover several days' driving in one session.

## Using the Solar Sharer free window

If you live in NSW, SE Queensland or South Australia and have a smart meter, you can opt in to the [Solar Sharer Offer](/au/guides/solar-sharer-free-electricity-explained). The AER set a free window of 11 am to 2 pm (12 pm to 3 pm in SA), with up to 24 kWh a day free. Usage outside the window costs 1 to 4 c/kWh more than the standard time-of-use default offer.

For EV owners who work from home or have a second car that stays home, that window is an easy way to charge without solar or a battery at all. Remember that the car and the rest of the house share the 24 kWh daily cap.

## What size battery for an EV?

If the car is only home in the evening and overnight, a battery can store daytime solar for it. Size it from your evening house load plus the car's daily need:

1. **Find your current overnight use** from your smart-meter data (roughly 4 pm to 7 am).
2. **Add the EV's daily kWh** from the table above, about 5 kWh for average driving.
3. **Check the rebate tiers.** The first 14 kWh of usable capacity earn the full STC factor. From 14 to 28 kWh they earn 60%, and above 28 kWh only 15% ([CER](https://cer.gov.au/news-and-media/news/2026/february/changes-to-rebate-solar-batteries-1-may)).

For many households, overnight house use plus about 5 kWh for the car lands within the first one or two tiers. CHOICE's June 2026 figures put a 15 kWh battery at $11,000 to $15,000 installed after the rebate and a 20 kWh battery at $14,000 to $19,000 ([CHOICE](https://www.choice.com.au/home-and-living/energy-and-water/energy-saving-products/articles/solar-home-battery-rebate)). Compare that with the cost of a cheap overnight EV plan or the Solar Sharer window before you buy capacity just for the car.

## Do you need more solar panels for an EV?

Probably a little. About 1,700 to 2,900 kWh a year of extra demand is a meaningful share of a typical home's use. If you're installing solar now and expect an EV, factor it in when choosing system size; see [solar panel cost by system size](/au/guides/solar-panel-cost-by-system-size). Remember that a battery needs spare daytime solar to fill it, as well as power for the car.

## The cheapest order to do things

1. Charge at home in daylight from your panels, or in the Solar Sharer window if available.
2. Use a timer or smart charger so the car charges when power is cheapest.
3. Only then size a battery for whatever EV charging still has to happen at night.
