If you own a home in the ACT, you can borrow $2,000 to $20,000 at 3% interest over up to 10 years for a home battery and other electric upgrades. There are no upfront costs or fees. It sits on top of the federal battery rebate, which comes off the price first. Since 1 July 2026 the scheme is no longer interest-free, but 3% is still cheap money for a battery bought before the federal rebate drops on 1 January 2027.

## Key numbers

- **Loan size:** **$2,000 to $20,000** (scheme page, October 2026).
- **Interest:** **3%**, with no upfront costs or fees.
- **Term:** up to **10 years**.
- **Earlier participants:** households that applied or were approved before **1 July 2026** can access up to **$15,000** over the life of the scheme.
- **Federal battery rebate:** **6.8 STCs per usable kWh** for the first 14 kWh if the battery is certified for compliance by 31 December 2026; **5.7** from 1 January 2027.
- **Canberra battery payback (CHOICE, June 2026, secondary):** about **12.1 years** for an $8,500 battery covering 7 kWh overnight, with the federal rebate only.

## How the ACT Sustainable Household Scheme works

The [Sustainable Household Scheme](https://www.climatechoices.act.gov.au/policy-programs/sustainable-household-scheme) is run by the ACT Government with Brighte as the loan provider. The page says eligible homeowners "can borrow up to $20,000" with "up to 10 years to repay it", and calls it "a low-interest loan, with a 3% interest rate and no upfront costs or fees".

Eligible products include:

- household battery storage systems
- electric heating and cooling systems
- heat pump hot water and evacuated tube solar hot water
- electric stove tops
- electric vehicles, zero-emission motorbikes and EV charging
- ceiling insulation
- electric cargo bikes

You can combine several products up to $20,000 over the life of the scheme. You may be able to borrow more subject to a credit check, but "the loan provider may charge interest on funds borrowed above the limit". Repayments start once the product is installed and operating.

## Who can apply

The page says the scheme is open to ACT residents who own a home (or hold a current driving licence when applying for an EV loan). You must meet the Scheme Guidelines and Brighte's lending criteria. That includes attending a free one-hour live workshop.

For concession card holders, the page points to the Home Energy Support Program, which offers zero-interest loans and loans for solar.

## What changed on 1 July 2026

The current page sets a 3% rate and a $20,000 maximum. It says households that applied or were approved before 1 July 2026 can access a maximum of $15,000 over the life of the scheme. If you read an older article describing an interest-free loan, check it against the current page.

## Using it for a battery

1. **Get the federal discount first.** The Cheaper Home Batteries Program requires the battery to be installed with solar, on the approved list, by an SAA-accredited installer ([CER](https://cer.gov.au/batteries)). The STC discount comes off the quote.
2. **Finance the balance.** Use the ACT loan for what's left, within the $20,000 limit.
3. **Mind the date.** The STC factor drops from 6.8 to 5.7 for batteries certified for compliance from 1 January 2027 ([regulation F2026L00093](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L00093/asmade/2026-02-09/text/original/pdf)). On a 10 kWh battery that is 68 STCs against 57. See [what you lose by waiting](/au/guides/battery-rebate-changes-2027).

## Does a battery pay in Canberra?

It is a middling case. CHOICE's 2026 table puts simple payback at about 12.1 years in Canberra, against 6.3 in Adelaide and 20.7 in Hobart. The same assumptions apply in every city ([CHOICE](https://www.choice.com.au/home-and-living/energy-and-water/energy-saving-products/articles/solar-home-battery-rebate), secondary). ActewAGL's regulated standing offer prices rose by an average of 2.7% from 1 July 2026, according to the [ICRC](https://www.icrc.act.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0011/3072782/Media-Release-Retail-electricity-price-recalibration-2026-27.pdf).

A 3% loan doesn't change the payback much, but it does mean you don't need the cash upfront. If your evening use is high and you have spare solar, it can make sense. If not, read [when a battery is not worth it](/au/guides/when-a-battery-is-not-worth-it).

## Checklist before you borrow

- **Confirm the battery qualifies for the federal rebate.** It must be 5 to 100 kWh nominal, on the Clean Energy Council approved list, able to join a virtual power plant if grid connected, and installed by an SAA-accredited installer with battery endorsement ([CER](https://cer.gov.au/batteries)).
- **Check the quote shows the STC discount.** Ask for the usable kWh, the number of STCs and the price per STC, so you only borrow the balance. See [how the rebate is calculated](/au/guides/how-battery-stcs-work).
- **Read the Scheme Guidelines** linked from the scheme page, and Brighte's lending criteria, before you book the workshop.
- **Know your limit.** If you applied before 1 July 2026, your lifetime cap is $15,000, not $20,000.
- **Plan the install date.** Repayments start when the battery is operating, and the federal factor depends on when it is certified for compliance.
