A typical 6.6 kW solar system costs about $6,000 to $9,000 installed in Australia in 2026, and a 10 kW system about $9,000 to $12,000, based on what buyers reported to SolarQuotes. The rule of thumb is about $1,000 per kW for a decent-quality system. Bigger systems cost less per kW, but they only pay off if you use the extra power, because feed-in tariffs are low.

## Key numbers

- **Installed price by size (SolarQuotes, October 2026, buyer-reported, secondary):** 3 kW **$3,500-$5,500**; 5 kW **$5,000-$7,500**; 6.6 kW **$6,000-$9,000**; 8 kW **$7,500-$11,000**; 10 kW **~$9,000-$12,000**; 13 kW **$11,000-$16,000**.
- **Rule of thumb:** **~$1,000 per kW** installed.
- **Most common size:** **6.6 kW** (CHOICE, March 2026).
- **STC rebate on 10 kW:** roughly **$2,500 to $3,000** at the point of sale in 2026 (SolarQuotes gives both figures).
- **Victoria:** Solar Homes rebate of up to **$1,400** for eligible households, with an optional interest-free loan (Solar Victoria).
- **Payback for 6.6 kW (CHOICE/SolarQuotes, January 2026):** from **3 years 9 months** (Darwin) to **6 years 5 months** (Melbourne).

## How the STC rebate works for solar panels

The federal Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme creates small-scale technology certificates (STCs) based on your system's size. SolarQuotes explains that installers usually take the value off your price at the point of sale, that the rebate depends on capacity rather than brand, and that its value "decreases slightly every January" as the scheme winds down. For a 10 kW system in 2026, it estimates the discount at around $2,500 in one section and about $3,000 in another. Check the STC line on your own quote.

## State solar rebates

- **Victoria:** Solar Victoria offers eligible households a rebate of up to $1,400 on solar panels, with the option of an interest-free loan. You must be approved before installing: "If you install your system before your eligibility is approved, you will not receive a rebate." Check the household income cap and other conditions on [Solar Victoria's page](https://www.solar.vic.gov.au/solar-panel-rebate).
- **Other states:** for state schemes that support batteries, see [state battery rebates](/au/guides/state-battery-rebates).

## What size system should you buy?

Feed-in tariffs have dropped, so exported power earns far less than power you use yourself. In Victoria, the regulator no longer sets a minimum feed-in tariff, and since 1 July 2025 retailers may set their own, with a floor of zero ([ESC](https://www.esc.vic.gov.au/electricity-and-gas/electricity-and-gas-tariffs-and-benchmarks/minimum-feed-tariff)). So:

- **Size for daytime use, plus what a battery will store.** CHOICE notes that if you're adding a battery, you'll want more panel capacity so there's enough daytime power to run the home and charge the battery.
- **Don't buy extra kW only to export,** unless your retailer pays a decent rate for it.
- **Plan for an EV or heat pump.** See [solar and batteries for EV charging](/au/guides/solar-battery-for-ev-charging).

## Get comparable quotes

Ask each installer for the system size in kW, the panel and inverter models, the STC discount in dollars, and expected yearly output. Then compare cost per kW.
