If your battery is installed and certified for compliance by 31 December 2026, it earns 6.8 small-scale technology certificates (STCs) per usable kWh. From 1 January 2027 the factor falls to 5.7, so a typical 10 to 14 kWh battery loses 11 to 16 certificates, worth roughly $440 to $640 at the STC price CHOICE's figures imply. The date that counts is installation and certification, not the date you sign, so a contract signed in December does not protect you if the installer can't finish in time.

## Key numbers

- **6.8 STCs per usable kWh** for batteries certified for compliance 1 May to 31 December 2026 (regulation F2026L00093, in force as of November 2026).
- **5.7 STCs per usable kWh** from 1 January to 30 June 2027, then **5.2** from 1 July 2027.
- The factor keeps falling every six months to **2.1** in July to December 2030.
- **Size tiers:** 100% of the factor on the first 14 kWh, 60% on 14 to 28 kWh, 15% on 28 to 50 kWh, with the total rounded down (from 1 May 2026).
- The CER says the aim is a discount of **about 30%** of the installed cost (17 February 2026).
- CHOICE puts the rebate at about **$272 per kWh** for May to December 2026 and **$228 per kWh** for January to June 2027, and says to expect about **10%** to go in admin fees (secondary source, updated 12 June 2026).

## When do battery rebates change in 2027?

Two changes are already locked in by regulation. The amending regulations set a table of factors by the date the battery "is certified for compliance". The [Federal Register of Legislation copy of F2026L00093](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L00093/asmade/2026-02-09/text/original/pdf) lists:

| Battery certified for compliance | STCs per usable kWh (first 14 kWh) |
|---|---|
| 1 May to 31 Dec 2026 | 6.8 |
| 1 Jan to 30 Jun 2027 | 5.7 |
| 1 Jul to 31 Dec 2027 | 5.2 |
| 1 Jan to 30 Jun 2028 | 4.6 |
| 1 Jul to 31 Dec 2028 | 4.1 |
| 1 Jan to 30 Jun 2029 | 3.6 |
| 1 Jul to 31 Dec 2029 | 3.1 |
| 1 Jan to 30 Jun 2030 | 2.6 |
| 1 Jul to 31 Dec 2030 | 2.1 |

The [Clean Energy Regulator's STC page](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-technology-certificates/calculate-small-scale-technology-certificate-entitlements) shows the same schedule. The CER's [February 2026 notice](https://cer.gov.au/news-and-media/news/2026/february/changes-to-rebate-solar-batteries-1-may) says the factor now falls more often (every six months) and faster, to track falling battery prices while keeping the discount at around 30%.

## How much do you lose by waiting until 2027?

We applied the regulation's method (factor times usable kWh, with the 60% and 15% tiers, rounded down) to common sizes. STC counts are exact under the rule. The dollar column uses $40 per STC, which is what CHOICE's $272 per kWh figure implies (6.8 × $40). It is an illustration, not a quote, and comes before admin fees.

| Usable capacity | STCs at 6.8 (2026) | STCs at 5.7 (Jan to Jun 2027) | STCs lost | About $ lost at $40/STC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 kWh | 68 | 57 | 11 | $440 |
| 13.5 kWh | 91 | 76 | 15 | $600 |
| 14 kWh | 95 | 79 | 16 | $640 |
| 20 kWh | 119 | 100 | 19 | $760 |
| 28 kWh | 152 | 127 | 25 | $1,000 |
| 40 kWh | 164 | 137 | 27 | $1,080 |
| 50 kWh | 174 | 146 | 28 | $1,120 |

Two things stand out. The loss grows slowly above 28 kWh because the top tier only counts at 15%. And waiting a further six months, to July 2027 at 5.2, costs a 14 kWh battery another 7 STCs (79 down to 72).

For the full method, see [how the battery rebate is calculated](/au/guides/how-battery-stcs-work).

## Does the contract date or the install date count?

The install. The CER's [March 2026 media release](https://cer.gov.au/news-and-media/media/2026/march/battery-rebates-are-changing-1-may-2026) says plainly that "rebates are determined by the installation date, not when a contract is signed". The regulation ties the factor to the date the battery is certified for compliance.

That has practical consequences in December:

- Ask the installer for a written install date and what happens to the price if they miss 31 December.
- The CER told retailers they "should not be promising installations before 1 May unless they are certain the installations can be completed on time", and that a quote must reflect the changes. The same logic applies to the 1 January step.
- If the quote shows the rebate as a fixed dollar discount, ask whether it is recalculated at the 5.7 factor if the job slips into 2027.

## Should you rush to beat the deadline?

Only if the battery suits your home anyway. The CER urged installers "not to rush installations to try and beat the changes", and the difference on a 10 kWh battery is in the hundreds of dollars, not thousands. A poorly sized or poorly installed battery costs more than that over its life.

A battery tends to make sense when you already have solar, export a lot in the middle of the day, and use a good share of power in the evening. Read [is a home battery worth it in Australia](/au/guides/is-a-solar-battery-worth-it-australia) and [when a battery doesn't pay](/au/guides/when-a-battery-is-not-worth-it) before you decide.

## What has to be true for the rebate to apply at all?

The [CER's battery page](https://cer.gov.au/batteries) lists the conditions, including:

- 5 to 100 kWh nominal capacity, with STCs only on the first 50 kWh of **usable** capacity.
- On the Clean Energy Council approved battery list.
- Installed with new or existing solar.
- Able to join a virtual power plant if grid connected (joining is not required).
- Installed by a Solar Accreditation Australia (SAA) accredited installer with battery endorsement.

## What to do now

1. Get two or three written quotes that show usable capacity, the number of STCs, the STC price assumed and any fees.
2. Check each quote's STC count against the table above (or the CER's calculator).
3. Ask for an install date in writing and what happens if it slips past 31 December 2026.
4. Check any state top-up you can stack. See [state battery rebates on top of the federal one](/au/guides/state-battery-rebates).
