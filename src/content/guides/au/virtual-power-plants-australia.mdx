A virtual power plant (VPP) is a provider that remotely controls a group of home batteries and sells their stored power to the grid when demand peaks. You get paid for it: in NSW an upfront state incentive plus whatever the provider offers; in WA joining is the price of the state rebate. Whether it's worth it comes down to the contract, mainly how much of your battery the provider can use and when.

## Key numbers

- **NSW VPP incentive:** upfront payment scaled to battery capacity **up to 28 kWh**; batteries over **2** and up to **50 kWh** are eligible (Energy NSW, updated 31 August 2026). CHOICE puts it at **up to $1,500**.
- **NSW status:** open, **no current closing date**; claimable **once per NMI**.
- **WA:** VPP membership is **required** for the state rebate of **$130/kWh** (Synergy, max $1,300) or **$380/kWh** (Horizon Power, max $3,800).
- **Federal rebate:** grid-connected batteries must be **VPP-capable** to claim STCs (CER). Joining is optional (CHOICE).

## What is a virtual power plant?

Energy NSW describes VPPs as "connected groups of batteries that allow excess stored power to be sold to the grid during times of peak demand." A VPP provider "remotely manage[s] multiple batteries," and each provider sets different conditions, including the price you get for exported power and when it can access your battery ([Energy NSW](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/households/grants-rebates/household-energy-saving-upgrades/connect-your-battery-virtual-power-plant)).

CHOICE says the benefit to you "might be bill credits, higher tariff rates or a cut of profits on electricity sold (depending on your VPP plan)."

|---|---|---|---|

### NSW details

Energy NSW says connecting to a VPP lets you be paid both an upfront incentive and, on an ongoing basis, for the stored energy you sell through the VPP. The upfront amount "depends on the battery capacity in kilowatt-hours (kWh) made available to the grid, up to 28 kWh," and also on the provider and the contract you agree to. You may receive it in instalments, as an upfront payment or as a bill credit. Batteries installed under the federal Cheaper Home Batteries Program up to 50 kWh may be eligible. If you claimed the incentive between 1 November 2024 and 30 June 2025, you can't claim it again.

### WA details

WA's scheme says rebates and loans "are only available to applicants that join a VPP product." Horizon Power customers must join Horizon Power's VPP. Synergy customers can choose Synergy's or an approved alternative listed by Energy Policy WA.

## Is joining a VPP worth it?

**It can be worth it when:**

- A state incentive (NSW or WA) pays you upfront, which cuts your effective battery price.
- Your battery is bigger than your overnight needs, so there's spare stored energy to sell.
- The contract pays well for exports at peak times and lets you keep a reserve.

**Be careful when:**

- You bought the battery mainly for blackout backup. Ask how much charge the provider can draw down before a storm or an evening peak.
- The plan's usage rates are higher than your current plan. A VPP credit can be wiped out by a worse retail tariff.
- The contract has an exit fee or a minimum term.

Energy NSW recommends comparing offers from more than one provider, and weighing "both the upfront incentive you receive, and how much income you can generate from the virtual power plant in the long-term."

## Questions to ask a VPP provider

1. How much of my battery can you use, and how often?
2. What minimum charge will you always leave me for backup?
3. What do you pay per kWh exported, and is it a credit or cash?
4. What are my usage rates and supply charge on your plan, compared with my current plan?
5. Is there a minimum term or exit fee? What happens if I move house?
6. How is the state incentive paid, and do I lose any of it if I leave early?

NSW says your provider must give you a cooling-off period after the paperwork, during which you can change the conditions or cancel.
