A home battery in Australia costs roughly $7,000 to $11,000 for 10 kWh and $14,000 to $19,000 for 20 kWh installed, after the federal rebate, based on CHOICE's June 2026 figures from SolarQuotes data. The federal rebate takes about $272 off each of the first 14 kWh, less above that. A state top-up in WA or NSW can cut the price further. Whether it pays back depends far more on where you live and how much power you use at night than on the sticker price.

## Key numbers

- **Installed price after the federal rebate (CHOICE, June 2026, secondary):** 10 kWh **$7,000-$11,000**; 15 kWh **$11,000-$15,000**; 20 kWh **$14,000-$19,000**; 30 kWh **$18,000-$22,000**; 50 kWh **$24,000-$30,000**.
- **Federal rebate value:** about **$272 per usable kWh** for the first 14 kWh from 1 May 2026, before admin fees (CHOICE). Expect to lose about **10%** to admin fees.
- **Tiers:** **100%** of the STC factor up to 14 kWh, **60%** from 14 to 28 kWh, **15%** from 28 to 50 kWh (CER).
- **Policy aim:** keep the discount at "around **30%**" across battery sizes (CER, February 2026).
- **Next drop:** STC factor **6.8** until 31 December 2026, then **5.7** from 1 January 2027 (regulation).
- **WA top-up:** **$130/kWh** (Synergy, max **$1,300**) or **$380/kWh** (Horizon Power, max **$3,800**), VPP required.

## How much does a solar battery cost by size?

| Battery size (usable) | Installed price after federal rebate | Price per kWh after rebate |
|---|---|---|
| 10 kWh | $7,000 to $11,000 | $700 to $1,100 |
| 15 kWh | $11,000 to $15,000 | $733 to $1,000 |
| 20 kWh | $14,000 to $19,000 | $700 to $950 |
| 30 kWh | $18,000 to $22,000 | $600 to $733 |
| 50 kWh | $24,000 to $30,000 | $480 to $600 |

Prices: [CHOICE](https://www.choice.com.au/home-and-living/energy-and-water/energy-saving-products/articles/solar-home-battery-rebate), using SolarQuotes information, last updated 12 June 2026. Per-kWh column is our division. CHOICE notes a complex install "can increase costs considerably". These ranges are a guide, not a quote.

Bigger batteries cost less per kWh, but you only save money on the kWh you actually use each night. A 30 kWh battery in a home that uses 8 kWh overnight doesn't earn three times what a 10 kWh one does. See [what size battery you need](/au/guides/solar-battery-size-guide).

## What the federal rebate is worth

The rebate is paid through small-scale technology certificates (STCs). Your installer usually claims them and takes the value off your price. The [CER](https://cer.gov.au/batteries) calculates STCs on usable capacity, only for the first 50 kWh, and only once per premises. Using CHOICE's $272 per kWh figure for the first tier:

| Usable kWh | Rebate tier | Approx. rebate before admin fees |
|---|---|---|
| First 14 | 100% | $272 per kWh, about $3,808 for 14 kWh |
| 14 to 28 | 60% | about $163 per extra kWh |
| 28 to 50 | 15% | about $41 per extra kWh |

These are approximate: STCs are rounded down and their market value moves. Our [STC calculation guide](/au/guides/how-battery-stcs-work) shows the exact certificate counts.

## Admin fees and other costs

CHOICE warns: "Expect to lose 10% of your rate to admin fees." Ask each installer to show:

- The number of STCs claimed and the dollar value applied
- Any admin or processing fee taken from the rebate
- Extras such as a backup circuit, switchboard upgrade or a new inverter

The CER also notes that a battery must be VPP-capable to claim STCs if it's grid-connected, and an existing inverter that doesn't meet the requirements may need replacing. That can add cost to retrofits.

## State rebates and loans on top

- **Western Australia:** the [Residential Battery Scheme](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/energy-policy-wa/wa-residential-battery-scheme-eligibility-requirements) pays $130 per kWh up to $1,300 for Synergy customers and $380 per kWh up to $3,800 for Horizon Power customers, capped at 10 kWh. You must join a VPP. No-interest loans are also available through the scheme.
- **New South Wales:** the [VPP incentive](https://www.energy.nsw.gov.au/households/grants-rebates/household-energy-saving-upgrades/connect-your-battery-virtual-power-plant) pays an upfront amount for connecting a battery to a virtual power plant, scaled to capacity up to 28 kWh. CHOICE says it is worth up to $1,500. It stacks with the federal rebate.
- **ACT:** low-interest loans for batteries; see our [ACT Sustainable Household Scheme guide](/au/guides/act-sustainable-household-loan-guide).

More detail in [state battery rebates](/au/guides/state-battery-rebates) and [virtual power plants](/au/guides/virtual-power-plants-australia).

## How long does a battery take to pay for itself?

CHOICE's examples assume an $8,500 installed cost (after the federal rebate only) and 7 kWh of overnight use:

| City | Annual saving | Simple payback |
|---|---|---|
| Adelaide | $1,350 | 6.3 years |
| Brisbane | $1,100 | 7.7 years |
| Perth | $1,120 | 7.6 years |
| Sydney | $1,030 | 8.3 years |
| Canberra | $700 | 12.1 years |
| Darwin | $620 | 13.7 years |
| Melbourne | $610 | 13.9 years |
| Hobart | $410 | 20.7 years |

CHOICE says Adelaide is quickest because of high electricity prices, and Hobart slowest because of relatively cheap electricity and higher feed-in tariffs. It also notes battery warranties are typically 10 years. A payback longer than the warranty is a warning sign; see [when a battery is not worth it](/au/guides/when-a-battery-is-not-worth-it).

## Buy now or wait?

The rebate drops every six months: the STC factor falls from 6.8 to 5.7 per kWh on 1 January 2027 and keeps falling after that. Battery prices may also fall, but nobody can promise by how much. [How much you lose by waiting](/au/guides/battery-rebate-changes-2027) works through the numbers.
