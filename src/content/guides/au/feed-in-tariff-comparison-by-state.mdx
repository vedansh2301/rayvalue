Most Australian solar exports earn between about 2 and 10 c/kWh in the middle of the day in 2026-27. The higher rates are only paid in the late afternoon and evening, and only on time-varying plans. Tasmania (9.276 c/kWh) and the NT (9.33 c/kWh anytime) still have regulated flat rates near 9 c. Victoria has had no minimum since 1 July 2025, so retailers can pay anything from 0 c up.

## Key numbers

- **NSW:** IPART's all-day benchmark for 2026-27 is **3.4 to 6.5 c/kWh**, down from 4.8 to 7.3 c/kWh in 2025-26. Evening time-of-day benchmarks are **16.9 to 33.3 c/kWh**, depending on the network.
- **Victoria:** **no minimum feed-in tariff** since 1 July 2025; the floor is 0 c/kWh (ESC).
- **Regional Queensland:** **6.006 c/kWh** for 2026-27, 31% lower than 8.660 c/kWh (QCA).
- **WA (DEBS):** **10 c/kWh** from 3 pm to 9 pm, **2 c** (Synergy) or **3 c** (Horizon Power) at other times. These are the rates as of 1 July 2024, the latest the WA government page shows.
- **Tasmania:** **9.276 c/kWh** from 1 July 2026 (Aurora Energy).
- **NT:** **9.33 c/kWh** anytime, or **18.66 c/kWh** from 3 pm to 9 pm with a smart meter (Jacana Energy, 2026-27).

## NSW: what the IPART benchmarks mean

IPART's [all-day benchmark](https://www.ipart.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/cm9_documents/Fact-Sheet-All-day-solar-feed-in-tariff-benchmark-tariff-2026-27.PDF) estimates your exports will be worth 3.4 to 6.5 c/kWh from 1 July 2026. Its [time-of-day benchmarks](https://www.ipart.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/cm9_documents/Fact-Sheet-Time-of-day-solar-feed-in-tariff-benchmarks-2026-27.PDF) split that by network:

| Network | Midday | Late afternoon | Evening peak | Overnight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ausgrid | 10 am to 3 pm: 3.9 to 4 c | 3 to 4 pm: 4.8 to 8.5 c | 4 to 9 pm: 17.2 to 18.7 c | 9 pm to 10 am: 3.2 to 4.9 c |
| Endeavour | 10 am to 2 pm: 3.1 to 3.2 c | 2 to 4 pm: 5.2 to 7.5 c | 4 to 8 pm: 16.9 to 19.9 c | 8 pm to 10 am: 3.2 to 4.8 c |
| Essential | 10 am to 3 pm: 3.3 to 4.4 c | 3 to 5 pm: 6 to 10 c | 5 to 8 pm: 26.6 to 33.3 c | 8 pm to 10 am: 3.3 to 5.3 c |

IPART says that to get the higher rates "you will need a battery that can store and discharge electricity and be on an energy plan with a feed-in tariff that varies based on the time of day". It also warns that plans with higher feed-in tariffs "may have conditions attached or be paired with higher prices".

## Victoria: no minimum since July 2025

The [Essential Services Commission](https://www.esc.vic.gov.au/electricity-and-gas/electricity-and-gas-tariffs-and-benchmarks/minimum-feed-tariff) says "there will be no minimum feed-in tariffs from 1 July 2025" and that retailers' tariffs "cannot be below zero ($0.00) cents per kWh". In practice that means shopping around matters more in Victoria than anywhere else.

## Queensland, WA, Tasmania and the NT

- **Regional Queensland.** The [QCA fact sheet](https://www.qca.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/solar-fit-fact-sheet-june-2026.pdf) sets 6.006 c/kWh for 2026-27, 31% below the 2025-26 rate of 8.660 c/kWh.
- **Western Australia.** Under the [Distributed Energy Buyback Scheme](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/energy-policy-wa/energy-buyback-schemes), exports from 3 pm to 9 pm earn 10 c/kWh. Exports from 9 pm to 3 pm earn 2 c (Synergy) or 3 c (most Horizon Power customers). The page shows rates as of 1 July 2024 and was last updated 12 December 2024.
- **Tasmania.** [Aurora Energy](https://www.auroraenergy.com.au/residential/payments/tasmanian-energy-prices) says the regulated solar feed-in tariff rose to 9.276 c/kWh from 1 July 2026, mainly due to higher loss factors and wholesale costs.
- **Northern Territory.** [Jacana Energy](https://jacanaenergy.com.au/your-home/pricing-and-plans/residential-pricing-and-tariffs) pays 9.33 c/kWh anytime. Its "Super FiT" of 18.66 c/kWh applies automatically from 3 pm to 9 pm for solar customers with smart meters.

## What this means for your solar

The pattern is the same in every state. Midday exports earn little, and evening exports and self-use earn much more. That is why using your own solar, shifting loads to the middle of the day, and in many homes a battery, now matter more than the headline feed-in tariff.
