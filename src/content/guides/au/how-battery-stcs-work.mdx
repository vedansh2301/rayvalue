The federal battery rebate is paid as small-scale technology certificates (STCs), not dollars. You take your battery's usable capacity and multiply the first 14 kWh by the factor (6.8 in 2026, 5.7 from 1 January 2027). The next 14 kWh count at 60% of the factor and the next 22 kWh at 15%. The total is rounded down to a whole number. Your installer usually turns those certificates into an upfront discount, so the dollar value depends on the STC price and fees.

## Key numbers

- **Factor:** 6.8 STCs per usable kWh for batteries certified for compliance 1 May to 31 December 2026; 5.7 from 1 January 2027; 5.2 from 1 July 2027 (regulation F2026L00093).
- **Tier 1:** 0 to 14 kWh at **100%** of the factor.
- **Tier 2:** above 14 up to 28 kWh at **60%**.
- **Tier 3:** above 28 up to 50 kWh at **15%**.
- **Rounding:** the total is rounded **down** to the nearest whole number.
- **Eligible size:** 5 to 100 kWh nominal; STCs only on the first **50 kWh usable** (CER).
- **Dollar value:** CHOICE puts it at about **$272 per kWh** for May to December 2026, before about **10%** in admin fees (secondary, 12 June 2026).

## How many STCs per kWh does a battery get?

The rule is in the method statement the 2026 amendments added to the Renewable Energy (Electricity) Regulations. The [registered text (F2026L00093)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L00093/asmade/2026-02-09/text/original/pdf) works in four steps:

1. Multiply usable capacity up to 14 kWh by the factor.
2. Multiply usable capacity above 14 kWh, up to 28 kWh, by the factor and by 0.6.
3. Multiply usable capacity above 28 kWh, up to 50 kWh, by the factor and by 0.15.
4. Add them up and round down to the nearest whole number.

The factor that applies depends on when "the battery for the unit is certified for compliance". The CER puts it more simply: "rebates are determined by the installation date, not when a contract is signed" ([CER, 18 March 2026](https://cer.gov.au/news-and-media/media/2026/march/battery-rebates-are-changing-1-may-2026)).

## Worked examples

**A 10 kWh usable battery in 2026.** 10 × 6.8 = 68 STCs. No tier 2 or 3.

**A 20 kWh usable battery in 2026.**
- Tier 1: 14 × 6.8 = 95.2
- Tier 2: 6 × 6.8 × 0.6 = 24.48
- Total 119.68, rounded down to **119 STCs**

**The same 20 kWh battery in January 2027.**
- Tier 1: 14 × 5.7 = 79.8
- Tier 2: 6 × 5.7 × 0.6 = 20.52
- Total 100.32, rounded down to **100 STCs**

**A 40 kWh usable battery in 2026.**
- Tier 1: 14 × 6.8 = 95.2
- Tier 2: 14 × 6.8 × 0.6 = 57.12
- Tier 3: 12 × 6.8 × 0.15 = 12.24
- Total 164.56, rounded down to **164 STCs**

## STCs by battery size

| Usable kWh | 2026 (6.8) | Jan to Jun 2027 (5.7) | Jul to Dec 2027 (5.2) | STCs per kWh in 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 34 | 28 | 26 | 6.8 |
| 10 | 68 | 57 | 52 | 6.8 |
| 14 | 95 | 79 | 72 | 6.8 |
| 20 | 119 | 100 | 91 | 6.0 |
| 28 | 152 | 127 | 116 | 5.4 |
| 40 | 164 | 137 | 125 | 4.1 |
| 50 | 174 | 146 | 133 | 3.5 |

The last column shows the effect of the tiers. Each extra kWh above 28 earns only about a seventh as many certificates as one of the first 14. That is deliberate. The [CER](https://cer.gov.au/news-and-media/news/2026/february/changes-to-rebate-solar-batteries-1-may) says the taper aims to keep the discount at around 30% for small, medium and large systems.

## Nominal vs usable capacity

The [CER's battery rules](https://cer.gov.au/batteries) use both:

- **Nominal capacity** (usually on the label) decides eligibility. It must be 5 to 100 kWh.
- **Usable capacity** (what you can actually draw) decides the STCs, capped at 50 kWh.

The CER's own example: a battery with 70 kWh nominal and 60 kWh usable may be eligible, but STCs are claimed on 50 kWh only. When you compare quotes, make sure each one uses usable capacity.

## From STCs to dollars

STCs are traded certificates. In most cases you assign them to your installer or an agent, who takes their value off your price. The CER's [STC entitlements page](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-technology-certificates/calculate-small-scale-technology-certificate-entitlements) explains how certificates are created. The dollar value moves with the market. CHOICE's schedule of $272 per kWh for 2026 and $228 for early 2027 implies about $40 per STC. CHOICE also says to "expect to lose 10% of your rate to admin fees". So a 10 kWh battery's 68 STCs are worth roughly $2,700 at $40, before fees.

A good quote shows:

- usable capacity in kWh
- the number of STCs
- the price per STC
- any agent or admin fee

If a quote only shows a lump "government rebate", ask for those four lines. See [battery quote red flags](/au/guides/battery-rebate-scams).
