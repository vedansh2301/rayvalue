Since 1 July 2026, households with a smart meter in NSW, south-east Queensland and South Australia can opt in to the Solar Sharer Offer (SSO). It gives you 3 hours of free electricity every day: 11 am to 2 pm in NSW and SE Queensland, 12 pm to 3 pm in South Australia, up to 24 kWh a day. The catch is that every other hour costs a little more. It pays if you can run the dishwasher, washing machine, pool pump, hot water or EV charging in that window.

## Key numbers (AER, 2026-27)

- **Start date:** **1 July 2026**, in default market offer (DMO) regions.
- **Free window:** **11 am-2 pm** (Ausgrid, Endeavour, Essential, Energex); **12 pm-3 pm** (SA Power Networks), fixed local time.
- **Daily free cap:** **24 kWh** in the free window. Usage above that pays a capped "reasonable use" rate.
- **Other hours:** usage charges **1 to 4 c/kWh higher** than the standard time-of-use DMO, depending on region.
- **Who must offer it:** retailers with more than **1,000** residential customers across DMO regions. It's opt-in and can't be the default plan.
- **Annual price at standard usage:** same as the time-of-use DMO, e.g. Ausgrid **$1,893** for 3,900 kWh, Energex **$1,914** for 4,600 kWh.

## What is the Solar Sharer Offer?

The Australian Government announced the SSO on 4 November 2025 as a new regulated tariff under the default market offer. The AER set its first prices in its [2026-27 final determination](https://www.aer.gov.au/system/files/2026-05/AER%20-%20Final%20determination%20-%20Default%20market%20offer%202026%E2%80%9327.pdf). In the AER's words, it gives "all residential customers with a smart meter the opportunity to access 3 hours of free electricity during the middle of the day," so households can benefit from Australia's midday solar "regardless of whether they have solar panels."

It is a standing offer. Retailers with more than 1,000 residential customers across DMO regions must offer it alongside their normal default plan. Because it can't be the default, you have to ask for it.

## Free electricity times by network

| Network (region) | Free window, every day | Daily free limit |
|---|---|---|
| Ausgrid (NSW) | 11 am to 2 pm | 24 kWh |
| Endeavour Energy (NSW) | 11 am to 2 pm | 24 kWh |
| Essential Energy (NSW) | 11 am to 2 pm | 24 kWh |
| Energex (SE Qld) | 11 am to 2 pm | 24 kWh |
| SA Power Networks (SA) | 12 pm to 3 pm | 24 kWh |

The AER says the 24 kWh cap is "roughly equivalent to a 5-person household shifting its total daily usage into the free hours." Above it, usage is charged at a rate based on the SSO's off-peak or solar soak rate.

## Why the other 21 hours cost more

The free hours aren't free to supply. The AER moved the cost of the free window onto the other periods of the day, by volume. It also assumed that customers won't shift any usage, because it had no data on how people would respond. The result: usage outside the free window is 1 to 4 c/kWh dearer than the standard time-of-use default offer.

That design means a household that does nothing different pays about the same. The AER's annual prices for 2026-27 show it:

| Network | Annual usage assumed | Time-of-use DMO | Solar Sharer DMO |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ausgrid | 3,900 kWh | $1,893 | $1,893 |
| Endeavour | 4,900 kWh | $2,320 | $2,320 |
| Essential | 4,600 kWh | $2,530 | $2,530 |
| Energex | 4,600 kWh | $1,914 | $1,914 |
| SA Power Networks | 4,000 kWh | $2,276 | $2,276 |

Source: [AER information kit](https://www.aer.gov.au/system/files/2026-05/Information%20Kit%20-%20Default%20Market%20Offer%20-%202026-27_0.pdf). These are maximum standing-offer prices including GST. Market offers can be cheaper.

The saving comes only from what you move into the free window. Every kWh shifted from the evening peak into 11 am-2 pm stops costing you the peak rate.

## Who should switch to Solar Sharer?

**Good fit:**

- Someone is home in the middle of the day, or appliances can run on timers.
- You have an EV that's parked at home at midday, or a heat pump or electric hot water system on a timer.
- You don't have solar, or your system is small, so midday power still comes from the grid.

**Poor fit:**

- The house is empty all day and most use is in the evening.
- You already have a large solar system covering your midday use. Your panels already make that power free, so the free window adds little while the other hours cost more.

## Solar Sharer if you already have solar or a battery

If you have solar, the SSO mostly matters for the hours when your panels can't cover your load. A battery can help on both sides: charge from the grid for free in the window on cloudy days (within the 24 kWh cap), then run the house through the evening. Whether that's allowed or worthwhile depends on your retailer's plan and your battery settings, so ask before you switch. Our [battery size guide](/au/guides/solar-battery-size-guide) explains how to size for overnight use.

The SSO also changes the value of new panels. If midday grid power is free, solar that only displaces midday use saves less. See [when solar doesn't pay off](/au/guides/when-solar-doesnt-pay-off-australia) and [feed-in tariffs by state](/au/guides/feed-in-tariff-comparison-by-state).

## How to get it

1. Check that you have a smart meter (your bill or retailer can confirm).
2. Ask your retailer for its Solar Sharer standing offer, and compare it with its market offers.
3. Look at your half-hourly data to see how much you could move into the free window.
4. Set timers for the dishwasher, laundry, pool pump, hot water and EV.
