Eskom's direct customers have paid 8.76% more since 1 April 2026, and NERSA has approved another 8.83% from April 2027. Municipalities pay Eskom 9.01% more for bulk power from 1 July 2026, and most pass a similar rise to households. For solar owners, each kWh your panels replace is now worth more. But part of the increase has moved into fixed daily charges that solar can't reduce.

## Key numbers

- **8.76%**: Eskom direct-customer increase from **1 April 2026**, including Homelight, Homepower and Homeflex ([Eskom](https://www.eskom.co.za/distribution/2026-2027-tariff-increase/)).
- **9.01%**: increase in Eskom's municipal (bulk) tariffs from **1 July 2026** (Eskom).
- **8.83%**: approved increase for **2027/28**, from NERSA's February 2026 redetermination ([SAnews](https://www.sanews.gov.za/node/79497)).
- **5.36% and 6.19%**: what NERSA had originally set for 2026/27 and 2027/28, before the redetermination (SAnews).
- **66.66%** (up from 33.33%): the fixed share of the Homepower and Homeflex service and administration charge (Eskom).
- **322.06 c/kWh** including VAT: Homepower energy charge from 1 April 2026 ([Eskom booklet](https://www.eskom.co.za/distribution/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Eskom-Tariff-booklet_Final.pdf)).

## Why were the increases higher than first announced?

NERSA originally set 5.36% for 2026/27 and 6.19% for 2027/28. A High Court judgment in December remitted part of NERSA's decision on Eskom's generation asset base. NERSA's redetermination raised the increases to 8.76% and 8.83% (SAnews, 9 February 2026). Eskom's published NERSA letter explains the mechanism: R12,000 million is recovered in 2026/27 and R23,013 million in 2027/28, with the rest recovered after the MYPD6 period. That adds 3.4% and 2.64% to the two years.

Together, the two increases compound to about 18.4% over two years (1.0876 x 1.0883).

## What changed inside the tariffs?

The headline percentage is an average. Eskom also changed how costs are recovered:

- **Generation capacity charge (GCC):** raised to 30% of the reference rand value, up from 20%, with energy rates lowered to compensate.
- **Homepower and Homeflex service and administration charge:** the fixed portion rose to 66.66% of the reference value, up from 33.33%. Energy rates were reduced so that the total average increase stays at 8.76%.

For a household, this means more of the bill is fixed and paid every day, and less depends on kWh. On Homepower the daily charges include a service and administration charge of R6.60, a network capacity charge (R15.17 a day on Homepower 1) and a generation capacity charge (R1.25 a day on Homepower 1), all including VAT (Eskom booklet 2026/27).

## What happened to municipal tariffs?

Most South Africans buy from their municipality, not Eskom. Each metro sets its own increase after the 9.01% bulk rise:

| Metro | 2026/27 change | Source |
|---|---|---|
| City of Cape Town (Home User) | 5.9% to 6.4% on the City's Home User example bills | City of Cape Town |
| eThekwini (Durban) | 9% average | eThekwini tariff booklet 2026/27 |
| City Power (Johannesburg) | 8.63% headline | City of Johannesburg tariff sheet |

Cape Town's Home User tariff now charges 355.95 c/kWh up to 600 kWh and 469.06 c/kWh above that, including VAT, plus a Services and Wires Charge of R424.30 a month (City of Cape Town). eThekwini's residential energy charge is 357.47 c/kWh before VAT, or 411.09 c/kWh with VAT (eThekwini).

## What do the increases mean for solar?

Two effects pull in opposite directions.

**Energy savings grow.** A kWh your panels produce and you use replaces a kWh at the current rate. In Johannesburg, PVGIS models about 1,720 kWh a year per kW of panels facing north at 30 degrees ([PVGIS](https://re.jrc.ec.europa.eu/api/v5_3/PVcalc?lat=-26.204&lon=28.047&peakpower=1&loss=14&angle=30&aspect=180&outputformat=json)). At Eskom's Homepower rate of 322.06 c/kWh, that is about R5,539 a year per kW of panels, if all of it is used at home. Every future increase raises that figure.

**Fixed charges don't shrink.** Service, network and capacity charges are paid daily whatever your kWh. Shifting the Homepower and Homeflex service charge further into the fixed part means solar offsets a slightly smaller share of the bill than before. Read [Eskom Homeflex explained](/za/guides/eskom-homeflex-explained) for how this works on the solar tariff.

**Grid-tied solar on Eskom supply needs a time-of-use tariff.** The Eskom booklet says a TOU tariff is mandatory for grid-tied generation, which for homes means Homeflex.

## What should you do now?

1. **Find your supplier.** Eskom direct or municipal. The rates and the export credit differ.
2. **Split your bill** into fixed charges (R per day or per month) and energy (c/kWh). Solar only reduces the energy part.
3. **Use the energy rate you actually pay** when checking a solar quote, and remember it rises roughly every year.
4. **Check the export rules** before sizing for export. See [solar feed-in tariffs by metro](/za/guides/solar-feed-in-tariffs-cape-town-johannesburg-durban).

For whether the case still holds now that load-shedding has stopped, read [is solar worth it in South Africa without load-shedding](/za/blog/is-solar-worth-it-south-africa-without-load-shedding).
