Each metro sets its own export credit, and the rules differ as much as the rates. For 2026/27, Cape Town credits residential exports at 107.98 c/kWh plus a 25 c/kWh incentive, both before VAT. eThekwini (Durban) credits 160.52 c/kWh before VAT but charges a monthly fee per kVA of inverter. We could not find City Power's (Johannesburg) 2026/27 export rate in a primary document, so we don't quote one. In every case you earn much less for an exported kWh than you pay for one.

## Key numbers

- **Cape Town:** **107.98 c/kWh** before VAT (124.18 c with VAT) residential SSEG feed-in, plus a **25.00 c/kWh** incentive (28.75 c with VAT), 2026/27 ([City of Cape Town Annexure 6](https://www.capetown.gov.za/_documents/resource.capetown.gov.za/documentcentre/Documents/Financial%20documents/Budget-2026-27_Ann06-25-ElectricityGenerationandDistribution-ConsumptionandGeneration.pdf)).
- **eThekwini:** **160.52 c/kWh** before VAT feed-in credit and a **R76.83 per kVA per month** Network Access Charge, 2026/27 ([eThekwini tariffs 2026/2027](https://durban.gov.za/uploads/0000/17/2026/10/06/tariffs-and-charges-2026-2027.pdf)).
- **City Power (Johannesburg):** not confirmed in a primary source as of 22 November 2026.
- **Eskom direct (Homeflex Gen-offset):** credits of **164.11 to 813.64 c/kWh** including VAT depending on season and time of day, capped at your consumption in each period (Eskom booklet 2026/27).

Before VAT, an exported kWh is worth about 43% of what you pay per kWh in Cape Town (132.98 c against the Home User rate of 309.52 c up to 600 kWh) and about 45% in eThekwini (160.52 c against 357.47 c). Using your own solar power is worth more than twice as much as exporting it.

## Cape Town: how does the feed-in work?

The City's 2026/27 Annexure 6 sets the residential SSEG feed-in tariff at 107.98 c/kWh before VAT, up 6.67% from 101.23 c. It is available only to approved residential SSEG connections on the **Home User** tariff, and an AMI administration fee applies. The City keeps the "green attributes" of the exported power. A separate **SSEG feed-in incentive** of 25.00 c/kWh applies to all feed-in tariffs and is reviewed annually by Council.

The City's application page sets out the steps. Install a grid-tied system with a City-approved inverter, and note that installers must apply to the City for authorisation before installing. The City fits an AMI meter, you move to or stay on Home User, and exports are credited to your monthly municipal account. Credits can offset your whole municipal account, not only electricity. If credits regularly cover the whole account, the **Cash for Power** programme pays residential customers once a year when the remaining credit exceeds R1,000.

Cape Town also lists an SSEG time-of-use feed-in tariff, but marks it as available only once back-end systems are in place.

## eThekwini (Durban): how does net billing work?

eThekwini's 2026/27 tariff booklet sets the Residential Net Billing Tariff:

- **Feed-in energy rate:** 160.52 c/kWh before VAT, credited to your account. In 2025/26 it was 147.46 c.
- **Network Access Charge:** R76.83 per kVA of inverter size per month before VAT (R70.07 in 2025/26). The municipality says it recovers fixed grid costs that a lower kWh purchase would otherwise leave unpaid.
- **Grid power** is still charged on the normal Scale 3 or 4 residential rates.

The NAC matters. A 5 kVA inverter costs 5 x R76.83 = R384.15 a month before VAT, whatever you export. At 160.52 c/kWh, you need to export about 239 kWh a month just to cover it. Size the inverter to your needs, not the installer's upsell.

The booklet also says credits are not redeemable for cash. Unregistered systems are treated as unauthorised and may have credits reversed.

## City Power (Johannesburg): why no figure?

City Power's 2026/27 tariff sheet on the City of Johannesburg site shows the 8.63% headline increase and residential prepaid block rates, but no SSEG export rate. Neither does the combined tariff PDF we opened. Some websites quote a figure, but we couldn't trace it to a City Power or City of Johannesburg document, so we leave it out. If you are a City Power customer, ask City Power for the SSEG tariff schedule in writing before sizing a system for export.

## Eskom direct customers

If Eskom supplies you directly, the Homeflex Gen-offset credit replaces a municipal feed-in rate. Credits are paid per time-of-use period and capped at what you consume in that period, from 164.11 c/kWh off-peak to 813.64 c/kWh at high-season peak, including VAT. Read [Eskom Homeflex explained](/za/guides/eskom-homeflex-explained).

## What this means for sizing

- **Size for self-use first.** With export worth about 43% to 45% of the energy price before VAT in Cape Town and Durban, kWh used at home do most of the work.
- **Count fixed costs of exporting:** the AMI meter and fee in Cape Town, the per-kVA NAC in Durban.
- **Register first.** Unregistered systems risk penalties and reversed credits (eThekwini). See [solar installer red flags and COC rules](/za/blog/solar-installer-red-flags-coc-rules).
