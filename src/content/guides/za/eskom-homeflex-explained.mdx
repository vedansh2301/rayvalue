Homeflex is Eskom's time-of-use (TOU) tariff for homes. If Eskom supplies you directly and you want grid-tied solar, it is the tariff you must use. Electricity costs more in the evening peak and in winter (June to August), and less off-peak. Exports earn a "Gen-offset" credit per TOU period, but only up to what you used in that same period. Homeflex rewards a home that uses its solar by day and avoids the winter evening peak, which is exactly what a battery does.

## Key numbers

All figures include 15% VAT, from 1 April 2026 (Eskom Tariffs and Charges Booklet 2026/27, unless stated).

- **847.10 c/kWh**: high-demand season (June to August) peak energy rate.
- **190.83 c/kWh**: off-peak energy rate, all year.
- **813.64 c/kWh**: Gen-offset credit for exports at high-season peak, against **164.11 c** off-peak.
- **R6.60 a day**: service and administration charge.
- **8.76%**: the 2026/27 average increase for Homeflex and Gen-offset Homeflex ([Eskom](https://www.eskom.co.za/distribution/2026-2027-tariff-increase/)).
- **Mandatory**: a TOU tariff is required for grid-tied generation on Eskom supply (booklet footnote).

## What are the Homeflex rates for 2026/27?

| Period | High-demand season (Jun to Aug) | Low-demand season (Sep to May) |
|---|---|---|
| Peak | 847.10 c/kWh | 394.55 c/kWh |
| Standard | 259.19 c/kWh | 245.51 c/kWh |
| Off-peak | 190.83 c/kWh | 190.83 c/kWh |

Source: Eskom booklet 2026/27, Homeflex non-local authority, including VAT. For comparison, the flat Homepower energy rate is 322.06 c/kWh including VAT.

On top of the energy rate, Homeflex adds several small per-kWh charges (network demand, ancillary service and legacy charges) and three daily charges:

| Daily charge (incl. VAT) | Homeflex 1 | Homeflex 4 |
|---|---|---|
| Service and administration | R6.60 | R6.60 |
| Network capacity | R15.17 | R10.44 |
| Generation capacity | R1.25 | R0.82 |
| Total per day | R23.02 | R17.86 |
| About per 30-day month | R690.60 | R535.80 |

Homeflex 4 is the single-phase 16 kVA (80 A) option. Homeflex 1 to 3 cover larger supplies, such as 32 kVA dual-phase or 25 kVA three-phase for Homeflex 1 (booklet p.28). You pay these daily charges whether or not you use any power. Eskom says it raised the fixed share of the service and administration charge on Homepower and Homeflex to 66.66% of the reference value, from 33.33%, and lowered energy rates to compensate.

## When are peak hours?

Eskom's booklet (Appendix A) shows the Homeflex periods as a chart rather than a table. For 2026/27 it notes that the evening peak grew from 2 to 3 hours, the morning peak shrank from 3 to 2 hours, and a new 2-hour standard period was added on Sunday evenings. Check the chart for exact hours before you program a battery or timer.

## How does the Gen-offset export credit work?

The booklet describes Homeflex as allowing net billing: at the end of each month you get a credit for energy exported, **up to and equal to your consumption in each TOU period**. The credit rate excludes part of the generation capacity charge and the service and administration charge built into the energy rate.

| Gen-offset credit (incl. VAT) | High-demand season | Low-demand season |
|---|---|---|
| Peak | 813.64 c/kWh | 366.15 c/kWh |
| Standard | 231.90 c/kWh | 218.35 c/kWh |
| Off-peak | 164.11 c/kWh | 164.11 c/kWh |

Source: Eskom booklet 2026/27, Gen-offset Homeflex non-local authority.

The cap matters most. Solar exports happen mainly in standard periods during the day. If you export 300 kWh in standard periods but only buy 200 kWh in standard periods that month, only 200 kWh earn a credit. The other 100 kWh earn nothing. So Homeflex doesn't pay you to oversize. It pays you for exporting roughly as much as you import in each period.

## How do you make Homeflex work with solar?

1. **Use solar by day.** Run the pool pump, geyser timer, dishwasher and washing machine in daylight standard hours.
2. **Avoid winter peak.** At 847.10 c/kWh, every kWh you avoid at peak from June to August is worth about 4.4 times an off-peak kWh (190.83 c). This is the strongest case for a battery on Eskom supply.
3. **Match exports to imports per period.** Credits beyond your own consumption in that period are lost.
4. **Size panels to your use.** In Pretoria, PVGIS models about 1,739 kWh a year per kW of panels facing north at 30 degrees ([PVGIS](https://re.jrc.ec.europa.eu/api/v5_3/PVcalc?lat=-25.746&lon=28.188&peakpower=1&loss=14&angle=30&aspect=180&outputformat=json)). Compare that with your yearly kWh before choosing system size.

## Is Homeflex better or worse than Homepower?

That depends on when you use power. Homeflex off-peak (190.83 c) and standard (245.51 to 259.19 c) are below Homepower's 322.06 c, but peak is far above it. A home that runs on solar and battery through the evening can come out ahead. A home with heavy winter evening use and no battery can pay more. If you install grid-tied solar on Eskom supply, the choice is made for you: the booklet says a TOU tariff is mandatory.

## Registration first

Eskom says national rules require SSEG systems under 100 kW connected to the network to be registered with the supplier. Eskom waived registration charges for systems up to 50 kW until 30 September 2026 and is testing a prepaid option for residential solar customers (Eskom statement, 26 March 2026). Register before you claim Gen-offset credits.
