Now that load-shedding has stopped, a battery usually makes more sense than a generator for a typical home. A battery can save money every day, especially on Eskom's Homeflex time-of-use tariff, and it switches over silently if the grid fails. A generator only earns its keep during outages, burns fuel while it runs, and sits idle when the grid is stable. A generator still wins for long outages, big loads and tight budgets. Either way, wiring into your home's fixed installation needs a registered electrician and a certificate of compliance.

## Key numbers

- **504 days** without load-shedding since **16 May 2025**, as reported in early October 2026 ([SAnews](https://www.sanews.gov.za/node/84735)).
- **847.10 c/kWh** at Homeflex high-season peak against **190.83 c/kWh** off-peak, including VAT, 2026/27 ([Eskom booklet](https://www.eskom.co.za/distribution/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Eskom-Tariff-booklet_Final.pdf)).
- **394.55 c/kWh** at Homeflex low-season peak (September to May), including VAT (Eskom booklet).
- **Certificate of compliance** required for any addition or alteration to a home's electrical installation (Electrical Installation Regulations, 2009, regulation 7).
- **Registration** required for grid-connected SSEG systems under 100 kW (Eskom, March 2026).

## How do they compare?

| | Battery (with inverter) | Generator |
|---|---|---|
| Switch-over in an outage | Automatic, usually seamless | Manual or via an automatic transfer switch, with a gap |
| Running cost | None to discharge; recharges from solar or cheap grid power | Fuel for every hour it runs, plus servicing |
| Earns money when grid is up | Yes, on a TOU tariff or with solar | No |
| Noise and fumes | None | Yes; must run outdoors |
| Long outages (days) | Limited to stored kWh unless solar recharges it | Runs as long as there is fuel |
| Large loads (geyser, oven, borehole pump) | Limited by inverter size | Handles larger loads if sized for them |
| Wiring into fixed installation | Needs a registered person and COC | Needs a registered person and COC |

## Can a battery save money without load-shedding?

Yes, where the tariff rewards moving power in time. On Eskom's Homeflex tariff (2026/27, including VAT):

| Season | Peak | Off-peak | Saving per kWh moved from peak to off-peak |
|---|---|---|---|
| High demand (Jun to Aug) | 847.10 c | 190.83 c | 656.27 c |
| Low demand (Sep to May) | 394.55 c | 190.83 c | 203.72 c |

A battery charged from solar or off-peak power and used during the evening peak captures most of that gap, less losses in the battery. Homeflex is mandatory for grid-tied generation on Eskom supply, so many solar homes on Eskom are already on it. Read [Eskom Homeflex explained](/za/guides/eskom-homeflex-explained).

On a flat municipal tariff, the battery's job is different. It stores midday solar you would otherwise export at a lower credit, such as Cape Town's 107.98 c plus 25 c incentive before VAT, and uses it in the evening at the full rate. Cape Town also offers an optional Residential TOU tariff for homes with an AMI meter. The City says it may suit customers with a battery that stores electricity for peak periods (City of Cape Town).

A generator saves nothing on a normal day. Its value is purely insurance.

## When does a generator still make sense?

- **Long outages:** local faults, cable theft or storms that last days. A battery without solar runs flat. A generator runs as long as there is fuel.
- **Big loads:** a borehole pump, welding or a whole-house geyser. These may exceed a home inverter's rating.
- **Small budget:** if you want only occasional backup and no savings, a generator is usually the cheaper purchase. We haven't sourced South African battery or generator prices, so compare quotes yourself.

Some homes combine the two: a battery for daily savings and short outages, and a small generator for rare long ones.

## What are the legal rules for wiring?

The Electrical Installation Regulations, 2009 apply to both:

- **Regulation 7:** every user or lessor of an electrical installation must have a valid certificate of compliance (COC) with a test report. After any addition or alteration, they need a COC covering at least that addition or alteration.
- **Regulation 9:** only a registered person may issue a COC, and only after inspecting and testing the work.
- **Regulation 2:** the user or lessor is responsible for the safety, safe use and maintenance of the installation.

A battery inverter or a generator changeover wired into your distribution board is an alteration. Ask for the COC and test report.

If the battery system is grid-tied, it may count as small-scale embedded generation. Eskom says national rules require SSEG systems under 100 kW to be registered with Eskom or the municipality (Eskom, March 2026). eThekwini says unregistered generation installations are treated as unauthorised and may be penalised (eThekwini tariffs 2026/2027).

## Which should you choose?

- **Choose a battery** if you are on Homeflex or a TOU tariff, have or plan solar, and want silent backup for short outages.
- **Choose a generator** if you need multi-day backup or large loads and don't care about daily savings.
- **Choose both** if your area has long local outages and you also want TOU savings.
