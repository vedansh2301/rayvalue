California homeowners who finish a solar system before January 1, 2027 can still have it excluded from property-tax reassessment. After that date the exclusion stops for new systems, unless the Legislature changes the law again. If you are close to signing, the deadline is now about six weeks away, so the main job is to confirm with your county assessor what "qualify before January 1, 2027" means for your project.

## Key numbers

- **January 1, 2027:** the date the section 73 new construction exclusion becomes inoperative (BOE Letter To Assessors 2026/034, dated September 1, 2026).
- **1980:** the year voters approved Proposition 7, which let the Legislature exclude active solar systems from new-construction assessment (same letter).
- **3 years:** from January 1, 2027, the first buyer of a new home with solar must file form BOE-64-SES within three years of purchase for the claim to be timely (AB 1516, per the same letter).
- **$0:** the federal residential solar credit (Section 25D) for systems placed in service after December 31, 2025, per the IRS.

## What is the California solar property-tax exclusion?

Normally, when you add something to your home, the county assessor can add its value to your assessment. Section 73 of the Revenue and Taxation Code, as reproduced in the BOE letter, says "newly constructed" does not include "the construction or addition of any active solar energy system." For systems that make electricity, the section says that includes storage devices, power conditioning equipment and transfer equipment.

In practice, a rooftop system that qualifies does not raise your assessed value. That keeps your annual property tax from going up because you added solar.

## When does the exclusion end?

The State Board of Equalization (BOE) told county assessors on [September 1, 2026](https://www.boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/pdf/lta26034.pdf) that Senate Bill 710 (Stats. 2025, ch. 328) made a technical change so the exclusion "will become inoperative, rather than repealed, on January 1, 2027."

The amended text enclosed with the letter says systems that qualify for an exclusion before January 1, 2027 continue to be excluded on and after that date, until there is a later change in ownership.

| Your situation | What the BOE letter and section 73 say |
|---|---|
| Solar already installed and excluded | Stays excluded until the property changes ownership |
| System that qualifies before January 1, 2027 | Continues to be excluded after that date, until a change in ownership |
| New system that does not qualify before January 1, 2027 | Not covered by the exclusion unless the law is changed |
| Buying a newly built home with solar | From January 1, 2027, file BOE-64-SES within 3 years of purchase to be timely |
| Buying a resale home with solar | The seller's exclusion lasts until the change in ownership |

## What should you do before January 1, 2027?

1. **Ask your county assessor one direct question.** "If my system is completed on [date], will it qualify for the section 73 exclusion?" Get the answer in writing or note the name and date. We did not find BOE guidance on how a system finished in the last weeks of 2026 is handled, so do not rely on a sales rep's view.
2. **Check your installer's real schedule.** Permits, inspection and utility approval all take time. Ask for the date the installation will be complete, not the date the panels arrive.
3. **Get the completion date into the contract.** If the timing matters to you, ask what happens if the job slips into 2027.
4. **Do not let the deadline rush a bad deal.** A property-tax exclusion is worth something only if the system itself makes sense. Run your numbers in the [California calculator](/us/solar-payback/california) first. It applies no federal credit.
5. **Keep your paperwork.** Save the permit, final inspection record and the utility's permission to operate.

## What changes for people buying a new home with solar?

Since 2008, the exclusion could pass to the first buyer of a new building that came with a solar system, if the buyer filed a claim. The BOE letter says AB 1516 (Stats. 2025, ch. 72) adds a filing deadline from January 1, 2027: the Initial Purchaser Claim (BOE-64-SES) must be filed within three years of the purchase date to be timely. A late claim gets relief only from the lien date of the year the valid claim is filed.

If you buy a new-build home with solar, ask the builder and the county assessor which form applies and file promptly.

## What does this mean for payback?

The exclusion does not cut what you pay for the system. It stops your annual property tax from rising because of it. After January 1, 2027, a new system that does not qualify could add to your assessed value, which would add an ongoing cost that works against your savings. How much depends on the value the assessor assigns and your county's tax rate, so ask the assessor how a system like yours would be valued.

The bigger drivers of payback in California are your utility's rates and the export rules.
