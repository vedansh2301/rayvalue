A typical home battery stores about 13.5 kWh. At the average Florida household's 2024 usage, that is roughly 9 hours of normal use, and one to several days if you run only essentials such as the fridge, lights, fans, Wi-Fi and phone charging. Paired with solar panels that can recharge it each sunny day, a battery can keep essentials running through a multi-day outage. Without solar, it runs down and stays down.

## Key numbers

- **1,104 kWh a month:** average Florida residential use in 2024, about 36 kWh a day (EIA Table 5A).
- **13.5 kWh and 5 kW:** the battery size and inverter power in the U.S. DOE's 2025 Q1 residential solar-plus-storage benchmark.
- **1,321.5 minutes:** average outage time per Florida customer in 2024, all events included, about 22 hours (EIA Electric Power Annual Table 11.3). In 2025 it was 70.5 minutes.
- **3.4 million:** Florida customers left without power by Hurricane Milton in October 2024 (EIA, December 1, 2025).
- **$2.1 per watt:** how much higher the median price of paired solar-plus-storage was than stand-alone solar in 2025 installs (Berkeley Lab, August 2026).

## How long does a home battery last in a power outage?

It depends on two things: how much energy the battery holds (kWh) and how much power your home draws while it runs (kW). Here is simple arithmetic using public figures. It is not a promise for any one home.

| What you run | Daily use (illustrative) | Hours from a full 13.5 kWh battery |
|---|---|---|
| Whole home at the 2024 Florida average | about 36 kWh a day (1,104 kWh ÷ 30.4 days) | about 9 hours |
| Half of average use (AC set higher, no pool pump) | about 18 kWh a day | about 18 hours |
| Essentials only (fridge, lights, fans, internet, chargers) | depends on your appliances; often a small fraction of average use | one day or more |

Two cautions. First, the battery's power limit matters too. The DOE benchmark battery has a 5 kW inverter. A central air conditioner, well pump or electric range can push a home past that, so the installer may back up only selected circuits. Second, batteries usually keep some charge in reserve, so usable energy can be a little less than the label.

## What can a battery run during a Florida hurricane outage?

Most backup installs use a "critical loads" panel. The circuits you choose stay on and the rest go dark. Common choices:

- Refrigerator and freezer
- Lights and ceiling fans
- Internet router and phone chargers
- Medical devices (talk to your provider about a plan, as Ready.gov advises)
- Garage door opener and well pump, if you have them

Whole-home backup is possible with more batteries or larger inverters, but it costs more. Ask for the list of backed-up circuits in writing.

## Does solar recharge the battery after a storm?

Yes, if the system is designed for it. A battery paired with solar can refill on sunny days after the storm passes, which is what turns 9 hours into many days for essentials. A battery without solar cannot refill until the grid returns. A solar system without a battery may not run your home at all during an outage, so ask the installer to confirm in writing what works when the grid is down.

Debris on panels, roof damage or a flooded equipment area can stop recharging, so ask the installer where the battery and inverter will sit relative to flood risk.

## How often does Florida lose power?

The outage data swings with hurricane seasons. EIA's Table 11.3 shows the average Florida customer was without power for 1,321.5 minutes in 2024, counting major events, compared with 70.5 minutes in 2025. Without major event days, the figures were 66.4 and 63.4 minutes. In other words, normal years are short outages and storm years are long ones.

EIA reported that U.S. customers averaged 11 hours without power in 2024, and that major events including Hurricanes Beryl, Helene and Milton caused 80% of those hours. Milton alone left 3.4 million Florida customers without power.

## Is a battery worth it in Florida?

For bill savings alone, usually not. Florida's large utilities use net metering, so solar exports earn kWh credits without a battery. See [is solar worth it in Florida without the federal tax credit](/guides/florida-solar-without-federal-tax-credit). The case for a battery is resilience: keeping food cold, staying cool enough to sleep, and running medical equipment when the grid is down for days.

Batteries add real cost. Berkeley Lab found paired solar-plus-storage was $2.1 per watt more than stand-alone solar at the median in 2025 installs, with most data from California. The federal 25D credit no longer applies to 2026 installs.
