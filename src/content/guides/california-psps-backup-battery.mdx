California's big utilities can cut power to areas at high wildfire risk during dangerous wind. PG&E aims to warn customers about a day or two before these Public Safety Power Shutoffs (PSPS) and to restore power within 24 hours of the weather clearing. A home battery, especially one that can recharge from your solar, is the quietest way to keep the fridge, lights, internet and medical devices running through one.

## Key numbers

- **Legal basis:** in **2012** the CPUC ruled that state law lets the investor-owned utilities shut off power to protect public safety (CPUC).
- **Notice:** PG&E's guide lists a **PSPS Watch 48 to 24 hours** before power is turned off; some priority notices go out **48 to 72 hours** ahead (PG&E guide, November 2025).
- **Restoration goal:** PG&E aims to restore power **within 24 hours** after severe weather has passed.
- **A 2026 example:** in the **June 10-11, 2026** PSPS, PG&E de-energized **4,256 customers** in eight counties, restored 100% within 24 hours of the weather all-clear, and reported an average restoration time of about **3.3 hours** (PG&E post-event report).
- **Battery sizes:** one Powerwall 3 is **13.5 kWh** and up to **11.5 kW**; one Enphase IQ Battery 5P is **5.0 kWh** and **3.84 kVA** (2025 datasheets).
- **Federal 25D credit:** **$0** for batteries installed in 2026 or later.

## What is a PSPS?

The CPUC explains that utilities "may temporarily turn off power to specific areas to reduce the risk of fires caused by electric infrastructure." Its 2012 ruling says Public Utilities Code sections 451 and 399.2(a) give the investor-owned utilities, including PG&E, SCE and SDG&E, the authority to do this ([CPUC PSPS page](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/psps/)). The CPUC has since added rules on notification, community resource centers, medical baseline customers and reporting, and utilities must file a report after each event.

You can check whether your address has been affected by a PSPS since October 2021 on the CPUC's public PSPS dashboard, linked from the same page.

## How much warning will you get?

PG&E's [November 2025 guide](https://www.pge.com/assets/pge/docs/outages-and-safety/outage-preparedness-and-support/your-guide-to-public-safety-power-shutoffs.pdf) lays out the sequence:

| When | What PG&E sends |
|---|---|
| 48-72 hours ahead | Early notices to some partners and priority customers |
| 48-24 hours ahead | PSPS Watch to customers |
| 1-4 hours ahead | Imminent shutoff notice (per PG&E's 2026 post-event report timeline) |
| During | Daily updates with estimated restoration |
| After | Notice that power has been restored |

PG&E's post-event reports show that fast-changing weather can shorten that window. Treat the 48-hour target as best case. A battery that stays charged and switches over automatically doesn't need the warning at all.

## How long do PSPS outages last?

After the wind passes, crews patrol and inspect the lines before turning power back on. PG&E says its "goal is to restore power within 24 hours after severe weather has passed." The June 10-11, 2026 event shows a smaller, faster case: 4,256 customers de-energized across eight counties, all restored within 24 hours of the all-clear, with an average restoration time of about 3.3 hours. The same report says mitigation kept roughly 28,539 customers in the final scope from being shut off.

The total outage includes the windy period too. Plan for at least a day without grid power, and for longer during a multi-day wind event.

## How big a battery do you need for a PSPS?

Start from what you must keep running for about a day:

1. **List must-run loads:** fridge, lights, internet, phone charging, medical devices, a well pump if you have one.
2. **Estimate daily kWh** from appliance labels or your utility's hourly usage data.
3. **Check power (kW)**, not just energy. A well pump or AC starting up needs a burst of power.
4. **Plan for recharging.** With solar that keeps working in backup mode, the battery refills during the day. Without it, the battery's kWh is all you have.

Two reference points from the datasheets: one [Powerwall 3](https://energylibrary.tesla.com/docs/Public/EnergyStorage/Powerwall/3/Datasheet/en-us/Powerwall-3-Datasheet.pdf) holds 13.5 kWh and is rated up to 11.5 kW continuous, and its datasheet lists an off-grid rating when running on solar. One [IQ Battery 5P](https://enphase.com/download/iq-battery-5p-data-sheet) holds 5.0 kWh at 3.84 kVA, and systems often stack several. Our [battery comparison guide](/guides/powerwall-vs-enphase-battery-comparison) covers what else to check.

A battery used only for backup earns no bill savings. Under California's net billing, the same battery can also shift solar into the expensive evening hours, which is where the payback comes from. See [California NEM 3.0 and batteries](/guides/california-nem-3-batteries).

## Rebates and programs for PSPS backup power

- **SGIP storage rebates (CPUC).** The CPUC says SGIP funding prioritizes communities in high fire-threat areas, communities that have had **two or more** PSPS events, and low-income and medically vulnerable customers ([SGIP participation page](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/industries-and-topics/electrical-energy/demand-side-management/self-generation-incentive-program/participating-in-self-generation-incentive-program-sgip)). Separately, the CPUC has authorized **$280 million** for SGIP's Residential Solar and Storage Equity budget for low-income customers, open for reservations from June 2, 2025 ([SGIP](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/industries-and-topics/electrical-energy/demand-side-management/self-generation-incentive-program)). Budgets open and close, so check status with an installer or SGIP administrator.
- **PG&E Generator and Battery Rebate Program.** The November 2025 guide offered **$300** off a qualifying generator or battery, plus **$200** for CARE or FERA customers, for customers in High Fire-Threat District Tier 2/3 or on an EPSS circuit. It set an application deadline of 12 months from purchase or December 31, 2025, whichever came first. Check pge.com/gbrp for any 2026 version.
- **PG&E Portable Battery Program.** For customers who rely on medical devices, PG&E lists no-cost portable backup batteries ([fact sheet](https://www.pge.com/assets/pge/docs/outages-and-safety/outage-preparedness-and-support/psps-customer-resources-fact-sheet.pdf)).
- **Medical Baseline.** PG&E's fact sheet points customers who rely on powered medical equipment to its Medical Baseline Program.
- **Backup Power Transfer Meter.** For generator owners in high fire-threat or EPSS areas, PG&E installs a meter that switches to generator power and back automatically.

There is no federal tax credit for batteries installed in 2026 or later.

## Battery or generator?

A generator runs as long as you have fuel, and a battery runs silently with no fuel to store but is limited by its kWh. Solar plus a backup-capable battery can recharge on sunny days. The trade-offs are the same ones Florida homeowners weigh in hurricane season: see [battery vs generator](/guides/generator-vs-battery-florida).

To see whether a battery also pays for itself on your bill, run the [California solar and battery calculator](/us/solar-payback/california) with your utility: [PG&E](/us/utility/california/pge), [SCE](/us/utility/california/sce) or [SDG&E](/us/utility/california/sdge).
