No, a Florida HOA cannot ban solar panels. Section 163.04 of the Florida Statutes says a deed restriction, covenant or similar agreement may not prohibit solar collectors, and a property owner may not be denied permission to install them. Your HOA can choose where on the roof they go, but only within limits that protect how well they work.

## Key numbers

- **45 degrees:** the HOA may set the roof location, as long as panels can face south or within 45 degrees east or west of due south (s. 163.04).
- **2026:** the statute text we checked is from the 2026 Florida Statutes.
- **0 to 10 kW:** FPL's Tier 1 net metering size range, based on the AC gross power rating (FPL net metering FAQ).

## What does Florida's solar access law say?

Section 163.04 has four parts that matter to homeowners:

| Part | What it says (paraphrased) |
|---|---|
| (1) Local ordinances | A local ordinance may not prohibit, or have the effect of prohibiting, solar collectors, clotheslines or other renewable energy devices |
| (2) Deed restrictions and HOAs | A deed restriction, covenant, declaration or similar agreement may not prohibit them; an owner may not be denied permission to install solar collectors |
| (2) Location | The HOA or similar entity may decide the specific roof location, within south to 45° east or west of due south, if it does not impair effective operation |
| (3) Legal fees | The prevailing party in litigation under the section gets costs and reasonable attorney's fees |

Part (4) states the purpose, encouraging renewable energy and preventing measures that make owning property more costly, and says the section does not apply to patio railings in condominiums, cooperatives or apartments.

## What can a Florida HOA still control?

- **Roof placement**, within the south to 45° east or west window, as long as the panels still work effectively.
- **The review process.** The law does not say you can skip your HOA's application. Submit plans as your documents require.

The statute does not list specific aesthetic rules, such as panel color or racking style, that an HOA may or may not impose. If your HOA asks for something that would reduce production, point to the "does not impair the effective operation" language and ask for the request in writing.

## How to handle an HOA solar application

1. **Read your HOA documents** for the architectural review process and timelines.
2. **Ask your installer for a layout** that shows panel orientation and expected production.
3. **Submit early** with the layout, equipment spec sheets and a copy of section 163.04.
4. **If the HOA proposes a different location**, ask the installer to estimate production on that roof face. If it falls outside south to 45° east or west, or would clearly hurt production, reply in writing citing the statute.
5. **If it turns into a dispute**, talk to a Florida attorney. The fee-shifting rule means the loser may pay the winner's costs.

## Does an HOA approval change payback?

It can. If the HOA moves panels to a weaker roof face, the system makes less power, and Florida's lower electricity prices leave less room for lost production.
