A standby generator can power more of your home for longer, as long as it has fuel. A home battery is silent and has no exhaust, but holds a fixed amount of energy, roughly 9 hours of average Florida use for a common 13.5 kWh unit, unless solar panels recharge it. For short outages a battery is simpler. For outages lasting days, a generator, or a battery paired with solar, keeps going longer.

## Key numbers

- **20 feet:** the minimum distance Ready.gov says generators and fuel should be kept from windows, doors and attached garages (page updated June 4, 2026).
- **1,321.5 vs 70.5 minutes:** average outage time per Florida customer in 2024 vs 2025, all events included (EIA Table 11.3).
- **13.5 kWh, 5 kW:** the battery in the DOE's 2025 Q1 residential solar-plus-storage benchmark.
- **About 36 kWh a day:** average Florida household use in 2024 (1,104 kWh a month, EIA Table 5A).
- **37%:** share of new U.S. residential solar installs paired with storage in 2025, up from 25% in 2024 (Berkeley Lab).

## Battery vs generator: side-by-side

| | Home battery (with solar) | Standby or portable generator |
|---|---|---|
| Energy source | Stored electricity; solar refills it in daylight | Natural gas, propane, gasoline or diesel |
| How long it runs | Until the battery empties; can restart each sunny day with solar | As long as fuel lasts and can be resupplied |
| Power for big loads (AC, well pump) | Limited by inverter size (5 kW in the DOE benchmark) | Depends on generator size |
| Exhaust and carbon monoxide | None | Yes; must run outdoors (Ready.gov) |
| Noise | Silent | Noticeable |
| Switch-over | Automatic with a backup-capable system | Automatic with standby units; manual with portable units |
| Bill savings | Small in Florida under net metering | None |
| Upkeep | Little routine maintenance | Fuel storage, exercise runs, servicing |

We have not added cost figures for generators because we have not found a primary, dated source. Get written quotes for both options that include installation, permits and any transfer switch.

## How long does each one run during a hurricane outage?

A battery's run time is simple arithmetic. A full 13.5 kWh battery at the 2024 Florida average of about 36 kWh a day lasts about 9 hours on whole-home use, and longer if you run only essentials. See [Florida hurricane battery backup](/guides/florida-hurricane-battery-backup) for a table.

A generator runs as long as it has fuel. After a major storm, roads, gas stations and fuel deliveries can be disrupted, so how much fuel you can store safely matters. A natural gas standby unit avoids refueling if gas service stays on.

How long outages last is the key question. EIA reported that U.S. customers averaged 11 hours without power in 2024, with Hurricanes Beryl, Helene and Milton among the major events that caused 80% of those hours. Milton left 3.4 million Florida customers without power. In 2025, a quieter year, the average Florida customer lost power for 70.5 minutes.

## Generator safety: what Ready.gov says

Generators burn fuel and produce carbon monoxide, which Ready.gov calls a colorless, odorless gas that can kill people and pets. Its guidance:

- Use generators and fuel outdoors only, at least 20 feet from windows, doors and attached garages.
- Install working carbon monoxide detectors on every level of your home.
- Connect appliances with heavy-duty extension cords.

If you want a generator to power home circuits directly, have a licensed electrician install a proper connection. Do not plug a generator into a wall outlet.

## When does a battery make more sense?

- You want backup that starts by itself, with no fuel and no noise.
- You are already adding solar and want it to work when the grid is down.
- You need quiet, exhaust-free power for medical devices. Talk to your medical provider about a plan, as Ready.gov advises.
- Your outages are usually short, in line with Florida's non-storm years.

## When does a generator make more sense?

- You need to run central AC or other large loads for days.
- You have no solar, or a shaded roof, so a battery cannot be recharged.
- You have natural gas service and want long run times without refueling.

Some homes use both: a battery for silent, instant backup and a generator for long outages.

## Cost and the tax credit

There is no federal residential credit to lean on for 2026 installs: the IRS says the Residential Clean Energy Credit (Section 25D) is not available for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. Berkeley Lab found that paired solar-plus-storage was $2.1 per watt more than stand-alone solar at the median in 2025. See [home battery costs and backup](/blog/home-battery-costs-and-backup).
