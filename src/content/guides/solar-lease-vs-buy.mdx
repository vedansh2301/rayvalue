## The short answer

You can buy solar with cash, buy it with a loan, lease it, or sign a power purchase agreement (PPA). With cash or a loan you own the system. With a lease or PPA a company owns it and you pay them. Purchased systems generally cost less over their life, while leases and PPAs usually need little or no money down, according to the Department of Energy.

## Cash

**Pros:** no interest, the lowest total cost, and you own the system and its savings.
**Cons:** the highest upfront cost, and you carry the risk if it underperforms.

## Loan

**Pros:** you own the system and can start with little upfront cost.
**Cons:** interest adds to the total cost, and some loans build a dealer fee into the price. Our calculator estimates buying with cash and does not include loan interest.

## Lease

You pay a fixed monthly amount to a company that owns the system.
**Pros:** little or no money down, and the company often handles maintenance.
**Cons:** you do not own the system or earn its tax benefits, the payment may rise every year, and the contract has to be handled if you sell the home.

## Power purchase agreement (PPA)

You pay a set price per kilowatt-hour the system produces.
**Pros:** little or no money down, and you pay only for what it makes.
**Cons:** the price may rise every year, you do not own the system, and production shortfalls and a home sale need to be understood in the contract.

## Questions to ask before you sign

- What is the monthly payment or price per kilowatt-hour in year one, and how much does it rise each year?
- What is the contract length, and what does it cost to end it early or buy the system out?
- What happens if I sell the home? Can the buyer take over the contract, and is anything recorded against the property?
- Who maintains and insures the system, and who pays if the inverter fails?
- What production does the installer expect each year, and what happens if it falls short?
- What is the total I will have paid at the end of the contract compared with the total cost to buy?
- Is there a fee for paying early or for transferring the agreement?
