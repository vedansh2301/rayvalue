Yes, you can usually add a battery to a California NEM 2 system and keep NEM 2. PG&E's NEM2 tariff says adding energy storage to an existing system does not disqualify you from the rest of your 20-year legacy period. Two things can still cost you NEM 2: adding more than a small amount of extra solar, and taking an SGIP battery incentive in budgets that require moving to the Net Billing Tariff.

## Key numbers

- **20 years:** NEM 2 legacy period, measured from the date PG&E first gave permission to operate (PG&E NEM2 tariff, Special Condition 8).
- **10% or 1 kW:** the most you can increase the system, whichever is greater, and stay on NEM 2 (same).
- **April 15, 2023:** applications on or after this date go on the Net Billing Tariff, not NEM 2 (CPUC).
- **June 4, 2024:** batteries added through SGIP with a reservation accepted on or after this date must move to the Net Billing Tariff, except in listed equity budgets (PG&E NEM2 tariff, citing D.24-03-071).
- **10 kW:** the line between "Small" and "Large" NEM Paired Storage in PG&E's tariff.

## Can you add a battery to NEM 2 without losing it?

PG&E's Electric Schedule NEM2, Special Condition 8, says: "Pursuant to D.22-12-056, the addition of energy storage to the Customer's existing Generating Facility shall not disqualify Customer from the remainder of their 20-year Transition Period on this Schedule."

So a battery on its own is allowed. What matters is what else changes at the same time.

| Change to your NEM 2 system | Effect on NEM 2 (PG&E tariff) |
|---|---|
| Add a battery, no SGIP | Keep NEM 2 for the rest of your 20 years |
| Add a battery with SGIP, reservation accepted on or after June 4, 2024 | Must move to the Net Billing Tariff, unless in an exempt budget |
| Add panels up to the greater of 10% or 1 kW, within annual load | Keep NEM 2 |
| Add more than that | Interconnect the addition separately (e.g. non-export), or move the whole system to the Net Billing Tariff |
| Like-for-like repair or replacement | Keep NEM 2 |

## The 10% or 1 kW rule

The tariff says a modified or repaired system stays eligible as long as the changes do not increase it by more than the greater of 10% of its original nameplate capacity or 1 kW, and do not push it above your annual on-site load.

If you go over, the tariff gives two options. You can interconnect the extra panels separately under another tariff, such as Rule 21 non-export, and your original 20-year period is not changed. Or you can move the whole system to the Net Billing Tariff, where you get a new 9-year legacy period, plus the 9-year export rate lock-in and ACC Plus based on the year the upgraded system gets permission to operate.

A common trap: some battery packages include a few extra panels "to charge the battery." Ask the installer for the before and after system size in writing, so you can check it against the limit.

## The SGIP catch

The Self-Generation Incentive Program (SGIP) can pay toward a battery, but the NEM2 tariff says customers adding storage through SGIP "will be required to transition to the Net Billing Tariff" if they apply and have a reservation accepted on or after June 4, 2024. The tariff lists exempt budgets, including Residential Solar and Storage Equity, low-income-qualified Equity Resiliency residential, and San Joaquin Valley Residential.

Before you accept an SGIP incentive, ask the installer which budget it comes from and whether it requires leaving NEM 2. Losing NEM 2 can cost more than the incentive is worth, because the Net Billing Tariff credits exports at hourly values that, on PG&E's 2026 price sheet, are only a few cents per kWh at most midday hours. Compare your current NEM 2 credits on your bill with those values before deciding. See [California NEM 3.0 export rates](/guides/california-solar-export-rates-by-utility).

## Battery operating rules under NEM 2

PG&E's tariff defines how a paired battery must behave so NEM credits come only from solar:

- **No Grid Charging:** the battery cannot charge from the grid, enforced by certified controls or a non-import relay.
- **No Storage Export:** the battery cannot send power to the grid.
- **PSPS exception:** a qualified No Grid Charging system may charge from the grid starting from PG&E's 48-hour Public Safety Power Shutoff notice, ending 24 hours after power returns, or 72 hours after the notice if power is not shut off.

Storage of 10 kW or less is "Small NEM Paired Storage." Above 10 kW is "Large," with extra metering or control requirements. The battery shares interconnection cost responsibility with the solar system it is paired with.

## Why add a battery to NEM 2 at all?

NEM 2 already gives you its older net metering credits for exports, so a battery's bill savings are typically smaller than under NEM 3, where midday exports earn little. The main reasons are backup during outages, including PSPS shutoffs, and shifting use away from expensive evening hours on a time-of-use rate. Weigh the cost against those benefits. The federal 25D credit no longer applies to batteries installed in 2026.
