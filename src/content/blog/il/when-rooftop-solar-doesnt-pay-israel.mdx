Rooftop solar pays well for many Israeli homeowners, but not for everyone. It tends to fail when you don't control the roof, when the roof is shaded or small, when you connect late, or when the sales pitch assumes more output than the sun delivers. Here are seven situations to check before you sign, with the numbers behind each.

## Key numbers

- **25 years, not linked to inflation**: the standard export tariff for new joiners (mako/Bizportal, 2026).
- **ILS 4,000 to 8,000**: one inverter replacement, expected once, with an inverter life of 10 to 15 years (mako/Bizportal).
- **Up to 15%**: output lost to dirt and dust without cleaning (mako/Bizportal).
- **About 60 steps, 2 to 6 months**: licensing, per Ministry of Energy figures (mako/Bizportal).
- **Over 32,000 kWh a year from 15 kW**: the Ministry guide's claim (Nadlan Center, Jan 2026). PVGIS models **23,700 to 28,350 kWh** for 15 kW across Israeli locations from Tiberias to Mitzpe Ramon.
- **9.09 ag/kWh**: IEC system management charge on self-consumed rooftop power (Globes English, 2026).

## 1. You rent, or the roof isn't yours to use

The export tariff and the bill savings go to whoever owns and connects the system. If you rent, the landlord decides. In an apartment building the roof is usually shared property, and you need an agreement with the other owners before anything goes up. Without one, the "worth it" numbers don't apply to you.

## 2. The roof is shaded, small or badly oriented

mako/Bizportal puts the roof area needed at about 7 to 10 m² per kW. A 10 kW system needs about 70 to 100 m² of clear, unshaded roof. Water heaters, antennas, stairwell structures and neighbouring towers all cut into that. Partial shade or a faulty cell causes local overheating that cuts output (mako/Bizportal). If the installer's layout puts panels in afternoon shade, ask for a shading report.

## 3. The output promise is too high

The Ministry of Energy guide, as reported by Nadlan Center, says a 15 kW system on a house produces over 32,000 kWh a year. That works out to over 2,130 kWh per kW. The EU's PVGIS model, run on 22 November 2026 with standard 14% losses and the best tilt, gives much less:

| Location | PVGIS, kWh per kW per year | 15 kW system, kWh per year | Ministry claim vs PVGIS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tiberias | 1,580 | 23,700 | about 35% higher |
| Tel Aviv | 1,721 | 25,815 | about 24% higher |
| Mitzpe Ramon | 1,890 | 28,350 | about 13% higher |

PVGIS is a model, and very clean panels or a different loss assumption can do better. But if a quote's payback depends on 2,000+ kWh per kW in the centre or north, ask how the figure was calculated. An overstated yield shortens the payback on paper only.

## 4. You connect after 2026

The current tracks are reported to close to new joiners on 31 December 2026. The Electricity Authority's October 2026 draft would start at 45.44 ag in H1 2027 for systems up to 15 kW, fall about 5% a year to 36.77 ag in 2030, and shorten the term from 25 to 20 years (Calcalist; Globes). If licensing takes 2 to 6 months, a contract signed in late November may not make it. Don't pay for "2026 terms" unless the contract names the milestone and what happens if it is missed. See [why connecting before 2027 matters](/il/blog/lock-in-before-2027).

## 5. Inflation eats a fixed tariff

The standard tariff is fixed in shekels for 25 years and not linked to inflation (mako/Bizportal). 48 agorot in 2040 buys less than 48 agorot today. pv magazine reported in March 2025 that the CPI-linked track was proposed precisely because of concerns that high inflation would erode the real value of rooftop solar. It starts lower, at 39 ag, but rises with inflation. If you expect high inflation, the fixed track's real value falls each year.

## 6. Running costs nobody quoted

- **Inverter:** expected to be replaced once at ILS 4,000 to 8,000 (mako/Bizportal).
- **Cleaning:** dirt can cost up to 15% of output, so panels need cleaning several times a year (mako/Bizportal).
- **System management charge:** 9.09 ag per self-consumed kWh. Globes reported that the IEC had not billed it for years and tried to collect two years at once. The Electricity Authority limited that to six months, but owners still faced bills of up to hundreds of shekels.
- **Fees:** registration of about ILS 704 and an aerial roof photo of about ILS 170 (mako/Bizportal).

None of these kills the case on a good roof, but a payback sheet without them is too optimistic.

## 7. You are in a weaker-sun location with a small system

Yield varies by about 20% across Israel: from 1,580 kWh per kW in Tiberias to 1,890 in Mitzpe Ramon (PVGIS). Fixed costs like fees, the aerial photo and licensing weigh more on a small system. A 5 kW roof in a low-yield, shaded spot outside the urban-premium towns is where the margin gets thin.

## So when does it still pay?

If you own the roof, it is mostly unshaded, the quote uses realistic output, and you connect on a known tariff, the arithmetic usually works. See [is rooftop solar worth it in Israel](/il/blog/is-rooftop-solar-worth-it-israel-2026) for the ranges. If two or more of the seven points above apply, get a second quote and ask the installer to put the yield, tariff track and running costs in writing.
