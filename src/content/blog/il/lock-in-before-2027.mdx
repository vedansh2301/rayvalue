If you want a rooftop system in Israel, the current export tariff is better than what is proposed for 2027. Press reports put it at about 48 agorot per kWh for home systems up to 15 kW, fixed for 25 years. The Electricity Authority's October 2026 draft would cut that to 45.44 agorot in the first half of 2027 and shorten the term to 20 years. The current tracks are reported to close to new joiners on 31 December 2026, so the question now is whether your installer can get you in on time.

## Key numbers

- **About 48 ag/kWh** for systems up to 15 kW, fixed for **25 years**, not linked to inflation (Globes English, March 2025; mako/Bizportal, 2026).
- **+6 ag/kWh urban premium** in cities above 50,000 residents, for the first 15 years (mako/Bizportal, 2026).
- **31 December 2026**: the current tariff tracks close to new joiners (Calcalist, 2026; sponsored-style article).
- **45.44 ag/kWh in H1 2027, 36.77 ag/kWh in 2030**: the draft path for systems up to 15 kW (Calcalist, 19 Oct 2026). This is a draft, not a final decision.
- **20 years** instead of 25: the draft term for 2027 systems, with all guarantees ending in 2047 (Globes, Oct 2026).
- **2 to 6 months**: how long licensing currently takes, according to the Ministry of Energy figures reported by mako/Bizportal.

The Electricity Authority PDFs are on gov.il, which blocks automated access, and we found no archived copy. Every tariff above comes from Israeli financial press. Check the numbers against the Authority's decision before you sign.

## What closes at the end of 2026?

According to Calcalist, the following close to new joiners on 31 December 2026:

- the fixed-tariff tiers (about 48 ag up to 15 kW and 41 ag from 15 to 100 kW, per mako/Bizportal);
- the accelerated track. When it was proposed in 2025, it paid 60 ag for the first five years and then 38.07 ag (pv magazine, March 2025);
- the CPI-linked track, which started at 39 ag and rises with inflation (same source);
- the complementary tariff for systems with storage;
- the urban premium.

Net metering is already closed to new entrants. Existing members keep it for 25 years (mako/Bizportal).

## What does the 2027 draft change?

The Electricity Authority published the draft for public comment on 19 October 2026, and comments closed on 10 November (Globes). We found no final decision as of 22 November 2026. As reported, the draft would make these changes:

| Item | Current (until 31.12.2026) | 2027 draft |
|---|---|---|
| Rate, systems up to 15 kW | about 48 ag/kWh | 45.44 ag (H1 2027), falling to 36.77 ag in 2030 |
| Annual cut | none | about 5% a year, 2027 to 2030 |
| Currency link | none | half of the shekel-dollar move against a 3.5 base is added to or offsets the cut |
| Term | 25 years | 20 years, shrinking so all terms end in 2047 |
| Storage incentives | complementary tariff | cancelled |
| Systems above 15 kW | connect normally | connection tender from January 2028 |

Sources: Calcalist (19 Oct 2026) and Globes (Oct 2026), both reporting the draft.

The currency rule matters. Globes worked an example: if the dollar stays around 3.1 shekels, the extra cut is about 6%, so a 2027 roof would get about 43 ag instead of 48. The IEC's reference exchange rate on 1 July 2026 was 2.979 shekels per dollar ([IEC tariff sheet](https://iecmedia.iec.co.il/media/5malqupk/n%D7%AA%D7%A2%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%A4%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%91%D7%90%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%AA-1726-%D7%9E%D7%A2%D7%9E-18.pdf)), which is below 3.1.

Globes also reports that the cuts apply only to new roofs from 2027. Systems already connected keep the price they were promised.

## How much is the difference worth?

Here is a simple illustration for a 10 kW roof in Tel Aviv. PVGIS models 1,721 kWh per year for each kW at the best tilt in Tel Aviv ([PVGIS](https://re.jrc.ec.europa.eu/api/v5_3/PVcalc?lat=32.0853&lon=34.7818&peakpower=1&loss=14&optimalangles=1&outputformat=json)), so 10 kW makes about 17,210 kWh a year. Assume every kWh is paid at the export rate. That is the upper bound, because kWh you use at home are valued differently.

| | Current track | Draft, H1 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Rate | 48 ag | 45.44 ag |
| Yearly value (17,210 kWh) | about ILS 8,260 | about ILS 7,820 |
| Years guaranteed | 25 | 20 |
| Nominal total over the term | about ILS 206,500 | about ILS 156,400 |

The table ignores panel ageing, the urban premium and inflation. The rate gap is only about ILS 440 a year. The real difference is the five lost years at the end of the term, which is why the cut is bigger than it first looks. With the urban premium (54 ag in total), the current track pays about ILS 9,290 a year for the first 15 years.

## Can you still make the deadline?

We can't confirm this for you. According to Ministry of Energy figures reported by mako/Bizportal, licensing involves about 60 bureaucratic steps with three or four regulators and takes 2 to 6 months. With about five weeks left in 2026, ask any installer these questions in writing:

1. **Which milestone secures the current tariff?** Is it a signed contract, an IEC application, connection or the meter? For the urban premium, Kipa (April 2025) reported that it is valid for anyone who receives a positive response from the grid distributor by the end of 2026. None of the sources we opened says clearly which milestone counts for the main tariff tracks.
2. **What happens if you miss it?** Will they cut the price, or let you walk away without penalty, if the system ends up under the 2027 rules?
3. **Will the contract name the tariff track** (fixed tier, accelerated or CPI-linked) and whether the urban premium applies to your town?
4. **Is your roof's grid connection available?** In congested areas the draft expects deeper cuts, and from 2028 systems above 15 kW face tenders (Calcalist).

If the installer can't put the tariff and the deadline in the contract, treat any "lock in now" pitch as marketing.

## Should you rush if you miss it?

No. A roof connected in 2027 on the draft terms can still pay back. The rate is lower and the term shorter, but most homes still save money on every kWh they use themselves instead of buying from the grid. The IEC residential price is 63.52 ag/kWh including VAT from 1 July 2026 ([IEC](https://iecmedia.iec.co.il/media/5malqupk/n%D7%AA%D7%A2%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%A4%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%91%D7%90%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%AA-1726-%D7%9E%D7%A2%D7%9E-18.pdf)). A rushed job on a bad roof costs more than a few agorot of tariff.

## FAQ

**Is the 2027 tariff final?** No. It was a draft for comment, and comments closed on 10 November 2026. We found no final decision as of 22 November 2026.

**Will existing systems lose their tariff?** According to Globes, no. The cuts apply to new roofs from 2027.

**Does the urban premium continue?** For eligible systems it is paid until 2042, but the joining window also closes at the end of 2026, according to Calcalist.
