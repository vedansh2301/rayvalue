For most Israeli homeowners with a sunny, unshaded roof they control, yes. Israel's sun gives 1,580 to 1,890 kWh a year per installed kW. The power you use yourself replaces grid electricity at 63.52 agorot per kWh including VAT. The export tariff is reported at about 48 agorot, fixed for 25 years, for systems connected before the end of 2026. On simple arithmetic, a typical 15 kW home system pays back in roughly 5 to 8 years. The main caveats are renting, shared roofs, shade and connecting after 2026.

## Key numbers

- **63.52 ag/kWh** including 18% VAT: the IEC residential flat rate from 1 July 2026 (53.83 ag before VAT) ([IEC](https://iecmedia.iec.co.il/media/5malqupk/n%D7%AA%D7%A2%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%A4%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%91%D7%90%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%AA-1726-%D7%9E%D7%A2%D7%9E-18.pdf)).
- **1,580 to 1,890 kWh per kW per year**: PVGIS modelled output from Tiberias to Mitzpe Ramon. Tel Aviv is 1,721 (run 22 Nov 2026).
- **About 48 ag/kWh** export for systems up to 15 kW, fixed for 25 years, plus **6 ag** in cities above 50,000 residents (Globes English; press report).
- **ILS 3,500 to 5,500 per kW** before VAT: typical installed cost (mako/Bizportal, 2026).
- **ILS 4,000 to 8,000**: one inverter replacement during the system's life (mako/Bizportal).
- **About 8 years**: the Ministry of Energy's own payback example for 15 kW with 10 kWh of storage costing ILS 90,000 to 100,000 (Nadlan Center, Jan 2026).

## Why does solar pay in Israel?

Three things line up:

1. **Strong sun.** PVGIS models 1,721 kWh per kW in Tel Aviv, 1,725 in Jerusalem, 1,760 in Be'er Sheva and 1,845 in Eilat. Few European markets get close.
2. **A fixed export price.** Under the current rules you are paid a known price per exported kWh for 25 years. Most European countries now pay a market-linked price for exports.
3. **A grid price that rose.** The IEC flat rate is 63.52 ag/kWh including VAT from 1 July 2026. Every kWh you use from your own roof avoids paying that, although Globes reports that the IEC charges a "system management" fee of 9.09 ag on self-consumed kWh.

## What does a typical system cost and earn?

Here is a 15 kW system in Tel Aviv, the top of the home tariff tier. It is an illustration, not a quote.

| Item | Low | High |
|---|---|---|
| Installed cost, ex VAT (15 x 3,500 to 5,500) | ILS 52,500 | ILS 82,500 |
| Installed cost incl. 18% VAT | ILS 61,950 | ILS 97,350 |
| Yearly output (15 x 1,721 kWh) | 25,815 kWh | 25,815 kWh |
| Yearly value if all exported at 48 ag | ILS 12,390 | ILS 12,390 |
| Simple payback | about 5.0 years | about 7.9 years |

With the urban premium (54 ag in total), the yearly value is about ILS 13,940 and the simple payback falls to roughly 4.4 to 7.0 years. If you connect under the 2027 draft rate of 45.44 ag, it is about ILS 11,730 a year and roughly 5.3 to 8.3 years.

This ignores panel ageing, inverter replacement, cleaning, the system management charge and any VAT or tax on export income. Treat it as a range to test quotes against. The Ministry's own example (about 8 years for 15 kW with storage at ILS 90,000 to 100,000) sits at the slow end of this range.

## Who does it pay for best?

- **Homeowners with a private roof** (a detached house or the top floor with roof rights). You decide alone, and a 15 kW system needs about 105 to 150 m² of roof at 7 to 10 m² per kW (mako/Bizportal).
- **Homes in the south and the Negev.** Be'er Sheva's 1,760 kWh per kW beats Haifa's 1,683 by about 5%.
- **Towns with the urban premium.** The extra 6 ag is worth about 12% more than 48 ag alone.
- **Homes that use power by day.** Air conditioning on summer afternoons lines up with solar output.
- **Anyone who can connect before 31 December 2026.** See [why connecting before 2027 matters](/il/blog/lock-in-before-2027).

## Who should think twice?

- **Renters.** The owner decides and collects the tariff.
- **Apartment residents** without an agreement on the shared roof.
- **Shaded roofs** (trees, taller buildings, water heaters, antennas).
- **People who may sell soon** and can't be sure a buyer will pay for the system.

We cover these in detail in [when rooftop solar doesn't pay in Israel](/il/blog/when-rooftop-solar-doesnt-pay-israel).

## Does the 2027 cut make it not worth it?

Not on its own. The draft rate for the first half of 2027 is 45.44 ag, about 5% below 48 (Calcalist, 19 Oct 2026). The bigger loss is the shorter term: 20 years instead of 25. A system connected in 2027 still earns something on every kWh, and self-consumed kWh still avoid the 63.52 ag grid price. Payback stretches by months, not years. Bigger cuts would come from 2028 onward, when the draft reaches 36.77 ag by 2030.

## How to check your own case

1. Find your kWh use on your IEC bill for the last 12 months.
2. Look up your town's yield. Use the PVGIS figure nearest to you, or ask your installer for a PVGIS or similar model, not a round marketing number.
3. Get at least two quotes in ILS per kW before VAT, and compare them against 3,500 to 5,500.
4. Ask in writing which tariff track, rate and term the contract assumes.

For how the money splits between self-use and export, see [how much rooftop solar earns in Israel](/il/guides/how-much-rooftop-solar-earns-israel). For what is on your bill, read [Israel electricity tariffs 2026 explained](/il/guides/israel-electricity-tariffs-2026-explained).
