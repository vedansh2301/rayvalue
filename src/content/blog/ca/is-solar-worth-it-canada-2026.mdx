Yes, for many Canadian homeowners solar is still worth a close look in 2026, even with the federal Greener Homes Grant gone. Ontario, British Columbia and Quebec each now pay their own solar rebate of $1,000 per kW, and Toronto, Montréal and Calgary all model above 1,250 kWh per kW of panels a year. Whether it pays for your home depends on your province's power price and how it treats the power you send to the grid.

## Key numbers

- **Federal Greener Homes Grant: closed.** 31 Dec 2025 was the last day to upload documents (NRCan).
- **Ontario**: $1,000 per kW of solar (max $5,000) plus $300 per kWh of battery (max $5,000), each capped at 50% of costs. No net metering if you take it (Home Renovation Savings, checked 22 Nov 2026).
- **British Columbia**: $1,000 per kW DC of solar, max $5,000; battery $500 per kWh, max $1,500, or $5,000 with Peak Saver (BC Hydro, checked 22 Nov 2026).
- **Quebec**: $1,000 per kW installed, up to 40% of cost (Hydro-Québec LogisVert guide, 31 Mar 2026).
- **Alberta**: no province-wide rebate found, but small micro-generators are credited for exports at their retail rate (Government of Alberta).
- **Modelled output**: Toronto 1,276, Montréal 1,267, Calgary 1,252, Edmonton 1,161, Vancouver 1,019 kWh per kW per year (NREL PVWatts v8, run 22 Nov 2026).

## Are solar panels worth it in Canada now that Greener Homes is gone?

The federal grant is closed. NRCan says applications ran from May 2021 to February 2024, and December 31, 2025 was the final day to upload documents. More than 409,000 households took part, with an average grant of about $4,430.

The provinces have partly filled the gap. Ontario, British Columbia and Quebec now pay a solar rebate of $1,000 per kW. For a 5 kW system that can mean $5,000 back, subject to each program's cost cap. See [Canada Greener Homes is closed: what's left](/ca/guides/canada-greener-homes-closed-whats-left) for the full list.

## How much sun does Canada get for solar?

More than many people assume. Using NREL's PVWatts model with the same settings in each city (1 kW, south-facing, 20 degree tilt, 14% losses):

| City | kWh per kW per year |
|---|---|
| Toronto | 1,276 |
| Montréal | 1,267 |
| Calgary | 1,252 |
| Edmonton | 1,161 |
| Vancouver | 1,019 |

Toronto, Montréal and Calgary are within about 2% of each other. Vancouver's cloudier winters bring it down about 20% from Toronto. Snow reduces winter output across the country, but the long days of May to August do most of the work.

## Which province pays best for solar?

It depends on three things: the rebate, the price you avoid paying, and what you earn for exports.

| Province | Rebate (2026) | Grid price you avoid | Exports |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ontario | $1,000/kW, max $5,000 + battery up to $5,000 | TOU 9.1¢ to 18.9¢/kWh (from 1 Nov 2026) | Net metering, 12-month credits, but not with the rebate |
| British Columbia | $1,000/kW DC, max $5,000 + battery | 11.87¢ Tier 1, 14.08¢ Tier 2 | Fixed 10¢/kWh under RS 2289 |
| Quebec | $1,000/kW, max 40% of cost | 7.065¢ first 40 kWh/day, 11.142¢ after (from 1 Apr 2026) | kWh bank, reset every 24 months at 4.730¢/kWh |
| Alberta | None found province-wide | Set by your retailer | Credited at your retail rate (systems under 150 kW) |

**Ontario** has the most generous combined rebate, up to $10,000 with a battery. Its time-of-use prices also make a battery useful, because on-peak power costs about twice off-peak. The trade-off: taking the rebate rules out net metering. Read [Ontario solar rebate vs net metering](/ca/guides/ontario-solar-rebate-vs-net-metering).

**British Columbia** pays rebates on both solar and batteries, and a battery can qualify on its own if it is enrolled in Peak Saver. New customers get 10¢ per kWh for exports instead of net metering. See [BC Hydro battery rebate and the end of net metering](/ca/guides/bc-hydro-battery-rebate-net-metering-end).

**Quebec** has the lowest electricity prices of the four, which makes the new grant important. At Montréal's modelled output, each kW of panels avoids about $90 to $141 a year of Rate D energy charges (1,267 kWh x 7.065¢ or 11.142¢). See [Subvention solaire Hydro-Québec](/ca/guides/subvention-solaire-hydro-quebec) (in French).

**Alberta** has no provincial rebate we could confirm, but its export rule is unusually fair: small systems are credited at the retail rate. Delivery charges remain on the bill, though. Read [Is solar worth it in Alberta?](/ca/blog/is-solar-worth-it-alberta).

## What is one kW of panels worth per year?

Simple arithmetic, using the modelled output and the prices above, if all the power displaces grid purchases:

- **Toronto**: 1,276 kWh x 9.1¢ to 18.9¢ = about **$116 to $241** a year in energy prices.
- **Vancouver**: 1,019 kWh x 10¢ (export) to 14.08¢ (Tier 2) = about **$102 to $143** a year.
- **Montréal**: 1,267 kWh x 7.065¢ to 11.142¢ = about **$90 to $141** a year.

These exclude any delivery charges a credit may also offset, rate increases, panel ageing and financing. They are a starting point to compare against your quotes, not a payback estimate. Multiply by your system size, then divide your net cost (after the rebate) by the yearly value to get a rough payback range.

## Who is solar most worth it for in 2026?

- **Ontario homeowners adding a battery**, who can claim up to $10,000 and use their own power through the expensive on-peak hours.
- **BC homeowners who will use most of their output** and can add a Peak Saver battery with up to $5,000 back.
- **Quebec homes with high consumption**, such as electric heating, where many kWh are billed at 11.142¢.
- **Southern Alberta homes** with a good roof and a retailer plan that credits exports fairly.
- **Anyone staying put.** Payback takes years, so homeowners planning to stay long enough to recover the cost get the most from it.

## How do I check my own home?

Pull a year of bills, find your energy price, ask for two or three quotes, subtract your provincial rebate, and compare that cost against a low and a high yearly value from the arithmetic above. If the payback range is longer than you expect to stay, read [when solar doesn't pay in Canada](/ca/blog/when-solar-doesnt-pay-canada) before you sign.
