Ofgem will announce the energy price cap for 1 January to 31 March 2027 on 25 November 2026. Until then, the cap is £1,723 a year for a typical dual-fuel household paying by Direct Debit, with electricity at an average of 26.32p per kWh. For solar owners, the number that matters is the electricity unit rate: every penny it moves changes the value of every kWh you use from your own panels by a penny.

*This article was written on 22 November 2026, before the January cap was published. We will update it with Ofgem's figures.*

## Key numbers

- **25 November 2026**: Ofgem announces the cap for 1 Jan–31 Mar 2027. Following dates: 23 Feb 2027 (Apr–Jun) and 26 May 2027 (Jul–Sep) (Ofgem).
- **£1,723 a year**: typical dual-fuel Direct Debit cap, 1 Oct–31 Dec 2026, up 4% on the previous period (Ofgem).
- **26.32p/kWh** and **54.83p/day**: average electricity unit rate and standing charge, same period, Great Britain, no VAT (Ofgem).
- **0% VAT** on domestic electricity in Great Britain from 1 Oct 2026 to 31 Mar 2027; extension to 30 June 2027 announced but not yet legislated (HMRC; OOTLAR).
- **About 4,090 kWh a year**: output of a 4 kWp optimally tilted system in London (PVGIS).

## What is the energy price cap for January 2027?

It has not been published yet. Ofgem's own schedule says the level for 1 January to 31 March 2027 will be announced by 25 November 2026, and that it may publish earlier if needed. Anything you read before that date is a forecast, not the cap.

The current cap, for 1 October to 31 December 2026, is:

| Item (GB average, Direct Debit) | Oct–Dec 2026 | Jan–Mar 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Typical dual-fuel bill | £1,723 a year | Due 25 Nov |
| Electricity unit rate | 26.32p/kWh | Due 25 Nov |
| Electricity standing charge | 54.83p/day | Due 25 Nov |

Regional rates differ. Ofgem publishes a table for each of its 14 regions, with a postcode lookup.

## Why does the unit rate matter for solar?

Solar saves you money mainly by replacing electricity you would otherwise buy. Each kWh you generate and use at home is worth your unit rate. So when the cap unit rate rises, solar is worth more; when it falls, solar is worth less.

The standing charge does not change with solar. You pay it whether you buy one kWh or a thousand, so solar cannot reduce it.

## How much does a 1p change make?

Simple arithmetic: a 4 kWp system in London produces about 4,090 kWh a year according to PVGIS. If you used all of it at home, each 1p change in the unit rate would change its yearly value by about £41. If you used half of it, about £20. Export income does not move with the cap, because export tariffs are set separately by suppliers.

At today's 26.32p, using 1,000 kWh of your own solar saves £263 a year. At 25p it would save £250, and at 28p, £280.

## What about VAT on electricity?

From 1 October 2026 domestic electricity in Great Britain has 0% VAT, under a temporary zero rate that HMRC says runs to 31 March 2027. Northern Ireland stays at the reduced rate of 5%. The Budget 2026 overview of tax legislation says the government will extend the GB zero rate to 30 June 2027 and lay the legislation in early 2027.

The cap figures above already exclude VAT. When the zero rate ends, the price you pay per kWh will rise by the VAT, and so will the value of each kWh of solar you use. Do not count on that in a payback estimate until the dates are law.

## How should I read the 25 November announcement?

When Ofgem publishes, look at these in order:

1. **The electricity unit rate**, not the headline dual-fuel bill. The headline includes gas, which solar does not affect.
2. **Your region's figure.** Ofgem publishes separate unit rates and standing charges for each region, with a postcode lookup. The GB average can differ from your region by a penny or more.
3. **The standing charge.** A rise here raises your bill but does not change what solar saves.
4. **Your payment method.** The figures above are for Direct Debit. Other payment methods have their own capped rates.
5. **The period.** The new cap applies from 1 January to 31 March 2027, the darkest quarter for solar. It tells you about bill levels, not about a full year of generation.

Then rerun any savings estimate with the new unit rate. If the rate moves by a penny, the value of 1,000 kWh of self-used solar moves by £10.

## Should I wait for the new cap before deciding on solar?

There is little reason to. One quarter's cap is a snapshot, and solar panels are expected to run for decades. A 5% move in the unit rate changes the yearly value of your self-used solar by 5%. The things that matter more for most homes are:

- the price you pay for the system ([check it against the official cost data](/uk/guides/are-solar-panels-worth-it-uk)),
- how much you use during the day,
- your export tariff ([SEG rates compared](/uk/guides/best-seg-rates)),
- and finishing before 0% VAT on installation ends on 31 March 2027 ([VAT deadline](/uk/guides/solar-panels-vat-deadline-2027)).

## Is my tariff on the cap at all?

The cap only limits standard variable (default) tariffs. Ofgem says you are not affected by cap changes if you have moved to a fixed tariff. Many solar homes are on time-of-use or export-linked tariffs, which have their own rates. See [Home batteries and time-of-use tariffs](/uk/guides/solar-battery-worth-it-uk) for one example.
