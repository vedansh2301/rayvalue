The Energy Company Obligation (ECO4) closes on 31 December 2026, and its sister scheme, the Great British Insulation Scheme, already ended on 31 March 2026. Help does not stop there. In England, the Warm Homes: Local Grant can pay for solar panels for lower-income households, the government has set aside about £5 billion for low-income schemes to 2030, and the Warm Homes Loan is open to homeowners across the UK.

## Key numbers

- **31 December 2026**: ECO4 ends, after a nine-month extension. It covered measures installed from 1 April 2022 (Ofgem, checked 22 Nov 2026).
- **31 March 2026**: the Great British Insulation Scheme ended (GOV.UK).
- **£5 billion** for low-income schemes to 2030, including **£4.4 billion** in direct capital grants (Warm Homes Plan).
- **£36,000**: usual household income limit for the Warm Homes: Local Grant in England, for homes rated EPC D to G (GOV.UK).
- **£15,000** for solar and **£15,000** for a battery: Warm Homes Loan maximums, UK-wide (DESNZ factsheet, 27 Oct 2026).

## When does ECO4 end?

Ofgem says ECO4 "applies to measures installed from 1 April 2022 and will run until 31 December 2026, following a nine-month extension." GOV.UK's consumer page says the scheme "is closing on 31 December 2026". Because the date applies to installed measures, a new application now leaves very little time. If you have an ECO4 job in progress, contact the energy supplier or installer to confirm it will be finished by the deadline.

## What did ECO4 cover?

ECO4 required larger energy suppliers to fund improvements for lower-income and vulnerable households. GOV.UK lists help with:

- insulation, for example loft or cavity walls;
- replacing or repairing a boiler, or other heating upgrades.

Owner-occupiers on certain benefits could qualify if their home was rated EPC D to G; private tenants needed a rating of E to G and the owner's permission. Qualifying benefits included Universal Credit, Pension Credit, income-based Jobseeker's Allowance, income-related Employment and Support Allowance, Income Support, Child Benefit and Housing Benefit.

GOV.UK also warns that "installers or agents might contact you directly", and advises checking with your council or the energy supplier if you are not sure an offer is genuine. With the scheme ending, expect some "last chance for free solar" approaches. Our [UK solar quote red flags](/uk/blog/solar-panel-scams-uk) explain what to look for.

## What replaces ECO4?

The government's Warm Homes Plan moves support from supplier obligations to public funding. It commits £15 billion of public investment to 2030, including:

| Part of the Warm Homes Plan | Funding to 2030 |
|---|---|
| Low-income schemes | £5 billion |
| Consumer loans | £2 billion |
| Boiler Upgrade Scheme | £2.7 billion |
| Warm Homes Fund (innovative finance) | £2.7 billion |
| Heat networks | £1.1 billion |
| Other programmes and devolved administrations | £1.5 billion |

The plan says low-income and fuel-poor households will benefit from around £5 billion, "with £4.4 billion in direct capital grants". It will deliver this "initially through the existing Warm Homes Social Housing Fund (WH:SHF) and Local Grant (WH:LG)". It also says part of its budget supports "an orderly closure of ECO".

## Can I still get free solar panels?

Possibly, if you are in England and on a lower income. The **Warm Homes: Local Grant** is run by local councils. GOV.UK says improvements "might" include solar panels, along with insulation, air source heat pumps and smart controls, and that the council "will then organise and pay for any improvement work". To qualify, your home must:

- be in England;
- be privately owned (by you or your landlord);
- have an EPC rating of D, E, F or G.

Household income must usually be £36,000 a year or less, though you may still qualify above that if you live in certain postcode areas or someone in the household receives certain benefits. Councils need funding available, and they usually contact applicants within 10 working days to arrange a survey.

In Scotland, see [what Home Energy Scotland funds now](/uk/guides/home-energy-scotland-solar), which no longer lists standard solar panels.

## What if I do not qualify for a grant?

The **Warm Homes Loan** launched on 27 October 2026 for households anywhere in the UK. It offers up to £15,000 for solar panels and up to £15,000 for a battery through ten lenders, some interest-free, with MCS-certified installers. See our [Warm Homes Loan guide](/uk/guides/warm-homes-loan-solar-battery).

Paying yourself before 31 March 2027 also keeps the 0% VAT rate on installed solar and batteries; it rises to 5% from 1 April 2027. See [Solar panels VAT: 0% until 31 March 2027](/uk/guides/solar-panels-vat-deadline-2027).

## What should I do now?

1. **ECO4 job under way?** Confirm with the supplier or installer that it will be installed by 31 December 2026.
2. **Lower income in England?** Apply for the Warm Homes: Local Grant through GOV.UK.
3. **Not eligible for grants?** Compare the [cost of solar](/uk/guides/how-much-do-solar-panels-cost-uk) with what a Warm Homes Loan would cost you each month.
4. **Cold-called?** Check with your council or the named supplier before agreeing to anything.

Dates and scheme details were checked against Ofgem and GOV.UK on 22 November 2026.
