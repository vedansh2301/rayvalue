Irish suppliers paid between 17.16c and 19.5c per kWh for exported solar power in November 2026. Electric Ireland and SSE Airtricity were highest at 19.5c. Bord Gáis Energy, Energia and Flogas paid 18.5c, and Yuno 17.16c before VAT. The gap between best and worst is small, so the import price you pay usually matters more than the export rate.

## Key numbers

- **19.5c/kWh**: Electric Ireland and SSE Airtricity (supplier pages, 22 Nov 2026).
- **18.5c/kWh**: Bord Gáis Energy, Energia (archived Aug 2026) and Flogas (€0.185, effective 24 June 2025).
- **17.16c/kWh ex VAT**: Yuno Energy.
- **0c**: the CRU floor; suppliers set their own rate above zero (CRU/21/131).
- **€400 a year**: export income exempt from income tax per person named on the bill, 2024–2028 (Revenue).
- **38.71c/kWh**: average household price, 2026 first half, all taxes, 2,500–5,000 kWh band (Eurostat).

All of these are variable rates. Most suppliers do not say whether the rate includes VAT; Yuno states its rate excludes VAT.

## Who sets the Clean Export Guarantee rate?

The Commission for Regulation of Utilities (CRU) decided in 2021 that each supplier sets its own competitive Clean Export Guarantee (CEG) tariff, with a floor of zero cent per kWh. There is no regulated minimum above zero, so rates can fall as well as rise. You are paid by your electricity supplier for the power you export.

## How do I start getting paid?

1. Your installer submits an **NC6 form** to ESB Networks. Electric Ireland says it learns you have microgeneration once the NC6 or NC5 form is processed.
2. ESB Networks aims to install a **smart meter** within four months of processing a valid NC6.
3. Until export can be metered, some suppliers pay on **deemed export**. Flogas, for example, assumes 357 kWh a year of export for every 1.2 kW of panels, so 1,428 kWh for a 4.8 kW system. Bord Gáis Energy says ESB Networks sends a deemed export quantity where metered export data is not available.

## How much will I earn from export?

Electric Ireland estimates customer export payments of €50 to €300 a year depending on system size and type, and about €150 a year for a typical 10-panel installation.

Simple arithmetic: 1,000 kWh exported earns €195 at 19.5c and €185 at 18.5c. The difference between the top two rates is €10 per 1,000 kWh. That is why switching supplier for export alone rarely makes a big difference.

## Does the export rate or the import price matter more?

Usually the import price. Each kWh you use from your panels saves you your full unit rate. Eurostat's average Irish household price for the first half of 2026 was 38.71c per kWh including taxes, about twice the best export rate. Yuno's published 24-hour unit rate, for example, is 39.10c including VAT.

So the questions to ask when choosing a supplier are:

- What is the unit rate and standing charge I will pay?
- Is there a day/night or smart tariff that suits a battery?
- Then: what is the export rate?

## Should I switch supplier for a better export rate?

Work out what the switch is worth before you move. Simple arithmetic: a home exporting 2,000 kWh a year earns €390 at 19.5c and €343 at 17.16c, a gap of about €47 a year (and Yuno's rate is quoted before VAT, so the real gap may be smaller).

Now compare the import side. Yuno, for example, publishes a 24-hour unit rate of 39.10c including VAT, with a standing charge of €264.73 a year for urban homes and €326.24 a year for rural homes. A difference of 1c per kWh on 4,000 kWh of imports is €40 a year, about the same as the export gap above. The standing charge depends on whether your connection is urban or rural, so check the right line on each supplier's price list.

A good order of checks:

1. Find your yearly import and export in kWh on your bill or app.
2. Price both at each supplier, including the standing charge.
3. Check for exit fees and how often export is paid. SSE Airtricity, for example, pays four times a year.

## Is export income taxed?

Revenue's guidance on section 216D says profits of up to €400 a year from microgeneration are exempt from income tax for each individual named on the electricity bill, for the years 2024 to 2028. The individual must use the premises as their main residence.

## FAQ

**Can rates go down?** Yes. All the rates above are variable, and the CRU floor is zero.

**Do I need a smart meter?** Metered payments need one. ESB Networks aims to fit it within four months of a valid NC6. Until then, deemed export may apply.

**Does a battery change this?** A battery lets you use more of your own power instead of exporting it. See [the €600 battery grant](/ie/guides/seai-battery-grant-how-to-claim).
