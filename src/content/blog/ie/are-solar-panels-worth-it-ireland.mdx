For many Irish homes, yes. Irish household electricity averaged 38.71c per kWh in the first half of 2026 on Eurostat's figures, so every kWh you use from your own roof is valuable. SEAI pays up to €1,800 towards panels and €600 more for a battery, and suppliers pay 17c to 19.5c for what you export. Solar pays best for owner-occupied homes with a decent roof and some daytime or evening use you can move.

## Key numbers

- **38.71c/kWh**: average Irish household electricity price, first half of 2026, all taxes, 2,500–5,000 kWh band (Eurostat; 40.42c in the second half of 2025).
- **929 to 996 kWh per kWp a year**: PVGIS yield for Galway and Cork; Dublin 988 (EU JRC PVGIS 5.3).
- **€1,800** solar grant, **€2,400** with a battery (SEAI, archived 27 Oct 2026).
- **17.16c to 19.5c/kWh**: supplier export rates (supplier pages, 22 Nov 2026).
- **€400 a year** of export income tax-free per person named on the bill, 2024–2028 (Revenue).

## Are solar panels worth it in Ireland?

Solar earns you money in two ways. Electricity you use at home replaces power you would buy at your unit rate. Electricity you export earns your supplier's Clean Export Guarantee rate. SEAI's own FAQ sums up the gap: a kWh used from your panels saves about €0.35, while an exported kWh earns about €0.18 to €0.20. Using more of your own power is the key to a good result.

## How much will panels produce in Ireland?

Ireland's weather is less of a problem than people think. PVGIS, the European Commission's solar model, gives these yields for a 1 kWp system at the optimal angle:

| Place | kWh per kWp per year | 4 kWp system, kWh per year |
|---|---|---|
| Cork | 996 | about 3,990 |
| Dublin | 988 | about 3,950 |
| Galway | 929 | about 3,710 |

Your roof direction and any shading will change this. These are yearly totals, with much more in summer than in winter.

## What is a 4 kWp system's output worth?

Simple arithmetic at today's prices, showing the two extremes. Real homes fall in between, depending on how much of the power is used at home.

| 4 kWp in Dublin (3,950 kWh) | Value per year |
|---|---|
| All exported at 18.5c (Bord Gáis Energy) | about €731 |
| All exported at 19.5c (Electric Ireland) | about €770 |
| All used at home at 38.71c (Eurostat average) | about €1,529 |

For comparison, Electric Ireland estimates customer export payments of €50 to €300 a year, which reflects that most homes use a good share of their solar themselves.

Because the export rate in Ireland is about half the average retail price, exported power still earns a useful amount. But each kWh you use at home is worth about twice as much.

## What does the grant cover?

| System | SEAI solar grant | With a 5 kWh+ battery |
|---|---|---|
| 2 kWp | €1,400 | €2,000 |
| 3 kWp | €1,600 | €2,200 |
| 4 kWp | €1,800 | €2,400 |

Homes built and occupied before 2025 qualify, and approval must be in place before work starts. See [the SEAI solar grant guide](/ie/guides/seai-solar-grant-2026). Homeowners on qualifying social welfare payments may get the enhanced grant of up to €8,400, or €9,000 with a battery.

We have not found an official source for typical Irish installed prices per kWp, so we do not give a payback figure here. Get at least three quotes from SEAI registered companies, and subtract the grant.

## Who does solar pay best for?

- **Owner-occupiers who plan to stay.** The grant is for homeowners, including landlords, and the savings build over years.
- **Homes with daytime use.** Working from home, children at home, heat pumps, or an EV charged during the day.
- **People who add a battery with the panels.** SEAI says a battery fitted with panels carries 0% VAT, against 23% if fitted alone, and the €600 grant applies. See [the battery grant guide](/ie/guides/seai-battery-grant-how-to-claim).
- **South, east or west-facing roofs without heavy shade**, especially in the south and east of the country.

## What about the export rate?

The best rates are close together: 19.5c at Electric Ireland and SSE Airtricity, 18.5c at Bord Gáis Energy, Energia and Flogas, and 17.16c ex VAT at Yuno. All are variable. The import unit rate and standing charge you pay usually matter more. See [export rates by supplier](/ie/blog/best-export-rate-ireland).

## Is the income taxed?

Revenue's guidance says up to €400 a year of export profit is exempt from income tax for each person named on the bill, for 2024 to 2028.

## FAQ

**Does solar work in Ireland's weather?** Yes. PVGIS gives roughly 930 to 1,000 kWh per kWp a year in Galway, Dublin and Cork.

**Can I install first and claim the grant later?** No. SEAI requires grant approval before work starts.

**Is a bigger system better?** The grant stops at 4 kWp. A bigger system can still make sense if you use the power, but the extra kWp do not get grant support.
