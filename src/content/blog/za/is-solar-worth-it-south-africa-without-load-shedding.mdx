Yes, for many homeowners, although the reason has changed. South Africa has had no load-shedding since 16 May 2025, so solar is no longer mainly a backup purchase. It has to pay through lower bills. With grid power at 322 to 469 c/kWh in 2026/27 and each kW of panels producing about 1,480 to 1,740 kWh a year, the output of each kW of panels, if you use it all at home, is worth roughly R5,500 to R6,200 a year. Whether that pays back depends on the price you pay, which we can't yet source independently.

## Key numbers

- **504 days** without load-shedding since **16 May 2025**, as reported in early October 2026 ([SAnews](https://www.sanews.gov.za/node/84735)).
- **322.06 c/kWh** including VAT: Eskom Homepower energy rate from 1 April 2026 ([Eskom booklet](https://www.eskom.co.za/distribution/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Eskom-Tariff-booklet_Final.pdf)).
- **355.95 / 469.06 c/kWh** including VAT: Cape Town Home User rate up to / above 600 kWh a month, 2026/27 (City of Cape Town).
- **411.09 c/kWh** including VAT: eThekwini residential energy charge, 2026/27 (eThekwini).
- **8.83%**: approved Eskom increase for 2027/28 (SAnews, Feb 2026).
- **1,481 to 1,739 kWh per kW per year**: PVGIS output for panels facing north at 30 degrees, Durban to Pretoria (run 22 Nov 2026).

## Why does solar still pay without load-shedding?

Because electricity is expensive, and getting more so. Eskom's direct tariffs rose 8.76% in April 2026, and NERSA approved another 8.83% for April 2027 (SAnews). Municipal prices follow. Each kWh your panels supply is one you don't buy at those rates, and the value rises with every increase.

South Africa also has strong sun. PVGIS, the EU's solar model, gives these yearly outputs for 1 kW of panels facing north at 30 degrees with 14% system losses:

| City | kWh per kW per year | Grid price you avoid (incl. VAT) | Value per kW if all used at home |
|---|---|---|---|
| Johannesburg (Eskom Homepower) | 1,720 | 322.06 c | about R5,539 |
| Pretoria (Eskom Homepower) | 1,739 | 322.06 c | about R5,600 |
| Cape Town (Home User, up to 600 kWh) | 1,728 | 355.95 c | about R6,151 |
| Durban (eThekwini residential) | 1,481 | 411.09 c | about R6,089 |

The Johannesburg and Pretoria rows use Eskom's Homepower rate as an example. Joburg and Tshwane municipal customers pay their city's rates. The last column is an upper bound: it assumes you use every kWh at home. Exported kWh are worth much less (see below).

## What changed now that backup matters less?

During load-shedding, many systems were sized around a battery and inverter to keep the lights on. Now:

- **A battery has to earn its keep through tariffs**, not just backup. On Eskom's Homeflex tariff that can work, because winter peak is 847.10 c/kWh against 190.83 c off-peak. On a flat municipal rate, a battery saves little beyond backup. Read [battery backup vs generator](/za/guides/battery-backup-vs-generator-south-africa).
- **Panels do most of the saving.** For the same budget, more panels and a smaller battery usually saves more on a flat tariff.
- **Exports pay less than imports.** Cape Town credits 107.98 c plus a 25 c incentive before VAT, and eThekwini 160.52 c, against 309.52 c and 357.47 c before VAT for grid power. Size for self-use. See [feed-in tariffs by metro](/za/guides/solar-feed-in-tariffs-cape-town-johannesburg-durban).

## Who does solar pay for best?

- **Homeowners with high daytime use:** pool pumps, home offices, electric geysers on timers.
- **Higher-use homes in Cape Town:** above 600 kWh a month, the Home User rate jumps to 469.06 c/kWh including VAT. Every kWh solar saves in that block is worth more.
- **Eskom direct customers who can shift use** away from the winter peak on Homeflex.
- **People who will stay long enough** to recover the cost.

## Who should think twice?

- **Low users with high fixed charges.** Cape Town Home User customers pay a Services and Wires Charge of R424.30 a month including VAT, and Eskom's daily charges are fixed. Solar doesn't reduce those.
- **Renters**, and anyone who may sell soon.
- **Anyone relying on the solar tax rebate.** SARS's Section 6C rebate was a one-year incentive for panels between 1 March 2023 and 29 February 2024. It is no longer available.

For a fuller list, see [solar installer red flags and COC rules](/za/blog/solar-installer-red-flags-coc-rules).

## How to check your own payback

1. Take 12 months of bills. Note your energy rate in c/kWh and your fixed charges separately.
2. Estimate how many kWh you can use in daylight, which is the part solar replaces at full value.
3. Multiply by your city's kWh per kW from the table above and your energy rate.
4. Get two or three quotes. Divide the price by the yearly saving to get a simple payback, and remember that tariffs are going up by about 9% a year for now.

We don't publish a payback in years for South Africa yet, because we haven't found an independent source for installed prices. Any installer's payback should show its own assumptions for price, self-use and tariff growth. For the tariff outlook, read [Eskom tariff increases 2026/27 and 2027/28](/za/guides/eskom-tariff-increase-2026-2027).
