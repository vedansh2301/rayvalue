Solar does not pay for every Florida home. Electricity here is cheaper than the U.S. average, the federal tax credit is gone for 2026 installs, and FPL keeps a $30 minimum bill even for net metering customers. If your bill is small, your roof is shaded, you plan to move soon, or the quote is high, the system may not earn back its cost in a time frame that makes sense.

## Key numbers

- **14.99 cents per kWh:** Florida's average residential price in August 2026, vs 18.16 cents for the U.S. (EIA, preliminary).
- **$156.09 a month:** Florida's average residential bill in 2024, at 1,104 kWh and 14.14 cents per kWh (EIA Table 5A).
- **$30:** FPL's minimum base bill, which net metering customers still pay (FPL net metering FAQ).
- **$0:** the federal 25D credit for property placed in service after December 31, 2025 (IRS). It was 30% for 2022 through 2025.
- **$2.4 to $6.3 per watt:** range of median prices across the top-100 U.S. residential installers in 2025 (Berkeley Lab).

## Why solar is harder to pay off in Florida than people expect

**Cheap power means smaller savings per kWh.** Solar saves you the price of each kWh it replaces. At 14.99 cents in Florida (August 2026), each kWh saves about half what it does in California, where the August figure was 29.75 cents. The system costs about the same to install, so it takes longer to pay off.

**The minimum bill stays.** FPL's FAQ says its "$30 minimum base bill" makes sure all customers, including net metering customers, contribute to fixed system costs. A system sized to wipe out your bill cannot take you below that.

**Extra kWh pay out at a low rate.** FPL rolls unused kWh forward month to month, then credits any surplus left at year-end on the December bill "based on our average annual cost of electricity generation," not the retail price. Oversizing a system to bank extra kWh earns little.

**No federal credit.** The IRS says the Residential Clean Energy Credit "is not available for any property placed in service after December 31, 2025." Before that it covered 30% of the cost, so a 2026 buyer pays the full price.

## Signs solar probably won't pay for your home

| Warning sign | Why it hurts payback |
|---|---|
| Your bill is near FPL's $30 minimum most months | Little left for solar to save |
| Heavy shade from trees or neighbors | Less production for the same cost |
| Roof needs replacing within a few years | Panels must come off and go back on |
| You plan to move soon | You may not stay long enough to earn back the cost |
| Quote well above the typical price range | Berkeley Lab found about 10% of top-100 installers had medians above $5 per watt in 2025 |
| Quote counts the federal tax credit | It does not exist for 2026 installs |
| Savings assume steep yearly rate increases | Rates also fall: Florida's August average dropped from 15.33 to 14.99 cents year over year (EIA) |
| Loan with a high dealer fee | Berkeley Lab found loan-financed systems were priced considerably higher than cash purchases |

## When a battery won't pay in Florida

A battery rarely pays for itself through bill savings in Florida, because net metering already credits exports as kWh. Its value is backup power during outages. If you rarely lose power, or a generator covers you, the battery's extra cost mostly buys peace of mind. See [Florida hurricane battery backup](/guides/florida-hurricane-battery-backup) and [battery vs generator in Florida](/guides/generator-vs-battery-florida).

## When solar does still make sense in Florida

To be fair to the other side: Florida's high summer use and FPL's month-to-month kWh banking under net metering still work in solar's favor for many homes. For the state tax treatment we have verified, see [Florida solar tax benefits](/us/solar-tax-benefits/florida) and [is solar worth it in Florida without the federal tax credit](/guides/florida-solar-without-federal-tax-credit).

## How to check your own numbers

1. Pull 12 months of bills and note your lowest and highest months.
2. Run the [Florida calculator](/us/solar-payback/florida). It shows low, likely and high payback, with no federal credit.
3. See your utility's rules: [FPL](/us/utility/florida/fpl), [Duke Energy Florida](/us/utility/florida/duke-energy-florida), [TECO](/us/utility/florida/teco).
4. Read [red flags in a solar quote](/blog/solar-quote-red-flags-2026) before signing.
