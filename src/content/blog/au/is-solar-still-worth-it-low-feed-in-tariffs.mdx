Yes, for most owner-occupied houses with a decent roof, as long as you use a good share of the power yourself. Exported solar now earns about 2 to 10 c/kWh in most of Australia. Every kWh you use at home instead of buying it saves the full usage rate, which the regulator caps at about 28 to 42 c/kWh in 2026-27. Solar now earns its money from self-use, not from export income.

## Key numbers

- **Export value is low:** NSW's all-day benchmark is **3.4 to 6.5 c/kWh** for 2026-27 (IPART). Regional Queensland's regulated rate is **6.006 c/kWh**, down 31% (QCA). Victoria has had **no minimum** since 1 July 2025 (ESC).
- **Self-use value is high:** 2026-27 flat-rate usage caps run from **27.97 c/kWh** (Energex) to **41.91 c/kWh** (SA Power Networks) (AER DMO). WA's regulated A1 rate is **33.2621 c/kWh** from 1 July 2026.
- **Solar homes paid less:** the ACCC found median annual bills in 2023-24 of **$1,279** with rooftop solar against **$1,565** for grid-only households, about 18% less.
- **Upfront STC discount shrinks yearly:** solar PV installed in 2026 gets a **5-year** deeming period, 2027 **4 years**, down to **1 year** in 2030 (CER).
- **Victoria:** solar panel rebate of up to **$1,400** is open until the program moves into VEU on **1 July 2027**.

## Why have feed-in tariffs fallen so far?

Regulators set or benchmark feed-in tariffs from the wholesale value of the power. Midday power is now cheap because so many rooftops export at once. IPART says its benchmark is based on "our estimate of the wholesale price of electricity" at the times solar exports. Its 2026-27 range of [3.4 to 6.5 c/kWh](https://www.ipart.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/cm9_documents/Fact-Sheet-All-day-solar-feed-in-tariff-benchmark-tariff-2026-27.PDF) is below the 4.8 to 7.3 c/kWh it set the year before. In regional Queensland, the [QCA](https://www.qca.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/solar-fit-fact-sheet-june-2026.pdf) cut the regulated rate from 8.660 to 6.006 c/kWh. In Victoria, the [ESC](https://www.esc.vic.gov.au/electricity-and-gas/electricity-and-gas-tariffs-and-benchmarks/minimum-feed-tariff) removed the minimum altogether.

For a full table, see [solar feed-in tariffs by state](/au/guides/feed-in-tariff-comparison-by-state).

## Is solar still worth it?

What matters is the gap between what you pay for grid power and what you get for exports:

| Where | Usage rate cap 2026-27 (flat) | Export value 2026-27 | Value of using 1 kWh yourself vs exporting it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ausgrid (Sydney) | 33.14 c | 3.4 to 6.5 c (IPART all-day) | about 27 to 30 c more |
| Energex (SE Qld) | 27.97 c | Retailer rate (not verified) | n/a |
| SA Power Networks | 41.91 c | Retailer rate (not verified) | n/a |
| WA (Synergy A1) | 33.26 c | 2 c (9 pm to 3 pm, DEBS as of 1 Jul 2024) | about 31 c more |

Usage caps are from the AER's [2026-27 DMO determination](https://www.aer.gov.au/system/files/2026-05/AER%20-%20Final%20determination%20-%20Default%20market%20offer%202026%E2%80%9327.pdf) and WA's [household pricing page](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/energy-policy-wa/household-electricity-pricing). Market offers can be cheaper than the cap, which narrows the gap.

Each kWh you use yourself is worth at least five times a kWh you export. A system that covers your daytime use still saves real money. A system sized only to export earns very little.

## How to get more of your solar into your own home

- **Move loads to the middle of the day.** Run the dishwasher, washing machine, pool pump and hot water on a timer between about 10 am and 3 pm.
- **Look at a Solar Sharer or time-of-use plan.** In NSW, SE Queensland and SA, the AER's opt-in Solar Sharer standing offer has applied since 1 July 2026. It has free usage from 11 am to 2 pm (12 pm to 3 pm in SA), with a 24 kWh daily cap. Rates outside that window are 1 to 4 c/kWh higher than the time-of-use default. It helps most if you can shift a lot of use into the free window.
- **Consider a battery.** A battery stores midday surplus for the evening. See [is a battery worth it](/au/guides/is-a-solar-battery-worth-it-australia).
- **Don't oversize purely for export.** With exports worth a few cents, extra panels mostly pay off if you can use the output, now or later (an EV, a heat pump).

## The bill evidence

The ACCC's [2025 electricity inquiry report](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/electricity-industry-on-notice-as-more-households-invest-in-subsidised-batteries-and-solar) compared actual bills across the National Electricity Market in 2023-24. The median household with rooftop solar paid $1,279 a year, against $1,565 for households drawing only from the grid. Homes with solar and a battery paid $936. These are different households, so the gap is not a guaranteed saving, but it is a large one.

## Deadlines that make waiting cost more

- **Solar STCs fall every January.** The [CER](https://cer.gov.au/schemes/renewable-energy-target/small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/small-scale-technology-certificates/calculate-small-scale-technology-certificate-entitlements) deeming period is 5 years for a 2026 install, 4 for 2027, and 1 for 2030. Fewer deemed years means fewer certificates and a smaller upfront discount on the same system.
- **Victoria's $1,400 rebate** moves into the Victorian Energy Upgrades program from 1 July 2027 ([Solar Victoria](https://www.solar.vic.gov.au/solar-panel-rebate)). It is for owner-occupiers with combined taxable income under $150,000 and a property under $3 million.

## When solar is a weaker buy

Low feed-in tariffs hurt most if you are out all day and can't shift use, or if your roof is shaded or faces south. They also hurt if you are likely to sell within a few years. Very small households on cheap market offers have the least to save. Run the numbers against your own bill before you sign.
